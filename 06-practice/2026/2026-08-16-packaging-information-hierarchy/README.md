## Learning object

Le Labo official personalization and fragrance product pages were used only to study the relationship between product identity, operational information, personalization and label/box consistency. This practice does **not** copy Le Labo branding, trade dress, typography or decorative styling.

Primary sources:
- https://www.lelabofragrances.com/personalization.html
- https://www.lelabofragrances.com/santal-33-1212.html

## Visible fact → inference → transfer

- Visible fact: official Le Labo pages expose personalization at both bottle-label and box-label level; fragrance name, size/product type and personalization belong to the same operating label system.
- Design inference: truthful operational information can contribute to identity when hierarchy is deliberate rather than buried as compliance residue.
- Transfer rule: `PRODUCT IDENTITY FIRST → GENUINE OPERATIONAL FIELDS SECOND → LEGAL / TRACE EVIDENCE REMAINS LEGIBLE`.

### Actual failure caught by visual readback

The first After version produced a text collision between the two lower columns. It was rejected as `POST-REVIEW FAIL` despite stronger first-read hierarchy. v2 wraps the notes inside their columns and was re-rendered/read back.

## Candidate skill

**Operational information can become brand identity only when it is real, useful and hierarchy-controlled.** Never invent batch, handmade, provenance, laboratory or formula language merely to imitate a premium visual code.

### Counterexample

A packaging layout may look premium at thumbnail scale yet still fail when actual instructions, quantity, trace or safety information is unreadable. `FIRST-READ PASS + NEAR-READ FAIL = REVISE`.
