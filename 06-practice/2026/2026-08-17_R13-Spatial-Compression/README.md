## Learning object
Jewish Museum Berlin, Daniel Libeskind, “Between the Lines”. Primary sources:

## Visible Fact
The official museum describes a zigzag building with underground axes, angled walls and bare concrete voids; the Holocaust Tower receives daylight only through a narrow slit. Studio Libeskind describes three underground axial routes and an inaccessible void that visitors repeatedly cross.

## Design Inference
Spatial compression reads first from boundary convergence, occlusion and controlled release. Darkness, fog, contrast or a narrow light slit can reinforce that relation but cannot substitute for absent geometry.

## Transfer Rule
Before accepting a compressed-space image, section or plan, verify a readable sequence of **convergence → occlusion → release** at first read. Only after geometry reads should material, tone, fog and lighting be judged.

## Counterexample
A narrow-looking image created only by vignette, dark walls, fog or a cropped camera can look atmospheric but must remain **REVISE** if plan/section boundaries stay parallel and the exit remains fully exposed.
