## A0｜Existing interpretation diagnosis

### Observed in project-source assets

1. The guide-map asset organizes the village as a large illustrated map plus a numbered list of 34 destinations. The first-reading unit is therefore **destination number + place name**.
2. The Zhenxiao/archway context photo shows more than one interpretation/signage element around the heritage object, confirming that the site was not an interpretation blank when the source photo was made.
3. Navigation, heritage-object explanation and contemporary-question framing are not visibly integrated into one reading sequence in the source assets.

### Diagnosis

- **Strength:** broad orientation coverage; many destinations can be located in one frame.
- **Weakness:** high information density; all destinations receive near-equal list treatment, so hierarchy and route purpose are weak at first glance.
- **Weakness:** object-level signage appears additive: a visitor can read a sign, but the visible system does not itself require evidence discrimination, comparison or a question that continues to another node.
- **Risk:** a new OLEANDER layer that merely redesigns the map/signs would duplicate an existing navigation layer instead of creating a new relationship mechanism.

### Design consequence

OLEANDER should not replace A0 with a prettier map. It should **sit on top of A0** and add a second layer:

`where am I? → what exactly am I looking at? → what is fact/source/inference/unknown? → what question remains? → where/who can answer next?`

A0 is retained as baseline/control infrastructure.

## C1｜Zhenxiao Archway deep reading

### Object lock

`贞孝牌坊 / Zhenxiao Archway`

Project-source data claims: Qing Daoguang 6 (1826), approx. 7.8 m high and 3.26 m wide, two-column / three-tier stone construction; the project-source caption calls out an Eight Immortals relief. These remain **project-source claims pending primary-record reconciliation**.

### Five evidence layers visible in the real photo

1. **Geometry:** strong axial symmetry, two principal stone piers, stacked three-tier roof/beam hierarchy.
2. **Inscription layer:** central plaque/inscription zone and vertical column inscriptions are physically distinct from the structural frame.
3. **Relief layer:** carved narrative/ornamental bands sit above the principal opening rather than functioning as structure.
4. **Material/time layer:** stone color variation, weathering and surface erosion make age legible without adding “historic” graphics.
5. **Context layer:** current/modern buildings and village infrastructure sit directly behind and beside the archway. The object is not an isolated museum specimen.

### C1 reading sequence

`whole form → one physical trace → source statement → conflict/unknown → one question → next verifier`

Mandatory questions:

- What can be seen directly without reading a label?
- Which historical statement comes from a source rather than from the stone itself?
- What cannot be concluded from the image?
- Who is being commemorated, and who is permitted to explain the meaning today?

### C1 decision

`KEEP`.

The object can support T1→T2 translation without historicist decoration because the real material, inscription and context already provide the reading structure.

## C2｜Three-archway real-evidence comparison

| Object | Project-source claim | Available visual evidence | Spatial relation visible | Evidence gap | Design question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 贞孝牌坊 | 1826; ~7.8×3.26 m; stone; project caption mentions Eight Immortals relief | full elevation + contextual project-source image | modern/village buildings directly adjacent | exact current condition, primary record, rights | What changes when a moral monument remains inside daily contemporary space? |
| 节孝牌坊 / 八股厅前 | 1802; project caption: He-shi, “守节68年”; ~7.9×3.6 m in project data | full village-context image | courtyard/lane and historic fabric around the object | primary record, contemporary local interpretation | How does location in front of the education-related node change the reading? |
| 卖菜牌坊 / 大爿地 | 1830; project caption: Liu-shi, widowed at 28, sold vegetables and raised children | **detail image only** in the currently mapped source set | not closed | full elevation/context, primary record, exact object-image mapping beyond caption | Does a biography of labor/care produce a different reading from a generic “chastity” category? |

### C2 key finding

The three objects must **not** be normalized into one `牌坊 / 礼序` symbol. Their dates, biographies, locations and current evidence completeness differ. C2 changes the first-reading unit from “this is a chastity archway” to:

> **Why do monuments in the same broad commemorative category produce different readings when biography, location, material evidence and missing evidence are compared?**

### C2 failure condition

If participants only identify formal differences (roof shape, color, size) and do not generate a question about biography, institution, location, evidence or interpretation rights, C2 fails.

### C2 decision

`KEEP AS LEAD EXTENSION / EVIDENCE COMPLETENESS GATE REQUIRED`.

Do not hide the third object's missing full-context image. The gap is part of the test.

## Real-photo A/B/C prototype

### Shared visual control

A/B/C all use the same Zhenxiao Archway real source photograph. No generated reconstruction is used.

### A｜Symbol / Lecture Control

First-reading unit: `贞孝牌坊 → 榜 → 礼序`.

Action: title and explanatory conclusion are shown first; participant repeats the supplied meaning.

**Status:** `KEEP AS CONTROL / REJECT AS PRIMARY`.

Failure mode: the interpretation is decided before the material is read; removing the label/graphic weakens the route.

### B｜Evidence Reading

First-reading unit: `geometry / inscription / relief / material / context`.

Action: select one visible trace and classify the response as `direct observation / source statement / inference / unknown`; then record what changed the initial judgement.

**Status:** `KEEP / REVISE`.

Success condition: at least one judgement is corrected because of a visible trace or a source-bound fact.

### C｜Relation / Behavior

First-reading unit: `approach → notice → question → compare → continue`.

Action: first look without supplied conclusion; choose one trace; ask who/what the monument regulated historically and who may explain it today; carry one unresolved question to C2, a local narrator or an archive.

**Status:** `KEEP AS LEAD CANDIDATE / HUMAN TEST NOT RUN`.

Success condition: the output is not a summary but a traceable unanswered question with a next verifier.
