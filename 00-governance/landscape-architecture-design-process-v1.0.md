## 1｜Why this process exists

Landscape architecture is not a planting plan placed around a completed building. It is a living, time-dependent professional system linking land rights and survey, topography, soils, water, ecology, movement, public use, accessibility, planting, hardscape, utilities, structures, stewardship, construction, establishment and long-term change.

Canonical chain:

```text
LAND / RIGHTS / EVIDENCE BASIS
→ SITE + COMMUNITY + ECOLOGY / WATER BRIEF
→ LANDSCAPE CONCEPT / SYSTEM OPTIONS
→ SPATIAL + LEVELS / WATER / MOVEMENT COORDINATION
→ TECHNICAL LANDSCAPE DESIGN
→ PRODUCTION / PROCUREMENT / CONSTRUCTION ADMINISTRATION
→ COMPLETION / ESTABLISHMENT / HANDOVER
→ USE / MAINTENANCE / ECOLOGICAL PERFORMANCE
→ END-OF-USE / REUSE / RECYCLING WHEN LIFECYCLE SCOPE TRIGGERS
```

Hard boundaries:

```text
GIS / PUBLIC MAP ≠ VERIFIED SURVEY
PLANTING PLAN ≠ PLANT ESTABLISHMENT
SPECIES LIST ≠ ECOLOGICAL PERFORMANCE
CONCEPT DRAINAGE ≠ HYDRAULIC / CIVIL APPROVAL
BIODIVERSITY MODEL ≠ FIELD ECOLOGY VERIFICATION
RENDER ≠ LANDSCAPE PERFORMANCE
COMPLETION ≠ LIVING-SYSTEM MATURITY
LANDSCAPE PASS ≠ PLANNING / CIVIL / GEOTECH / ECOLOGY / STATUTORY PASS OUTSIDE RESPONSIBILITY
LANDSCAPE PROCESS PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP
```

## 2｜Professional-source alignment and stage identity

The Landscape Institute Digital Plan of Work combines landscape scope-of-services thinking with RIBA project stages and landscape-specific information needs. Current LI Carbon Overlay guidance continues to organize landscape-carbon action by RIBA-aligned stages and extends lifecycle attention through end of use. OLEANDER therefore uses source-aligned stage semantics without claiming the LI has published these internal IDs:

```text
LA-PW0  Strategic Definition
LA-PW1  Preparation and Briefing
LA-PW2  Concept Design
LA-PW3  Spatial Coordination
LA-PW4  Technical Design
LA-PW5  Manufacturing and Construction / Construction Administration
LA-PW6  Handover / Establishment
LA-PW7  Use / Stewardship / Performance Evaluation
LA-EOL  End-of-use / reuse / recycling when lifecycle scope is triggered
```

These IDs do not synchronize by number with Architecture, Structure, MEP, Interior or any other profession. `LA-EOL` is an OLEANDER lifecycle carrier informed by current landscape-carbon guidance; it does not invent a new RIBA universal project stage.

## 3｜Trigger

Trigger Landscape Architecture when the project makes material claims about:

- external public/private space, access, arrival or open-space sequence;
- topography, grading, retaining or earthworks strategy;
- surface water, SuDS, water bodies or landscape drainage interfaces;
- soil, planting, tree, habitat or ecological systems;
- landscape character / visual effects where assigned;
- hardscape, site furniture, walls, steps, ramps, edges or external structures;
- external accessibility / inclusive-use routes;
- landscape lighting, irrigation or utilities interfaces;
- play/recreation/event/open-space programming;
- stakeholder/community engagement affecting landscape decisions;
- planting procurement, establishment, maintenance or replacement;
- landscape carbon, material reuse, biodiversity or water-performance claims;
- construction administration, inspections, defects, establishment or warranty;
- in-use landscape performance / stewardship.

## 5｜Cross-cutting Landscape control objects

### 5.1 Site / Landscape Evidence Register

Preserve `SOURCE → GEOMETRY → ATTRIBUTE → DATUM → DATE / VERSION → ACCURACY / CONFIDENCE → OWNER → APPLICABILITY → DESIGN EFFECT → RECHECK TRIGGER`.

### 5.2 Landscape Brief / Use–Community–Stewardship Matrix

Record users, seasonal/temporal states, event/use patterns, inclusion, stewardship, management capability, public/community needs, maintenance resources and safety/operational states.

### 5.3 Levels–Water–Earthworks Model

Coordinate building thresholds, accessible routes, roads/paths, earthworks, retaining, drainage, flood/water paths, tree/soil protection, underground services and adjacent land. “Cut/fill balance” is not a sufficient decision criterion.

### 5.4 Ecology / Soil / Planting Register

Track existing habitat/trees/soil, retention/removal, protection, soil profile and improvement, planting intent, species/provenance, establishment requirements, irrigation/water assumptions, seasonal state, replacement and monitoring.

### 5.5 Material / Carbon / Circularity Register

Track quantities, provenance, transport/earthworks where relevant, reused/retained material, embodied-carbon assumptions, durability, maintenance, future disassembly/reuse/recycling and uncertainty.

### 5.6 Engagement / Decision Register

Where stakeholders/community/landowners/operators materially shape the landscape, record who was engaged, what evidence was obtained, conflicting needs, design consequence and unresolved representation gaps. Engagement evidence does not become universal need.

## 6｜LA-PW0 — Strategic Definition

**Question:** What site, landscape, ecological, water, rights and stewardship constraints/opportunities materially affect the strategic project decision?

Work: identify fatal evidence gaps; assess retention/reuse/open-space opportunities; identify surveys/ecology/hydrology/soil/tree/community work needed; expose climate/water/carbon/biodiversity/stewardship questions; test whether proposed development strategy is compatible with landscape systems at a strategic ceiling.

**Native outputs:** strategic landscape constraints/opportunities map; investigation/survey brief; initial stakeholder/ecology/water risk register.

**Does not prove:** planning approval, verified ecology, detailed grading/drainage, plant establishment or public acceptance.

---

## 7｜LA-PW1 — Preparation and Briefing

**Question:** Is there an authoritative enough site/landscape brief and evidence basis to start concept options?

Develop:
- current site evidence register;
- landscape use/community/stewardship brief;
- survey/geotech/ecology/arboriculture/hydrology/soil requirements;
- statutory/rights interfaces;
- access/transport/fire/accessibility constraints;
- utilities/servicing constraints;
- climate/water/biodiversity/carbon objectives;
- maintenance/establishment capability;
- landscape responsibility / scope-of-services matrix.

**Native outputs:** Landscape Brief; evidence/gap register; responsibility matrix; engagement plan where triggered; initial performance/maintenance criteria.

**Does not prove:** selected concept, technical landscape design, ecological gain or drainage approval.

---

## 8｜LA-PW2 — Concept Design

**Question:** Which landscape concept credibly organizes landform, movement, water, ecology, use and material/planting systems?

Compare alternatives for:
- landform/levels and threshold relationships;
- routes, access, arrival, pause and activity;
- public/private/community use and safety;
- water/SuDS strategy;
- habitat/tree/soil retention and enhancement;
- hardscape/softscape balance;
- planting structure and seasonal/time evolution;
- external lighting/service interfaces;
- material/carbon/circularity consequences;
- maintenance/stewardship burden;
- planning/visual/community consequences.

**Native outputs:** landscape concept plans/sections; levels/water/ecology/movement diagrams; concept planting structure; material/carbon strategy; option comparison and engagement response where applicable.

**Does not prove:** hydraulic capacity, final species suitability, planting establishment, detailed civil/structural adequacy or planning consent.

---

## 9｜LA-PW3 — Spatial Coordination

**Question:** Are levels, water, routes, structures, planting/soil zones, services and building thresholds coordinated enough for other disciplines to rely on the landscape geometry?

Coordinate:
- precise thresholds and accessible routes;
- paths/steps/ramps/edges;
- grading, slopes, earthworks and retaining interfaces;
- surface/subsurface drainage and overland flow paths;
- tree/soil/root zones;
- utilities/irrigation/lighting;
- external structures, foundations and equipment;
- fire/emergency/service access;
- facade/roof/terrace interfaces;
- below-ground structures and waterproofing;
- maintenance/replacement access.

**Native outputs:** coordinated landscape plans/sections/model; levels/drainage strategy; hardscape/softscape zones; tree/soil/planting zones; service/structure interface register.

**Exit:** in-claim MAJOR/CRITICAL landscape interfaces meet required Integration maturity.

**Does not prove:** technical civil/drainage approval, final planting details or field construction.

---

## 10｜LA-PW4 — Technical Design

**Question:** Is the landscape technically resolved at the declared professional boundary for procurement/construction and establishment?

Develop:
- setting out and levels;
- paving/kerb/edge/step/ramp details;
- walls/retaining/external structures interfaces;
- drainage/SuDS landscape components within responsibility;
- soil profiles and preparation;
- tree pits/rooting/structural-soil interfaces;
- planting plans, schedules, specifications and establishment requirements;
- irrigation/water strategy where assigned;
- site furniture/fixtures;
- lighting/MEP interface details;
- accessibility/safety interfaces;
- habitat/ecology mitigation/enhancement requirements;
- material specification and circularity/carbon information;
- maintenance/establishment specification;
- mockup/sample/test requirements.

**Native outputs:** technical landscape drawings/model; specifications; planting/tree/soil/material schedules; details; performance and maintenance requirements; technical review/evidence refs.

**Hard REVISE:** levels conflict with buildings/access/water; drainage path unresolved; planting relies on unknown soil/water; root zones clash with utilities/structure; material/product assumptions stale; design claims ecological/hydraulic performance beyond evidence.

**Does not prove:** contractor means/methods, installed quality, plant survival, hydraulic/civil approval outside scope or statutory acceptance.

---

## 11｜LA-PW5 — Manufacturing and Construction / Construction Administration

**Question:** Do procurement, specialist information and constructed landscape preserve the technical intent, living-system prerequisites and approved evidence basis?

Work includes, as appointed:
- contractor/submittal/shop information review;
- material/plant/tree/nursery/source approvals;
- sample/mockup review;
- soil/material test evidence;
- site queries and design changes;
- construction-contract administration;
- site inspections within scope;
- grading/drainage/soil/root-zone/planting installation readback;
- substitutions and nonconformities;
- protection of retained trees/habitats/soil/water systems;
- establishment prerequisites.

**Native outputs:** submittal/sample register; nursery/plant/material evidence; site-review records; change/nonconformity decisions; construction-stage design updates; observed installation evidence.

**Does not prove:** unobserved workmanship, concealed conditions, future plant establishment or ecological outcome.

---

## 12｜LA-PW6 — Handover / Establishment

**Question:** Is the landscape handed over with defects, establishment obligations, maintenance/stewardship and monitoring responsibilities explicit?

Landscape completion is not biological maturity. Handover must preserve:
- snagging/defects state;
- as-built/record information where assigned;
- plant/tree/soil/material identity;
- watering/establishment requirements;
- seasonal operations;
- replacement/failed-plant process;
- maintenance/stewardship responsibilities;
- monitoring requirements;
- biodiversity/water/carbon evidence boundaries.

**Native outputs:** landscape handover record; establishment/maintenance plan; plant/material records; defect/replacement register; monitoring plan.

**Does not prove:** successful establishment, future biodiversity, mature canopy/visual effect or long-term maintenance quality.

---

## 13｜LA-PW7 — Use / Stewardship / Performance Evaluation

**Question:** What does actual seasonal use and living-system performance reveal about the landscape, and which design/maintenance assumptions need correction?

Review:
- plant survival/growth/health;
- soil condition and compaction;
- water/drainage/flooding/drought behavior;
- biodiversity/habitat evidence where monitored;
- route desire-lines, accessibility and conflict;
- public/community use and safety;
- material wear and defects;
- maintenance workload/cost;
- tree/root/service conflicts;
- heat/shade/microclimate where measured;
- stewardship/operational change.

**Native outputs:** seasonal/in-use landscape assessment; establishment/defect records; performance comparison; corrective actions; bounded G9 lessons.

Each review closes only its observation window and evidence scope.

---

## 14｜LA-EOL — End-of-use / Reuse / Recycling

Trigger only when lifecycle/end-of-use scope is material. Review retention, relocation, reuse, salvage, recycling, soil handling, tree/plant relocation where realistic, material recovery, demolition/removal effects and carbon/waste consequences.

`LA-EOL` is a lifecycle carrier, not a claim that every project has a separate statutory stage.

**Does not prove:** future reuse markets, guaranteed recovery rates or ecological restoration without evidence.

## 15｜Cross-disciplinary interface contract

| Interface | Shared decisions |
|---|---|
| Architecture | building thresholds, courts, terraces, entries, levels, outdoor programme, envelope edges |
| Civil / Hydrology | drainage, SuDS, roads, earthworks, utilities, flood and water systems |
| Geotechnical | ground, slopes, retaining, soil/groundwater/contamination |
| Structure | retaining/canopies/external structures, loads, foundations, below-ground conflicts |
| MEP | utilities, irrigation, drainage, external plant, lighting, heat rejection, service routes |
| Fire/Life Safety | emergency/fire-service access, external egress/assembly and landscape fire interfaces |
| Accessibility | continuous external routes, gradients/levels, rest/use and inclusive participation |
| Ecology/Arboriculture | habitat/tree survey, protection, mitigation/enhancement and monitoring |
| Planning/Heritage | statutory landscape/visual/heritage obligations |
| FM/Operations | establishment, maintenance, access, replacement, stewardship resources |
| Cost/Procurement | quantities, nursery/material supply, earthworks, maintenance and lifecycle |

Actual maturity remains per interface / Acceptance Contract; landscape stage IDs do not own Integration state.

## 16｜Shared DD / design-quality binding

Landscape engages shared DD strongly through intent, concept, experience, human relation, sensory quality, form/composition, design language, material/craft, adaptation/time and integration. Technical or ecological evidence does not automatically yield Design KEEP; visual quality cannot override water, ecology, access, maintenance or evidence boundaries.

---

## 17｜Professional receipt / mandatory HOLD

Receipt minimally preserves: stage/baseline, Site/Landscape Evidence Register, Landscape Brief, responsibilities, levels/water/earthworks state, ecology/soil/planting refs, carbon/material refs where triggered, engagement refs, native outputs, active interfaces, site/establishment evidence, reviews/readbacks, professional verdict, open/stale/reopen items, claim ceiling and `does_not_prove`.

Mandatory REVISE/HOLD when applicable:
- boundary/survey/datum conflict remains;
- geotech/water/ecology/tree/soil evidence is missing but a strong claim depends on it;
- thresholds/access/levels/drainage conflict;
- underground services/root/structure conflicts remain;
- planting concept ignores soil/water/maintenance reality;
- field ecology or hydraulic claims are inferred from model/render only;
- critical stakeholder/statutory conflict is unresolved within claim;
- construction substitution breaks material/plant/soil/water assumptions;
- establishment or monitoring evidence is missing for in-use claims.
