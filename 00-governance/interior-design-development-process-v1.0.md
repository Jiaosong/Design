## 0｜Professional intent

Develop interior environments as real occupied, furnished, material, serviced and maintainable systems rather than decorative fit-out layers.

The document is intentionally deeper than a stage checklist. A stage only closes when the underlying professional relation is actually developed, represented in an appropriate native carrier, read back, and bounded by explicit failure/reopen conditions.

`PROCESS DOCUMENTED ≠ PROFESSIONAL DESIGN DEVELOPED`.

`NATIVE ARTIFACT EXISTS ≠ ACTUAL CONDITION READ BACK ≠ PROFESSIONAL PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

### In scope
- interior brief, activity and room-use translation;
- space planning, thresholds, circulation and privacy;
- FF&E, equipment, storage and ergonomic fit;
- material/finish/CMF selection with performance and failure reasoning;
- floor/wall/ceiling/joinery/detail assemblies;
- lighting/acoustic/thermal/MEP and controls integration;
- wet/hygienic/high-wear/special-use interior conditions;
- samples, mockups, specifications, procurement and substitutions;
- installation/site review, defects, handover and in-use learning;

### Explicitly outside this owner's authority
- architectural base-building authority;
- structural or MEP engineering approval;
- fire-life-safety or accessibility certification;
- lighting-engineering or acoustic-engineering approval;
- manufacturer marketing claims as automatic material proof;
- cost certainty, statutory approval or construction completion;

Where another professional/statutory owner controls a criterion, this process records the shared variable, geometry/material/design consequence, required maturity and current evidence. It does not copy or reissue that owner's PASS.

## 1｜Evidence and decision discipline

For every material professional decision, preserve:

`QUESTION / USE / FAILURE RISK → CURRENT SOURCE + ASSUMPTIONS → REAL ALTERNATIVES OR DESIGN MOVE → NATIVE GEOMETRY / MATERIAL / CALCULATION / SAMPLE / MOCKUP → ACTUAL READBACK → STRONGEST CONTRADICTION → BOUNDED VERDICT → HANDOFF → REOPEN TRIGGER`.

Rules:
- quantitative thresholds must carry Current source, unit/metric, applicability and tolerance/condition; this process does not invent universal numbers.
- manufacturer/vendor data remains product/configuration evidence, not independent proof of installed/project performance.
- rendered or diagrammatic appearance cannot substitute for geometry, material, physical, field, calculation or specialist evidence when those are in claim.
- one observed location, mockup, season, user condition or project does not automatically represent unobserved work, future performance or universal practice.
- project observations remain observation until causal explanation is supported; reusable learning routes only as a bounded G9/R-B candidate with transfer boundary and counterevidence.
- R-F remains the actual interface maturity/disposition owner; this domain records stage-side requirements only.
- professional development can remain bounded while specialist/statutory items are OPEN only if those OPEN items do not contradict the current claim ceiling.

## 2｜Deep professional decision body

The following sections are the professional substance. They are not replaced by stage completion, matrix coverage or a receipt.

### 2.1｜Activity, room archetype and operating-state model

- Start from what people actually do in the room, not the room label. Record actors, tasks, duration, sequence, simultaneous activities, supervision/privacy, peak occupancy, cleaning/service states and after-hours or degraded states where material.
- Separate fixed requirements from design hypotheses. A named room type does not establish furniture count, workstation posture, acoustic need, storage need or technology load without a current brief/source.
- For repeat room types, define an archetype only after verifying which variables are genuinely repeatable and which vary by orientation, equipment, user cohort, daylight, services or local context.
- Identify the dominant task plane, reach zone, viewing relation, social distance and privacy/control requirement where those materially shape geometry.
- Model transition states such as arrival, coat/bag storage, setup, use, reset, cleaning, maintenance and evacuation/closure interfaces rather than validating the occupied hero state only.
- Attack a room brief by asking what happens when the least convenient legitimate task is performed, when furniture is moved, when a mobility aid or service cart enters, and when a device/equipment door is open.
- Room-use acceptance requires a furnished/equipped native plan or model plus a bounded task readback; an empty room polygon cannot close interior usability.

**Decision attack:** if the current activity, room archetype and operating-state model can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.2｜Space planning, circulation, thresholds and privacy

- Resolve usable clear zones after furniture, joinery, door swings, equipment, columns, services, curtains and temporary occupation are present; gross room area is not a substitute for usable geometry.
- Treat circulation as a sequence of approach, decision, passage, pause, transfer and recovery conditions. Check queueing, waiting, turning, passing, opening, carrying and cleaning/service movements when material.
- At thresholds, coordinate floor level/finish change, door/frame, skirting/base, acoustic/privacy boundary, light transition, controls, signage, access hardware and cleaning strategy as one condition.
- Privacy is multi-channel: visual, acoustic, territorial, informational and operational. A full-height partition may still fail privacy through glazing, door gaps, shared waiting, sightlines, controls or sound paths.
- Wayfinding and orientation should emerge from spatial hierarchy, landmarks, sightlines, light, material and naming before adding signage as a repair layer.
- Open-plan layouts require explicit territory, noise, glare, distraction, supervision and retreat logic; 'flexible' cannot mean unresolved allocation.
- Where movable, operable or demountable partitions materially change room use, design the **change states** rather than validating only the open and closed photographs. Read open, closed, partially deployed, parked/stored and changeover conditions against furniture, circulation, door/egress and accessible-route geometry, floor/ceiling tracks, seals, controls, AV/display, lighting/MEP devices, acoustic/privacy boundaries and the actual parking/storage position. Record who is expected to reconfigure the partition, the handling/setup sequence, what must move first, what temporary obstruction or supervision occurs and how the room returns to a known usable state. Interior owns the spatial, furniture, finish and changeover-use consequence; Architecture, Fire/Life Safety, Accessibility, Acoustics, Structure, MEP and the partition specialist retain their respective performance/approval criteria. A room is not genuinely flexible if one legitimate configuration creates an unplanned residual corridor, inaccessible control, unusable furniture field or uncoordinated specialist boundary.
- Any material change to partitions, doors, furniture families, ceiling height, room function or occupancy reopens affected circulation/privacy evidence.

**Decision attack:** if the current space planning, circulation, thresholds and privacy can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.3｜Human factors, inclusion and accessibility-aware interior fit

- Translate population/task evidence into actual reach, clearance, posture, grip, force, visibility and cognitive-demand variables; do not use one average mannequin as universal proof.
- Percentile or dimensional source selection follows the failure mode and target population. The process carries source/applicability; it does not install one universal percentile rule.
- Check approach and use, not only nominal clearance: transfer, side/front approach, hand use, line of sight, companion/carer condition, assisted use and recovery from error may matter.
- Where seated and standing use coexist, test control/display/work surfaces for both rather than assuming adjustability exists because a product is marketed as ergonomic.
- Decompose each critical task into approach → position → reach/grasp → operate/view → transfer/handling → exit/reset, and test the combined body + object + furniture/equipment operating envelope rather than a static clearance box.
- Distinguish nominal reach from repeated reach, precision/force demand, hand preference, posture change, visual angle and recovery from a mis-operation where these can alter layout or product choice.
- Where adjustability is relied on, record who adjusts it, under which use state, what range/locking/reset behavior is assumed and whether the setting remains usable after turnover between users; marketed adjustability alone is not fit evidence.
- Do not validate critical fit only with one small/large body case or one wheelchair/mannequin profile. Where population variation is material, test combinations that can control different parts of the task: stature, seated/standing eye and reach position, hand size/grip, mobility-device envelope, body breadth, carried objects, companion/carer presence, temporary impairment or task-specific clothing/PPE where actually in scope. Check simultaneous-use conditions as well as isolated users: passing, side-by-side work, assisted transfer, shared controls and one user operating while another occupies the adjacent clearance. Select task-specific anthropometric/human evidence from the Current source appropriate to the target population and failure mode; Interior owns spatial/product fit while Accessibility or clinical/ergonomic owners retain their formal criteria;
- Interior may resolve spatial/product fit and task usability; statutory accessibility criteria or specialist clinical/occupational ergonomic limits remain with their Current sources/owners.
- Inclusive design evidence is distinct from statutory accessibility certification. Interior Design must preserve the geometry and operating assumptions required by the accessibility owner without self-awarding compliance.
- High-cognitive-load environments require information hierarchy, control grouping, sensory distraction and recovery/escape to be treated as design variables.
- Representative task simulation or physical mockup is required when critical fit cannot be established credibly from drawings/model alone.

**Decision attack:** if the current human factors, inclusion and accessibility-aware interior fit can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.4｜FF&E, equipment, storage and serviceability

- Every material furniture/equipment item needs a role: task, support, storage, boundary, collaboration, rest, display, service, mobility or identity. Decorative occupancy must not consume required task clearances.
- Use actual or bounded product dimensions, movement envelopes, access panels, drawers/doors, cable/service zones, replacement paths and anchorage/support assumptions where they affect layout.
- Storage is a capacity and access problem, not leftover joinery. Record stored object type, volume/quantity basis, frequency, security, ventilation, weight, replenishment and retrieval posture when material.
- Derive storage capacity from the actual object classes, quantity/volume basis, replenishment cycle, peak/seasonal state, security/segregation need and retrieval sequence; external cabinet volume is not usable storage capacity.
- Read usable internal capacity after shelves, drawers, hinges/runners, door thickness, ventilation/service voids, protective liners and other fittings are present, and test loading/retrieval without blocking adjacent task or circulation space.
- Separate daily-access, occasional, bulk/replenishment, personal, consumable and equipment storage where their reach, security or servicing consequences differ; test both full-storage and replenishment/removal states.
- For operational inventory, model the replenishment loop rather than only the final stored quantity: delivery/receiving source → bulk/secure hold where applicable → decant or issue → point-of-use storage → return/used-item segregation → packaging/waste/removal. Test replenishment during the actual occupied/service state, including carts/totes, temporary staging, doors/drawers open, stock counting/inspection, rotation/expiry or lot separation where the Operations owner requires it, and the peak condition immediately before/after replenishment. Define where overflow is allowed rather than letting corridors, worktops or accessible clear zones become informal buffer stock. Operations/Service/Clinical or other applicable owner controls stock policy, quantity, replenishment frequency, security and rotation rules; Interior owns the storage geometry, access sequence, staging/parking and spatial consequence those requirements create.
- Mobile/reconfigurable FF&E needs a credible home/parking position, transport/stacking/nesting envelope and charging/service condition where applicable so flexibility does not create uncontrolled corridor or room occupation.
- Check furniture density at real use states, including chairs pulled out, bags, mobile whiteboards, equipment parked, trolleys, waste/recycling and temporary teaching/event configurations.
- Built-in joinery and loose FF&E require a responsibility boundary for power/data, lighting, sprinkler/detector conflicts, structural support, fixing substrate and floor/ceiling interfaces.
- Selection based on image/catalog alone is HOLD when durability, cleanability, fire/accessibility, service, repair or supply continuity materially affects the project.
- A substitution reopens fit, material, color/reflectance, maintenance and interface evidence whenever the substitute changes more than a non-material cosmetic attribute.
- For procurement-critical FF&E, carry the conditions that actually govern fit: overall and operating envelope, assembled/disassembled state, weight/load assumption, leveling/glide/castor condition, anchorage/service interface, delivery path and field-assembly space where material.
- Check the selected item against Current floor level/flatness, skirting/base projections, wall/column irregularity, sockets/data/floor boxes, door/lift/loading access and adjacent furniture—not only nominal room dimensions. Where supplier tolerance, upholstery/compression, adjustable feet, castors or site variance can change alignment/reach/clearance, identify the adjustment mechanism and the site-measure/sample-unit hold point before bulk release. Procurement acceptance or catalog dimensions do not prove installed fit; Structure/MEP/Accessibility remain owners of their controlled criteria.

**Decision attack:** if the current ff&e, equipment, storage and serviceability can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.5｜Material and finish selection by exposure and failure mechanism

- Select materials from function + exposure + life + maintenance + failure consequence, not from sample-board appearance alone.
- For floors consider expected traffic, wheeled loads, contamination/water, slip condition, acoustic contribution, transitions, edge damage, cleaning regime, repairability and replacement phasing as applicable.
- For walls consider impact, abrasion, staining, touch frequency, cleanability, acoustic/perforated behavior, substrate, fixing, corner protection, joints, wet exposure and repair visibility.
- For worktops/joinery consider heat, moisture, chemicals, scratching, edge failure, joint swelling, fastener pull-out, unsupported span, sealant interfaces and field-cut exposure where relevant.
- For soft finishes consider wear path, soiling, cleaning chemistry, seam behavior, flammability authority, acoustic role, replacement tile/panel strategy and color/pattern batch risk where material.
- Datasheet property does not equal installed performance. Thickness, substrate, adhesive, grout, sealer, edge detail, workmanship, moisture and cleaning practice can control failure.
- Record rival failure hypotheses and the detail/maintenance action that mitigates them; do not call a material 'durable' without stating the exposure and failure modes in claim.

**Decision attack:** if the current material and finish selection by exposure and failure mechanism can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.6｜Floor, wall, ceiling and interior-envelope assembly logic

- Develop each major surface as an assembly with substrate, build-up, joint pattern, movement/tolerance, termination, penetration, access, backing/support, finish and maintenance consequence.
- Coordinate floor build-ups against thresholds, drains, doors, fixed equipment, accessibility transitions, skirtings/bases, moisture/waterproofing and future replacement.
- Coordinate partition thickness and composition against acoustic/privacy/fire/interface requirements, recessed equipment, doors/frames, services, structure and fixing loads without claiming specialist approval.
- Ceiling design must reconcile hierarchy, module/joint logic, lights, diffusers, grilles, sprinklers/detectors, speakers, access panels, tracks, curtains, signage, structure and maintenance reach.
- Do not scatter ceiling devices independently. Establish alignment/priority rules and permitted exceptions, then test them against actual service constraints.
- Movement joints, shadow gaps, trimless details and flush junctions require tolerance and buildability reasoning; visual minimalism cannot assume zero construction tolerance.
- A rendered seamless surface is presentation evidence only until native detail and assembly readback support the intended relation.
- At every specialist-controlled boundary, resolve the actual continuity path rather than relying on a wall/ceiling type tag. Check door/frame/reveal, glazing perimeter, ceiling/soffit termination, access panel, recessed joinery/equipment, skirting/base, service penetration, trim/shadow gap and movement joint where they can interrupt fire/smoke, acoustic, waterproofing/condensation or MEP access requirements.
- Record which finish, backing, seal, hatch, fixing, void closure or removable element belongs to Interior and which performance criterion/approval remains with Fire, Acoustics, MEP, Structure or another specialist. If an Interior detail changes the assumed enclosure, penetration, access, lining build-up or service path after specialist coordination, reopen that owner's affected evidence before retaining the detail.

**Decision attack:** if the current floor, wall, ceiling and interior-envelope assembly logic can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.7｜Joinery, casework, hardware and detailed craft

- Resolve carcass/face/support/fixing/edge/joint/hardware/service access as a system. Thin lines in elevation do not prove buildable joinery.
- Check door/drawer swing, hand clearance, internal fittings, hinges/runners, handle/edge grip, soft-close or restraint requirements, cleaning access and adjacent collision.
- Heavy, cantilevered, wall-hung or equipment-bearing elements require the correct structural/substrate owner and explicit fixing assumptions.
- Hardware selection must match frequency, abuse, security, accessibility, hygiene, corrosion/environment and replacement needs where material.
- Critical visible junctions need enlarged native details that show which material terminates, overlaps, returns, shadows, seals or remains demountable.
- Tolerance accumulation should be resolved at long runs, repeated bays, panel grids, reveals and interfaces to site-built base conditions; forcing the final module into a residual sliver is a design defect.
- Establish a datum hierarchy for each critical run: verified base-building/site datum, finished floor, wall/ceiling control lines and fabrication module. Do not allow successive trades or repeated bays to chase accumulated error without a declared reset point.
- Separate fabrication tolerance, substrate/site variance, installation adjustment and material movement/moisture behavior; allocate where mismatch is intentionally absorbed through scribe, trim, reveal, adjustable support or another detailed mechanism.
- For repeated panels/bays, define start/end conditions and the permitted residual-adjustment location so hardware, appliance/equipment openings, door clearances and critical visual modules are not consumed by the final tolerance error.
- Where site-built geometry controls fabrication, require a site-measure/hold-point before release and read back the installed alignment, reveals, hardware adjustment and service-access relation. Structural/fixing capacity remains with the responsible Structure/substrate owner.
- For edges, seals and transitions exposed to repeated touch, impact, cleaning or moisture, resolve the failure mechanism over use rather than only the initial flush fit. Check exposed/end-grain or field-cut edges, laminate/veneer returns, worktop and sink junctions, cabinet/plinth bases, door fronts, panel corners, sealant interfaces and hardware zones for differential movement, swelling/shrinkage, racking, peeling/chipping, capillary wetting, dirt capture and loss of seal continuity where applicable. Detail where movement is absorbed, where an edge is protected or sacrificial, what can be resealed/replaced independently and how repair avoids propagating damage into adjacent finish or waterproofing. Interior may define the material/detail consequence and maintenance access; waterproofing, structural fixing and specialist material-performance approval remain owner-native;
- Prototype or first-article review is required when finish quality depends on fabrication method, edge treatment, grain/pattern match, metalwork, upholstery or custom hardware behavior.

**Decision attack:** if the current joinery, casework, hardware and detailed craft can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.8｜Wet, hygienic, food, high-wear and special-use interiors

- Wet areas require a continuous relation among falls, drain locations, waterproofing interfaces, thresholds, skirtings/upstands, penetrations, access panels, sealants, slip/cleaning condition and fixture clearances.
- Do not infer waterproofing or drainage performance from finish drawings. Coordinate exact specialist/MEP authority and keep the interior claim to geometry/material/interface unless tested evidence supports more.
- Resolve the wet-area plan and section as one continuity chain from dry floor through threshold to wet zone: finish/build-up, fall strategy, drain body/interface, floor-wall junction, membrane/upstand or specialist waterproofing return, penetrations, screen/partition bases, fixture fixings, sealant/movement joints and removable access where applicable.
- Distinguish finish-surface geometry from hydraulic/drainage design. Interior must prove that tile/finish module, build-up depth, threshold, local falls and access remain geometrically compatible with the Current MEP/waterproofing design; drainage capacity, invert/hydraulic performance and waterproofing approval remain with their responsible owners.
- Attack ponding and cleanability risk at corners, door thresholds, channel/grating edges, pedestal/cabinet bases, sanitary fixtures, screens and other interruptions where a nominal room fall can be locally defeated.
- Where concealed valves, traps, cleanouts, sensors or service connections sit behind finished work, coordinate an access/removal route that can be used without destroying the waterproofing or adjacent finish benchmark unless destructive access is explicitly accepted.
- For cleaning-critical or contamination-sensitive areas, test the actual cleaning operation: body/tool approach, mop/cloth/nozzle reach, equipment parking, chemical/water exposure, waste removal, drying and reset. Identify ledges, deep reveals, tight equipment-to-wall/floor gaps, inaccessible undersides, porous or damaged edges, unsealed penetrations and removable panels/grilles whose detail can trap soil or moisture or prevent inspection. Where clean/dirty separation is in claim, coordinate the route and storage of cleaning tools, consumables and waste so the maintenance act does not cross the protected sequence it is meant to preserve. Interior owns cleanable geometry/material/detail response; infection-control, food-safety, hygiene protocol and validated decontamination criteria remain with their specialist owners;
- Food/service spaces require workflow, clean/dirty separation, equipment clearances, heat/moisture/grease exposure, ventilation/service interfaces, cleaning and waste logic appropriate to the project claim.
- High-wear public interiors should identify impact zones, queue/edge wear, replaceable sacrificial components, cleaning/storage logistics and repair access rather than merely specifying a harder finish.
- Healthcare/lab/workshop or other specialist spaces require task-specific contamination, chemical, impact, service and safety criteria from the proper authority; generic interior precedent is insufficient.
- Toilet/changing/shower fit must be checked furnished with dispensers, bins, grab/support elements, door swings, transfer/approach conditions, cleaning and maintenance access.
- Aesthetic continuity can be deliberately broken where hygiene, safety, maintainability or specialist performance requires a different assembly; record the reason instead of hiding the exception.

**Decision attack:** if the current wet, hygienic, food, high-wear and special-use interiors can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.9｜Acoustic, lighting, thermal and sensory integration

- Interior owns the spatial/material consequence of sensory goals but not specialist engineering approval. Translate target experience into absorptive/reflective surfaces, enclosure, zoning, views, brightness hierarchy, tactile contact and control interfaces.
- Acoustic privacy requires source-path-receiver reasoning across partitions, doors, glazing, ceilings, return-air paths and room adjacency; acoustic panels added late cannot repair a broken separation strategy.
- Separate room-acoustic control from sound isolation/privacy. Absorption or reverberant behavior inside a room cannot by itself prove separation from an adjacent source, and high-isolation construction does not by itself create suitable internal room acoustics.
- For privacy-critical adjacencies, map the material source and receiver states plus direct and flanking paths through doors/undercuts, glazing and frames, ceiling/plenum routes, transfer grilles/ducts, service penetrations, access panels, operable partitions and shared return-air paths.
- Coordinate the interior consequences—partition/door/ceiling continuity, seals, thresholds, absorptive/reflective finishes, furniture zoning and service-interface detailing—against the Acoustics/MEP owners’ criteria. Interior does not self-award acoustic performance or test compliance.
- Re-read privacy after actual doors, ceiling/service penetrations and FF&E are coordinated; a nominal wall type on plan is insufficient when the installed path differs from the assumed enclosure.
- Lighting integration begins with visual tasks, surface reflectance, furniture/work-plane position, daylight/opening relation, ceiling geometry and scene intent before fixture styling.
- Coordinate luminaire positions, beam/brightness consequences, controls and maintenance with the Lighting owner; Interior must not use render brightness as photometric proof.
- Thermal comfort and air movement consequences can affect furniture placement, perimeter use, curtains, screens and user control. Route performance claims to MEP/environmental owners.
- At perimeter and high-contact zones, translate the owner-native thermal/environmental basis into actual **use-position consequences**. Read seats, desks, beds, waiting positions, counters and touch surfaces against glazing/external-wall/door exposure, direct solar or cold-radiant conditions, supply/return air paths, local downdraft or draft risk, radiant panels/emitters and seasonal/control states supplied by MEP/Envelope. Check whether furniture, curtains, screens, joinery or stored items block intended air movement/emitters or force occupants into a repeatedly avoided zone. For hand/arm/body contact, consider how material type, finish, thermal mass/conductivity, moisture and prolonged contact affect perceived comfort and whether a different contact material, geometry, buffer or placement is needed. Interior may change furniture placement, contact materials, screens and spatial use in response; MEP/Envelope retain thermal, condensation, airflow and environmental-performance authority, and specialist limits remain source-bound rather than invented here.
- Tactile, olfactory and acoustic material consequences should be intentional where material, especially in close-contact/high-dwell environments; decorative texture does not prove comfort or cleanability.
- Sensory claims require actual sample/mockup/site readback appropriate to the medium when material appearance changes under light, angle, scale or aging.

**Decision attack:** if the current acoustic, lighting, thermal and sensory integration can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.10｜Color, reflectance, pattern and design-language control

- Color selection is relational: adjacent surfaces, light spectrum, gloss/texture, viewing angle, daylight/electric-light state, scale and aging can change the perceived result.
- Record which surfaces are dominant, supporting, orienting, protective or replaceable. A palette board without spatial role is insufficient.
- Pattern and grain need scale/orientation/seam/repeat logic across actual panel/tile widths, corners, doors and junctions; cropped samples can conceal problematic repetition.
- Reflectance and finish affect both visual hierarchy and lighting performance. Coordinate actual material data where Lighting analysis depends on it rather than allowing a rendered material to become source truth.
- Use contrast deliberately for orientation, edge recognition, controls, hazards or visual hierarchy where applicable; accessibility thresholds remain with the Current governing source/owner.
- Design language should survive supplier changes and legitimate module variations through explicit invariant relations, not depend on one exact sample that cannot be procured.
- Any late value-engineering substitution that changes hue, gloss, texture, joint, module or reflectance reopens affected design-language and lighting readback.

**Decision attack:** if the current color, reflectance, pattern and design-language control can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.11｜Sample, mockup and benchmark strategy

- Choose sample scale based on failure risk: small swatch for basic color/material identity, larger panel for texture/repeat/gloss, junction mockup for edge/joint, room/zone mockup for combined spatial-lighting-acoustic/use behavior.
- A sample must record manufacturer/product/finish/batch or controlled identity, substrate/application assumptions and whether it is indicative, approved benchmark or installed comparison reference.
- Review samples under relevant illumination/viewing conditions where color, gloss or texture is material; office desk lighting is not adequate evidence for every installed condition.
- Mockups should answer explicit questions such as joint quality, tolerance, lighting reveal, acoustic integration, cleaning, touch, alignment, durability or maintenance access.
- For material/lighting-critical decisions, define the mockup as a **controlled scene**: exact material/product/batch and substrate/application, surface orientation/scale, adjacent finishes, representative viewing/task positions, daylight/electric-light condition, luminaire/source/control scene supplied by the Lighting owner and the question being judged.
- Review hue/neutrality, gloss/specular response, texture relief, shadow/reveal behavior, veiling reflection/glare tendency, apparent contrast and junction readability across the material conditions that can change the decision. Camera/photograph is documentation only; visual evidence comes from actual mockup/readback. A mockup can support Interior material/spatial judgment but does not establish photometric compliance or Lighting Engineering PASS. Reopen when material/batch/finish, luminaire/source, control scene, ceiling geometry or critical adjacency changes materially.
- Retain failed/rejected samples and the reason where they materially explain the final decision; approval history is design evidence, not clutter.
- Approved sample ≠ installed pass. Site work must still be compared with the benchmark for workmanship, jointing, substrate, color/batch and interface conditions.
- A digital render can previsualize but cannot replace physical sample evidence for tactile, reflectance, gloss, texture, edge, workmanship or actual-light behavior where these are in claim.

**Decision attack:** if the current sample, mockup and benchmark strategy can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.12｜Specification, procurement, substitution and installation control

- Specification must preserve performance and design intent through measurable/identifiable requirements, approved references, acceptable variation and required submittals rather than brand-name shorthand alone.
- Identify long-lead, custom, batch-sensitive, locally scarce, repair-critical or single-source items early enough to test alternatives before design lock.
- Submittal review must distinguish technical/document completeness from design approval and from specialist compliance approval.
- A proposed equal/substitute should be compared across the attributes that drove selection: geometry, module, thickness, joint, color/reflectance, texture, durability, cleaning, acoustic/light interaction, warranty/supply and replacement.
- Before reviewing a proposed substitute, classify the design-driving attributes as invariant, bounded-variable or secondary for this exact application. Equivalence is judged against those declared attributes and the Current assembly/interface, not against a product-family marketing description.
- Compare the exact proposed product/configuration for module and cut pattern, edge/joint behavior, thickness and build-up, weight/support/fixing consequence, color/reflectance/texture, cleaning chemistry, repair/spares, service/access implications and any specialist evidence that the current claim depends on.
- If an attribute remains unverified, keep that part of the substitution OPEN or conditional and identify the sample, mockup, technical submittal, revised detail/model or owner evidence required before it can be consumed downstream.
- A substitution that changes a coordinated interface must reopen the affected Architecture/MEP/Lighting/Acoustics/FM evidence at its own owner boundary; Interior acceptance of appearance or design intent cannot certify those domains or procurement/cost availability.
- Installation sequencing matters where one trade hides another interface, access panel, waterproofing, fixing or service path. Resolve inspectability before closure.
- Site review records observed work against exact drawing/spec/sample/revision. Observation of one area does not prove unobserved work or contractor-wide compliance.
- For fitted work whose quality depends on base-building geometry, define pre-install readiness/site-measure hold points for substrate level/flatness/alignment, openings, service positions, backing/fixing zones and other controlling site conditions before fabrication or irreversible installation proceeds.
- Record defects by exact location, current design/sample benchmark and failure type—fit/alignment, joint, finish/batch, damage, substrate, access, service/interface or other material cause—so a cosmetic symptom is not detached from the relation that actually failed.
- A repair/rework proposal must identify removal extent, retained work, new interfaces and the risk of damaging adjacent waterproofing, acoustic/fire construction, MEP services, substrates or benchmark finishes; touching another owner’s controlled relation reopens that owner/interface before closure.
- Read back the repaired condition against the same controlling geometry/detail/sample and record any accepted residual deviation. Repetition of the same defect should trigger a wider cause/scope review rather than treating every occurrence as an isolated punch item.
- Punch/defect closure requires readback of the repaired condition when the defect affected design intent, use, material performance or interface integrity.

**Decision attack:** if the current specification, procurement, substitution and installation control can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.13｜Maintenance, cleaning, replacement and lifecycle

- Design for the actual cleaning method, access, chemicals/equipment and frequency where these influence material, joint, furniture or detail selection.
- Identify components expected to wear first and whether they can be replaced without dismantling disproportionate adjacent work.
- Maintenance access must include body/tool approach, removal path, isolation/safe state and protection of adjacent finishes where material.
- Build maintainability around actual task classes: routine cleaning, inspection/adjustment, consumable replacement, repair, component replacement and planned reconfiguration. Each can require a different access opening, working position, tool path, isolation state and disruption zone.
- For maintenance-critical interior elements, record the access/removal sequence, adjacent-finish protection, service isolation dependency, replacement route and reinstatement consequence at the level needed to show that maintenance can occur without disproportionate demolition.
- Identify intended sacrificial/wear components and the interfaces designed to be demountable or renewed independently; preserve spare/batch/finish-reference information where later visual matching or discontinued products would materially affect repair quality.
- FM/Operations provides the Current maintenance method, frequency, cleaning equipment/chemistry and observed in-use evidence; Interior owns the resulting material/detail/access design response, while MEP isolation/service procedures remain with MEP.
- Record spare material, batch/lot, touch-up and replacement strategy when visual continuity or discontinued products would create material lifecycle risk.
- Future churn/flexibility should identify what can move, be refinished, be reconfigured or be demounted, and which fixed interfaces constrain change.
- Post-occupancy observations such as wear, clutter, improvised storage, glare avoidance, furniture migration and blocked access are signals to investigate, not automatic proof of a single cause.
- Read in-use change as a condition map, not a single defect count. Distinguish expected patina from unacceptable abrasion, gloss/color shift, UV/light exposure, staining, moisture, cleaning-chemistry attack, joint/sealant aging, edge damage, batch mismatch and conspicuous repair. Bind observations to location, exposure, use intensity, maintenance regime and installed product/batch where material.
- Separate material aging from user adaptation: furniture migration, added mats/protection, improvised storage, blocked vents/access or altered lighting may indicate task mismatch, maintenance practice or operational change rather than material failure alone. Classify adaptations as benign, performance-supporting, conflicting or safety/access/service-critical; only the affected design relation is reopened. FM/Operations owns operating/maintenance evidence; Interior owns the resulting spatial/material/detail response and does not infer specialist root cause without owner evidence.
- Reusable lessons route as candidates with counterevidence/transfer boundaries; one interior fit-out does not establish a universal detail or material rule.

**Decision attack:** if the current maintenance, cleaning, replacement and lifecycle can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

## 5｜Native output and readback hierarchy

Use the lowest-fidelity carrier that can actually test the relation, then increase fidelity only when the failure cannot be resolved otherwise. Depending on the question this may include editable plan/section/model/schedule, calculation, material sample, physical mockup, control/state model, shop/submittal data, field measurement, installation observation or in-use readback.

Preferred evidence order when the claim escalates:

`SOURCE / BASIS → EDITABLE NATIVE DESIGN → COORDINATED CURRENT CONFIGURATION → SAMPLE / MODEL / MOCKUP / ANALYSIS → INSTALLED / FIELD / RUNTIME READBACK → PROFESSIONAL INTERPRETATION`.

A derivative can support communication but cannot silently replace the authority-bearing/native carrier.

---

## 6｜Handoff and reopen semantics

A Interior Design handoff must identify the exact decision, shared variable, native source/revision, required receiving-owner maturity, remaining OPEN item and change that would invalidate the handoff. 'Coordinated' as free prose is insufficient.

Material changes propagate only through affected dependencies; they do not reset the whole process by default. Historical evidence remains truthful for its original condition and is never rewritten to impersonate a successor.

---

## 7｜Independent review and anti-overclaim

Independent review must attack the strongest plausible Interior Design failure, not simply confirm document completeness. Review should include adverse/edge/maintenance/field states appropriate to the claim and bind the exact native/configuration revision reviewed.

Hard boundaries:
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove architectural base-building authority.
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove structural or MEP engineering approval.
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove fire-life-safety or accessibility certification.
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove lighting-engineering or acoustic-engineering approval.
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove manufacturer marketing claims as automatic material proof.
- `INTERIOR DESIGN PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove cost certainty, statutory approval or construction completion.
- Definition/schema validation does not prove project exercise or professional PASS.
- One project success/failure does not establish a universal rule or causality.
- Candidate process validation does not change Current architecture state.
