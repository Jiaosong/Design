# OLEANDER Design Quality & Design Development Specification v1.0

**Status:** ACTIVE
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Scope:** all OLEANDER design work in which a material claim is made about design quality, professional design development, experience, form, visual language, spatial quality, product character, interaction quality, editorial quality, presentation quality, craft, distinctiveness or design readiness.
**Position:** execution specification beneath `design-intelligence-routing-and-review-v1.0.md` and above discipline/process-specific execution, Skills, artifacts and Design Review. It does not create a second Knowledge Architecture, Project Axis, professional discipline taxonomy, Artifact Review system, technical approval system or evidence authority.

**Runtime binding:** `complex-project-master-runtime-v1.0.md` invokes and tracks this shared design-development dependency; the Master does not absorb its design judgment or replace professional-domain process ownership.

---

## 1｜Why this specification exists

OLEANDER already separates Knowledge, Project, Design Review, Artifact Review, technical gates, evidence and promotion. A further design-development specification is required because a project can still appear complete while remaining aesthetically generic, formally unresolved, experientially weak, over-dependent on presentation polish, under-developed at detail scale, or incoherent across media and disciplines.

Typical false positives include:

- a correct brief with no strong design proposition;
- a coherent grid with no visual ownership;
- a consistent component system with no authorship or distinctiveness;
- a moodboard with no generative design rules;
- an attractive render whose spatial/formal system is weak;
- a strong hero with generic supporting pages;
- polished typography masking weak content hierarchy;
- a concept explained in prose but not visible in form;
- a technically solved detail that destroys the project design language;
- a strong single state that collapses under responsive, operational, seasonal or lifecycle variation;
- a visual identity reduced to color and font tokens;
- a culturally themed design that copies motifs without translating underlying relations;
- a process receipt used as proof that the resulting design is professionally strong.

Therefore OLEANDER treats design quality as an independent, developed state that must be constructed, compared, read back and judged.

Canonical invariants:

`CORRECT CONTENT ≠ STRONG DESIGN`
`TECHNICAL PASS ≠ DESIGN QUALITY`
`CONSISTENCY ≠ DISTINCTIVENESS`
`STYLE ≠ DESIGN LANGUAGE`
`MOODBOARD ≠ DESIGN DIRECTION`
`COMPONENT SYSTEM ≠ VISUAL AUTHORSHIP`
`DETAIL DENSITY ≠ CRAFT`
`RENDER REALISM ≠ FORMAL QUALITY`
`ONE STRONG HERO ≠ SYSTEM QUALITY`
`PROCESS PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`

### 2.1｜Knowledge-input admission boundary

Design development may consume only knowledge that has been resolved through the current Knowledge system at the level required by the task and claim. Existing knowledge pages are not automatically trusted merely because they are `CURRENT`, well written, linked from a project, or already used by another stage.

Keep these states independent:

`CONTENT COMPLETE ≠ KNOWLEDGE INTEGRITY VERIFIED`

`KNOWLEDGE INTEGRITY VERIFIED ≠ OPERATIONALLY ELIGIBLE FOR EVERY CLAIM`

`OPERATIONALLY ELIGIBLE KNOWLEDGE ≠ DQ MATURITY`

`DQ MATURITY ≠ PROFESSIONAL-STAGE COMPLETION`.

The runtime admission owner is `knowledge-integrity-and-operational-mount-v1.0.md`. It mounts the existing canonical corpus without copying knowledge into `DD-*` or professional-stage structures. Professional stages reference canonical knowledge objects through task/claim-scoped operational eligibility, applicability, claim ceiling, freshness and `does_not_prove` boundaries.

Knowledge pollution discovered during design execution must be routed back to the appropriate Knowledge owner. A design team may record and respect the issue, but Design Quality review must not silently repair graph authority, duplicate canonical ownership, provenance, lifecycle or source identity by treating the design artifact as proof.

## 3｜Three independent design-result axes

Every material design result must keep three axes separate.

### A｜Truth Axis

Question: **Are the claims, sources, assumptions and represented states truthful to the declared claim ceiling?**

Typical owners: source/evidence governance, research, standards, field verification, claim ledger.

### B｜Performance Axis

Question: **Does the design work for its required use, system, human, technical and lifecycle conditions?**

Typical owners: professional domain process, specialist engineering, HCD/usability, accessibility, operations, simulation, prototype/test.

### C｜Design Axis

Question: **Is the design intentional, coherent, experientially resolved, formally strong, distinctive, crafted and appropriate to context?**

Typical owners: professional design review, discipline-specific design lens, art direction, visual/spatial/product/interaction design review.

Rules:

- none of the axes may self-certify another;
- one axis may be `PASS` while another remains `REVISE / HOLD`;
- a safety, truth or required-performance blocker cannot be averaged away by Design strength;
- strong compliance or evidence cannot be used to force `DESIGN KEEP`;
- Design Axis uses professional judgment with traceable reasons, not a universal numerical score.

## 4｜Shared Design Development Kernel

The following twelve dimensions form the shared design-making kernel. They are **responsibility dimensions**, not a mandatory checklist with equal weight.

### DD-01 Intent

Resolve why the design exists, what change it should make, for whom, and what it explicitly does not seek to solve.

Required as applicable:

- design question;
- desired change;
- desired experience;
- non-goals;
- stakes;
- claim ceiling;
- success/failure condition.

Hard failure: visual/formal activity proceeds without a resolvable design question or consequential intent.

### DD-02 Concept / Generative Principle

Concept must generate or constrain design, not merely explain it after the fact.

Record:

- conceptual source;
- abstraction step;
- generative principle;
- transformation rule;
- invariants;
- controlled variables;
- rejected literal/metaphoric shortcuts;
- visible consequence in form/system/behavior.

`CONCEPT STORY ≠ GENERATIVE DESIGN RULE`.

### DD-03 Experience Architecture

Resolve the experience through time, space, use or interaction rather than as a static object only.

Possible sequence carriers include:

`approach → entry → orientation → engagement → intensification → pause → transition → return / exit → memory`.

Record experience moments only when applicable to the medium and task.

### DD-04 Form / Composition

Resolve primary formal relations such as:

- hierarchy and dominant field;
- figure / ground;
- scale and proportion;
- balance and tension;
- axis / alignment;
- cluster / isolation;
- overlap / crop / bleed / edge pressure;
- negative space;
- repetition / rupture;
- sequence / rhythm;
- mass / void / geometry for spatial work.

The grid, component library or CAD/BIM framework may support composition but may not become the design concept by default.

### DD-05 Human Relation

Where people materially use, inhabit, operate, read or encounter the design, resolve applicable relations such as:

- body / posture / reach;
- movement;
- gaze and field of view;
- proximity / territory / privacy;
- effort;
- affordance;
- control / feedback;
- social relation;
- accessibility / inclusion interfaces;
- comfort and cognitive demand.

A human silhouette, persona or accessibility label is not evidence that human relation has been designed.

### DD-06 Sensory Design

Resolve relevant sensory carriers rather than treating experience as visual-only.

Possible carriers:

- light and shadow;
- color;
- texture / touch;
- weight / resistance;
- temperature;
- sound / acoustics;
- air / movement;
- material reflectivity / transparency;
- smell or other project-relevant sensory conditions.

Not every project needs every carrier. Unused carriers should not be fabricated for completeness.

### DD-07 Design Language / Style

Resolve the reusable formal grammar that produces recognizable project character.

Design Language may include:

- geometry grammar;
- proportion grammar;
- edge / termination grammar;
- surface / depth grammar;
- composition grammar;
- color character;
- typography character;
- image language;
- material language;
- light language;
- motion character;
- spatial grammar;
- detail / joint language;
- signature moves;
- variation envelope;
- forbidden moves.

`STYLE` is the perceived result; `DESIGN LANGUAGE` is the rule system capable of producing and adapting that result. `ART DIRECTION` is the project-specific orchestration of image, framing, material, light, color, typography, sequence and expressive intensity that makes the language perceptible in a given medium or experience. Art Direction may vary by project and medium but must remain traceable to the Current Design DNA when one exists.

### DD-08 Detail / Craft

Resolve close-range and transition conditions appropriate to the design medium.

Review as applicable:

- edge;
- joint;
- seam;
- gap;
- termination;
- opening;
- transition;
- fastening / assembly expression;
- tolerance;
- touch point;
- finish;
- micro hierarchy;
- micro interaction;
- caption/legend/line/spacing finish;
- construction or production consequence.

The detail should not contradict the project-level concept unless the contradiction is an explicit design decision.

### DD-09 Prototype / Design Experiment

Prototypes may serve different roles and must be typed accordingly:

- `DISCOVERY`;
- `COMPARISON`;
- `VALIDATION`;
- `COMMUNICATION`;
- `PRODUCTION`.

For design experiments record:

`question → variable → fixed conditions → candidates → observation → unexpected behavior → design consequence → validation ceiling`.

`PROTOTYPE EXISTS ≠ DESIGN VALIDATED`.

### DD-10 Adaptation / Variation

Test whether the design survives legitimate variation.

Possible stress states:

- large / small;
- sparse / dense;
- desktop / mobile;
- day / night;
- individual / crowd;
- normal / degraded / closed;
- seasonal / weather state;
- new / aged;
- concept / production;
- different content lengths / languages;
- different room/use states.

Record invariants, variables, ranges, context rules, exceptions and failure boundaries.

### DD-11 Meaning / Memory / Cultural Reading

Where relevant, define what should be recognized, associated, remembered or culturally legible.

Record:

- intended meaning;
- cultural/context basis;
- design carrier;
- literal vs abstract translation;
- possible conflicting reading;
- misuse/cliché risk;
- memory carrier;
- repetition rule;
- transfer boundary.

`CULTURAL REFERENCE ≠ LICENSE TO COPY MOTIF`.

### DD-12 Integration / Coherence

Review whether the project operates as one design rather than a set of individually polished parts.

Check relations among:

`Intent ↔ Concept ↔ Form ↔ Function ↔ Human Use ↔ Material ↔ Detail ↔ Experience ↔ Expression ↔ Technical System`.

`LOCAL QUALITY ≠ SYSTEM COHERENCE`.

## 6｜Design Direction & Aesthetic Position

Design direction must translate qualitative intent into visible or experiential consequences.

Weak form:

`calm / premium / natural / modern / poetic`.

Required form:

`desired quality → formal consequence → allowed range → exception → failure sign`.

Example:

`quiet → low competing salience + longer pauses + restrained contrast → hero moments may intensify → fail when every module competes equally`.

Useful aesthetic tensions may include, when relevant:

- controlled ↔ expressive;
- minimal ↔ dense;
- quiet ↔ dramatic;
- precise ↔ organic;
- formal ↔ informal;
- tactile ↔ immaterial;
- static ↔ dynamic;
- monumental ↔ intimate;
- polished ↔ raw;
- consistent ↔ varied;
- familiar ↔ novel;
- contextual ↔ iconic.

For each material tension record:

`chosen position / reason / design consequence / accepted cost / allowed variation / reopen condition`.

Do not reduce these to automated 1–10 aesthetic scores.

## 7｜Design Language / Formal Grammar Contract

Where a repeated design language is material, resolve the minimum sufficient grammar rather than collecting style attributes.

### 7.1 Geometry grammar

Examples of variables: rectilinear/curved, orthogonal/oblique, continuous/fragmented, regular/irregular, sharp/soft, monolithic/articulated.

For each governing rule record `scope / reason / invariant / variable / exception / failure condition`.

### 7.2 Proportion grammar

Record recurring proportion behavior such as slender/wide, vertical/horizontal, compressed/open, deep/flat, large-scale jump vs close scale family.

### 7.3 Edge and termination grammar

Record rules for hard edge, radius, bevel, cut, fold, frame, open edge, bleed, reveal, joint and terminal conditions.

### 7.4 Surface and depth grammar

Record whether the project depends on flat fields, shallow layering, transparency, reflection, texture, deep spatial hierarchy, overlap, shadow or other depth relations.

### 7.5 Composition grammar

Record only project-relevant relations such as dominance, balance, tension, axis, field, cluster, isolation, overlap, crop, bleed, edge pressure, void, rhythm, repetition, rupture and scale jump.

### 7.6 Chromatic character

Separate semantic color roles from aesthetic color character. When material, record:

- temperature;
- saturation range;
- contrast range;
- lightness range;
- neutral bias;
- accent frequency;
- color field scale;
- color adjacency;
- material/image color relationship.

### 7.7 Typographic character

In addition to functional type roles, record project-specific character when material:

- voice;
- scale contrast;
- weight behavior;
- width/density;
- case behavior;
- spacing character;
- alignment;
- editorial behavior;
- numeric/technical treatment;
- CJK/Latin relationship.

### 7.8 Image language

Record applicable camera/art-direction relations:

- camera distance / height;
- viewpoint behavior;
- subject framing;
- people presence/absence;
- movement;
- depth;
- time / weather / light;
- contrast / focus / grain;
- crop behavior;
- documentary ↔ staged;
- literal ↔ abstract;
- whole ↔ detail sequence.

### 7.9 Material / sensory language

Record relevant relations among mass, weight, hardness, softness, roughness, reflectivity, transparency, aging, weathering, sound, touch, joint and light response.

### 7.10 Light language

Record relevant directionality, softness, contrast, shadow depth, ambient condition, highlight/reflection behavior, temperature relation and temporal change.

### 7.11 Motion character

Separate motion semantics from motion character. When material, record tempo, weight, inertia, continuity, elasticity, abruptness, delay, rhythm, choreography and frequency.

## 8｜Visual Ownership and Dominant Relation

For every material frame, page, board, screen, scene, view or comparable composition, resolve the intended primary visual owner where useful.

Candidate owner types include:

`IMAGE / FORM / TYPE / DATA / SPACE / MATERIAL / DIAGRAM / VOID / MOTION`.

The owner is not a mandatory metadata field for every artifact. It is a diagnostic when hierarchy is weak or multiple modules compete equally.

Hard warning conditions:

- repeated `headline + equal cards + small image` without project-specific reason;
- every page has equal visual mass;
- visual hierarchy is achieved only by changing font size;
- decorative effects create the only first-read signal;
- supporting evidence dominates the primary design claim;
- the designated owner is weak while secondary modules are louder.

---

## 9｜Signature, System, Exception and Experiment

Classify material design-language elements as one of:

- `SYSTEM_RULE` — recurring base grammar;
- `SIGNATURE_MOVE` — limited, memorable, high-recognition move;
- `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` — context-specific departure;
- `EXPERIMENT` — unpromoted candidate behavior;
- `LEGACY / SUPERSEDED` — no longer Current.

Rules:

- a successful signature move must not automatically become a system rule;
- repeating a signature everywhere may reduce it to a gimmick;
- a local exception must not silently mutate the global Design DNA;
- an observed recurring implementation pattern is not `SYSTEM_RULE` without authority or deliberate promotion;
- legacy patterns remain provenance, not parallel Current design language.

## 10｜Variation Envelope

Every material system rule should identify, where applicable:

```yaml
invariant:
variable:
allowed_range:
context_rule:
exception:
failure_boundary:
```

The design system should preserve identity without forcing uniformity.

Example:

`invariant = one dominant field`
`variable = owner may be IMAGE / TYPE / DIAGRAM`
`failure_boundary = two or more equal dominant fields make the reading hierarchy ambiguous`.

## 11｜Style Intensity and Aesthetic Rhythm

When a project contains several pages, spaces, screens, scenes or states, control expressive intensity rather than making every moment equally loud.

Optional style-intensity states:

- `S0 INFRASTRUCTURAL / NEUTRAL`;
- `S1 RESTRAINED`;
- `S2 CHARACTERISTIC`;
- `S3 EXPRESSIVE`;
- `S4 SIGNATURE`.

These are local project control states, not universal quality levels.

Possible aesthetic sequence:

`quiet → dense → release → focus → rupture → return`.

Spatial equivalent may be:

`compression → threshold → expansion → pause → framed view → release`.

The exact rhythm is project-specific. Do not impose fixed pacing formulas.

## 12｜Content, Information and Design Communication Binding

Design development must preserve the distinction among:

### Content Governance

What can be claimed, what evidence supports it, what remains uncertain and what must be retained.

### Information Architecture

What belongs together, hierarchy, order, canonical home, navigation, cross-reference and progressive disclosure.

### Language / Terminology

Wording, naming, technical precision, bilingual equivalence, voice profile and status/action language.

### Visual Communication

Hierarchy, composition, typography, image relation, diagram relation, color, density, reading distance and visual craft.

### Editorial / Publication

Long-form sequence, page/spread role, pacing, body measure, captions, folios, appendix/reference behavior and publication readback.

### Presentation / Projection

Audience, decision context, shown vs spoken, Claim–Proof relation, medium, density and release context.

### Interaction / Temporal Communication

State, action, consequence, feedback, recovery, motion and re-entry.

### Production / Delivery

Native source, editable master, export, delivery conditions, reproducibility and artifact persistence.

These systems may be triggered independently. A presentation artifact does not automatically require every editorial or interaction rule.

## 13｜Content Projection / No-loss Contract

When source content is projected into a board, deck, page, web screen, film, diagram or other communication surface, OLEANDER permits:

- reorder;
- summarize;
- layer;
- collapse;
- cross-reference;
- move depth to appendix/support;
- adapt wording to audience and medium without changing truth.

OLEANDER forbids:

- deleting a qualification that materially changes the claim;
- upgrading claim strength;
- removing contradictory evidence in a way that makes the projected claim misleading;
- erasing uncertainty or OPEN/HOLD state;
- changing source meaning;
- converting a proposal into implemented condition;
- converting simulation into field truth;
- using a decorative visual as evidence.

For consequential projection, classify information where useful as:

- `MUST_SHOW`;
- `MAY_COLLAPSE`;
- `MUST_RETAIN`;
- `MUST_COLOCATE`.

Typical `MUST_COLOCATE` pairs:

- claim ↔ critical limitation;
- number ↔ unit;
- requirement ↔ authority;
- figure ↔ legend;
- decision ↔ unresolved blocker;
- status ↔ reason;
- evidence result ↔ `does_not_establish` when omission would mislead.

## 14｜Design Content Depth and Density

Do not govern content by universal word counts.

Optional content-depth classes:

- `CD0 LOCATOR` — identity, location, status;
- `CD1 SUMMARY` — question, main claim, boundary;
- `CD2 OPERATIONAL` — inputs, method, output, conditions;
- `CD3 PROFESSIONAL` — mechanism, evidence, failure, limits, transfer, validation;
- `CD4 RESEARCH / EVIDENCE DEEP` — protocol, data, bias, uncertainty, counterevidence, reproducibility.

Optional reading-density classes:

- `DENSITY-A SIGNAL` — immediate conclusion;
- `DENSITY-B SCAN` — relation understandable by scanning;
- `DENSITY-C READ` — continuous explanatory reading;
- `DENSITY-D STUDY` — professional comparison/annotation;
- `DENSITY-E REFERENCE` — lookup-oriented detail.

These classes describe intended reading behavior. They do not create universal maximum word counts, character counts or page density scores.

## 15｜Content Role Profiles

When a design artifact contains formal body content, route it to the appropriate profile rather than one universal text template.

### 15.1 THEORY / FRAMEWORK

Minimum applicable responsibilities:

`Problem / Phenomenon → Definition → Conceptual Model → Key Constructs → Relations → Mechanism → Supporting Evidence → Competing Explanation → Boundary Conditions → Criticism / Failure → Design Interpretation → Misuse / Non-transfer → Validation / Maturity → Open Questions`.

### 15.2 METHOD

`Purpose → When to Use → When Not to Use → Preconditions → Inputs → Required Evidence → Roles → Procedure → Decision Points → Branch Conditions → Outputs → Validation → Failure Modes → Common Misuse → Tool/Capability Dependencies → Professional Judgment Boundary → Practice Example → Reopen Conditions`.

### 15.3 SOURCE

`Identity → Issuer/Author → Version/Edition/Date → Official Status → Scope → Jurisdiction/Applicability → Contents → What It Supports → What It Does Not Support → Relevant Locator → Freshness/Supersession → Access/Rights → Current OLEANDER Use`.

### 15.4 EVIDENCE

`Evidence Question → Provenance → Generation Method → Sample/Instrument/Model → Conditions → Raw/Derived Distinction → Result → Uncertainty → Quality/Bias → Reproducibility → Supports → Contradicts → Does Not Establish → Validation State → Availability`.

### 15.5 CASE / PRECEDENT

`Problem → Context → Selection Rationale → Source Hierarchy → Documented Facts → Design Move → Mechanism → Outcome → Failure/Limitation → Transferable Principle → Non-transferable Condition → Current Project Binding`.

Always distinguish:

`WHAT HAPPENED ≠ WHY IT WORKED ≠ WHAT WE SHOULD DO`.

### 15.6 PRACTICE

`Task → Initial State → Method Applied → Actual Execution → Native Artifact → Deviation → Result → Failure → Readback → Reviewer Finding → Lesson → Reuse Boundary`.

### 15.7 TOOL

`Identity → Version → Capability → Intended Task → Inputs → Outputs → Native Format → Exchange Format → Automation Surface → Runtime Requirements → Known Loss → Failure Conditions → Validation/Readback → Claim Ceiling → Alternatives → Deprecation/Freshness`.

### 15.8 STANDARD / CODE

`Authority → Jurisdiction → Edition → Effective Date → Scope → Relevant Clauses → Mandatory/Advisory Distinction → Interpretation Boundary → Project Applicability → Conflict/Precedence → Verification Method → Supersession/Amendment`.

### 15.9 PROJECT DECISION

`Question → Context → Authority → Alternatives → Evaluation Criteria → Evidence → Trade-offs → Selected Decision → Rejected Alternatives → Accepted Cost → Dependencies → Affected Artifacts → Affected Interfaces → Assumptions → Open Items → Reopen Condition → Reviewer → Date`.

### 15.10 DESIGN DEVELOPMENT BODY

`Design Question → Existing Condition → Evidence/Constraint → Design Intent → Design Criteria → Alternatives → Selected Move → Formal/Spatial/Functional Mechanism → Human Experience Consequence → Technical Consequence → Cross-disciplinary Impact → Trade-off → Risk → Assumption → Unresolved Item → Validation → Next Development Action`.

Profiles are semantic responsibilities, not mandatory visible headings.

## 16｜Paragraph and Sentence Truth Patterns

Preferred professional paragraph patterns include:

- `Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Boundary`;
- `Condition → Mechanism → Consequence → Exception`;
- `A ↔ Criterion ↔ B → Difference → Consequence`;
- `Question → Option → Trade-off → Decision → Reopen Condition`;
- `Known → Unknown → Why → Impact → Next Validation`.

Avoid:

- generic AI filler;
- decorative headings without substantive content;
- one-sentence paragraph spam;
- dense paragraphs containing several unrelated semantic moves;
- citation lists without synthesis;
- unsupported evaluative language;
- generic `Benefits / Challenges / Future Outlook` structure where it does not fit the object role.

Controlled strong terms require evidence at the claimed level:

`VALIDATED / VERIFIED / EVIDENCE-BASED / USER-TESTED / FIELD-VERIFIED / COMPLIANT / SAFE / OPTIMAL / PROVEN / BEST PRACTICE`.

When evidence is weaker, use bounded language such as:

`SUPPORTED BY / INDICATED BY / PRELIMINARY / PREDICTED / REFERENCE / CANDIDATE / ASSUMED / VALIDATION OPEN`.

## 18｜Typography, Reading Distance and Bilingual Design

Typography must be role-based rather than an arbitrary heading-size ladder.

Typical roles include:

`DISPLAY / SECTION / CLAIM / BODY / EVIDENCE / CAPTION / SOURCE / LABEL / STATUS / NUMERIC / TECHNICAL / CODE-ID`.

For physical or large-format outputs, consider reading layers where applicable:

- `FAR` — dominant object / headline / major section;
- `MID` — claim / diagram / annotation;
- `NEAR` — body / caption / evidence;
- `DETAIL` — source / dimension / technical metadata.

Bilingual design requires more than semantic translation parity. Where applicable check:

- semantic parity;
- terminology parity;
- hierarchy parity;
- reading-weight parity;
- line-break quality;
- reference parity;
- numbers / units / dates / versions;
- mixed-script rhythm.

Equal semantic role does not require equal literal line count or equal font size across scripts.

## 19｜Editorial / Publication and Presentation Separation

### Editorial / Publication

Owns long-form reading systems such as report, book, catalog, manual and long-form case study.

Typical control objects:

`content inventory → flat plan → page/spread role → master/grid options → type/image/caption/folio → pacing → production conditions → full-sequence readback`.

### Presentation / Projection

Owns time-bounded or audience-specific communication such as live review, pitch, jury, roadshow or decision deck.

For material presentation pages use, where helpful:

`Audience Question → Primary Claim / Decision → Primary Proof → Visual Relation → Spoken Context → Next Narrative Step`.

`REPORT / DOCUMENT ≠ LIVE PRESENTATION`.

Presentation compression may route depth to support/appendix but must preserve no-loss traceability.

## 20｜Design Resolution Scale

Review design at the scales relevant to the medium:

- `R0 SYSTEM`;
- `R1 WHOLE`;
- `R2 ZONE / CHAPTER / SUBSYSTEM`;
- `R3 ELEMENT`;
- `R4 COMPONENT`;
- `R5 DETAIL`;
- `R6 MICRO / FINISH`.

Examples:

- architecture: campus → building → room → wall/opening → component → joint → finish;
- editorial: publication → spread → page → module → element → typographic/graphic detail;
- product: family → product → assembly → part → interface → surface → finish.

A strong R1 hero does not compensate for failed R3–R6 resolution where those scales are in claim.

## 21｜Design Maturity States

Design maturity is separate from Project Status, Evidence Code, professional stage and artifact review state.

Use when helpful. `DQ*` is reserved inside this specification as a **Design Quality maturity prefix only**; it is not a Project Axis, Knowledge level, Delivery Priority, Evidence Code, professional stage, Gate family or Case identifier.

States:

- `DQ0 UNDEFINED` — no meaningful design position yet;
- `DQ1 DIRECTION` — intent / direction exists but is weakly instantiated;
- `DQ2 COHERENT CANDIDATE` — core relations are visible and internally coherent;
- `DQ3 DEVELOPED SYSTEM` — design survives multiple elements/states/scales with detail development;
- `DQ4 DISTINCTIVE RESOLVED SYSTEM` — coherent, crafted, project-specific and professionally reviewable at intended scope;
- `DQ5 PROVEN CROSS-CONTEXT LANGUAGE` — repeated real application/readback demonstrates the language can adapt without losing identity.

Rules:

- DQ maturity is not a statutory, field, engineering or evidence level;
- machines may validate presence/consistency of declared maturity fields but may not auto-award DQ3–DQ5;
- DQ4/DQ5 require independent design judgment;
- a project may remain `CURRENT` while design maturity is intentionally lower if Current Authority and claim ceiling say so.

## 22｜Design Review Lens

When Design Quality is material, review applicable questions from the following lens. Do not average into a total score.

### DQR-01 Concept-to-form

Does the visible/spatial/interactive form actually embody the stated concept?

### DQR-02 Visual / Spatial Ownership

Is there deliberate hierarchy and ownership, or only equally weighted modules/elements?

### DQR-03 Composition Strength

Do relations of dominance, balance, tension, void, rhythm and sequence produce a convincing whole?

### DQR-04 Proportion / Scale

Are macro, meso and micro proportions intentional and contextually appropriate?

### DQR-05 Figure–Ground / Mass–Void

Is the primary perceptual structure legible and controlled?

### DQR-06 Experience Sequence

Does temporal/spatial/user sequence support the stated experience?

### DQR-07 Human Relation

Does body, behavior, use, control or reading remain credible?

### DQR-08 Sensory Quality

Are light/material/sound/touch/etc. aligned with the design intent where relevant?

### DQR-09 Color Character

Does color operate as a coherent semantic and aesthetic system rather than decoration?

### DQR-10 Typography Character

Does type simultaneously support role, readability, identity and medium?

### DQR-11 Image / Camera Direction

Does image selection/framing/crop/light/sequence carry the intended design relation?

### DQR-12 Material / Detail / Craft

Do edges, joints, transitions and finishes sustain the project language at close range?

### DQR-13 Signature / Memorability

Is there a justified reason the work is recognizably this project rather than a generic template?

### DQR-14 Variation Control

Can the design vary without either flattening into sameness or losing identity?

### DQR-15 Cross-media / Cross-state Translation

Does the same design logic survive different media, responsive states, operational conditions or scales where required?

### DQR-16 Genericity / Cliché Attack

Attack with at least these questions when relevant:

1. If project name, logo and project-specific imagery are removed, is meaningful project-specific design logic still visible?
2. Is distinctiveness carried only by font/color choice?
3. If decorative effects are removed, does the composition/form still work?
4. Is the design relying on a common template pattern because no stronger project-specific relation was resolved?
5. Is a cultural/style reference being imitated rather than transformed through project logic?

### DQR-17 Coherence

Do intent, concept, function, form, material, detail, experience and expression support rather than undermine each other?

### DQR-18 Final Craft

At the intended final viewing/use condition, are spacing, alignment, edge, crop, caption, transition, line, material, motion and other last-mile relations professionally resolved?

## 23｜Comparison-First Design Development

When a material design question is genuinely open, use comparison as a selection mechanism.

Default logic:

`Question → design hypothesis → materially distinct candidate A/B/... → fixed comparison condition → whole-frame / relevant-scale readback → attack → select / reject / branch → develop`.

Rules:

- changing only font size, spacing, color, corner radius, shadow or another micro-parameter does not create a materially different composition when the structural question remains unchanged;
- comparison count is not a quality metric;
- a single option is acceptable when authority/technical conditions have already removed the meaningful alternative space;
- keep rejected alternatives when their failure materially improves future reasoning;
- if no candidate improves the named root cause, retain `REVISE` and reopen the design hypothesis.

---

## 24｜Root Cause and Convergence

After an actual Design `REVISE`, identify the dominant root cause before producing another version.

Possible root-cause layers include:

- design question / intent;
- concept;
- content / evidence;
- information architecture;
- composition architecture;
- spatial relation;
- asset/image quality;
- design language;
- typography;
- material/detail;
- interaction/state;
- professional interface;
- technical constraint;
- execution/tool limitation.

If the same dominant root cause survives two consecutive revisions, stop micro-tuning and reclassify the problem layer before another candidate.

`ITERATION COUNT ≠ CONVERGENCE`.

---

## 25｜Hard FAIL / Mandatory REVISE Conditions

The following cannot be averaged away by attractive styling, process completeness or technical artifact quality:

- concept exists only as explanatory prose with no visible/operational consequence;
- a project-specific design-quality claim relies only on generic template structure;
- primary hierarchy is unresolved and multiple elements compete equally without intent;
- critical content/evidence is visually suppressed to strengthen the narrative;
- style reference is copied as surface identity rather than translated through project logic;
- project-specific Design DNA is contradicted by the promoted artifact without a recorded exception/revision;
- signature move is repeated mechanically until it overrides function/clarity;
- local polish masks weak whole-system composition;
- strong hero state is promoted while required supporting states/pages/spaces collapse;
- detail resolution contradicts function, construction, accessibility, interaction or professional truth;
- a responsive/adaptive state loses required hierarchy or identity;
- a technical repair materially damages the design intent and no redesign/readback follows;
- decorative effect is presented as evidence;
- a design claim exceeds the actual prototype/readback/evidence ceiling;
- current artifact has not been reviewed at its intended size/state/medium;
- old Design Review receipt is reused after a material change to content, geometry, composition, Design DNA, interface or native asset.

---

## 26｜Reopen / Change Propagation

Material changes must reopen the affected design scope.

Typical triggers include:

- design question / brief / audience change;
- material program/use/operational change;
- major geometry / composition / hierarchy change;
- new or replaced primary asset;
- Design DNA or brand authority change;
- change to signature move or formal grammar;
- change to material / color / typography that alters identity or readability;
- major interface/technical repair;
- new contradictory evidence;
- change in target medium / size / viewport / production condition;
- major language/localization expansion;
- failed prototype/usability/field/readback evidence;
- new lifecycle/maintenance/aging condition;
- change to a controlled variable from another professional domain.

The previous receipt becomes `STALE_AT_AFFECTED_SCOPE` until re-readback and review.

## 28｜Machine-verifiable vs Human Design Judgment

Machines may validate, as applicable:

- required fields exist;
- IDs and refs resolve;
- stale state is coherent;
- required readback conditions are declared;
- comparison artifacts exist when claimed;
- Design DNA reference/version is current;
- no invalid `KEEP + stale=true` combination;
- required independent review record exists;
- promoted artifact references actual native/derived identities;
- declared language/content/figure/state fields are structurally complete;
- prohibited authority-state combinations are absent.

Machines must not auto-certify:

- beauty;
- elegance;
- originality;
- professional aesthetic quality;
- cultural appropriateness;
- coherence;
- memorability;
- craft;
- design maturity DQ3–DQ5;
- Design KEEP.

Automated measurements may generate diagnostic signals only.

## 29｜Promotion Rule

Design-quality promotion is eligible only when:

1. Current Authority and Design Intelligence Packet remain valid;
2. all triggered professional process dependencies are current at the promoted claim ceiling;
3. the current design artifact/native source has been actually read back at the relevant size/state/medium;
4. applicable design-development dimensions have been resolved to the level required by the claim;
5. material Design DNA / Design Language decisions are current where those claims are made;
6. no hard Design FAIL remains in promoted scope;
7. Truth and Performance blockers do not contradict the promoted result;
8. applicable Cross-Disciplinary Integration interfaces remain current;
9. Artifact Review and specialized technical/evidence gates close as required;
10. an independent design reviewer permits the intended `KEEP` / promotion level;
11. the current `DESIGN_QUALITY_DEVELOPMENT_RECEIPT` is not stale;
12. persistence/release gates pass where triggered.

Design KEEP remains a professional judgment, not a machine state inferred from checklist completion.

## 30｜Professional-domain binding

Each Professional Domain Process must map its authentic stages to the shared Design Development Kernel rather than copy a universal stage sequence.

The shared reusable envelope is `professional-domain-process-contract-v1.0.md`. It defines the minimum Definition / Trigger / Knowledge / Authentic Stage / Interface / Execution / Assurance / Change / Completion contract without prescribing one universal professional sequence. Project and stage execution instances remain runtime/project objects rather than new Knowledge levels.

Minimum stage binding should identify:

`professional question / required design dimensions / knowledge inputs + operational eligibility / native outputs / interfaces / design-language consequences / human-experience consequences / technical consequences / required readback / review owner / reopen triggers`.

Knowledge inputs must remain canonical refs. A stage may discover many candidate objects, but consequential decision use should carry the applicable `OE1 / OE2 / OE3` state, claim ceiling, applicability/freshness condition and `does_not_prove` boundary from the Knowledge Operational Mount contract.

Example Architecture mapping:

- ADD-05 Zoning Alternatives → DD-02 Concept, DD-04 Form, DD-12 Coherence;
- ADD-06 Flow Systems → DD-03 Experience, DD-05 Human Relation, DD-12 Integration;
- ADD-07 Circulation → DD-03, DD-04, DD-05, DD-08;
- ADD-10 System Fit-back → DD-07 Design Language, DD-08 Detail, DD-12 Integration;
- ADD-11 Environmental Behavior → DD-06 Sensory, DD-07 Light/Material Language;
- ADD-13 Room Use → DD-05 Human Relation, DD-06 Sensory, DD-08 Detail;
- ADD-17 Independent Plan Review → applicable DD-01…DD-12 whole-system reread.

Equivalent mappings should be built for Interior, Landscape, Structural, MEP, Lighting, Product, HCD, Service, Systems Engineering and other triggered domains using their real professional semantics.

## 31｜Skill, Tool and Native Output Binding

Professional Design Requirement determines what must be resolved. Skill/Capability Resolution determines who can execute it. Tool selection determines the runtime used to make it.

Canonical relation:

`Professional Stage → Professional Question / Decision Object → Knowledge Inputs → Operational Knowledge Mount → Required Capability Roles → Current Execution Owners / Skills → Native Outputs → Actual Readback → Independent Review → Stage Closure`.

Tool / Adapter, DD responsibility, interface and claim-scope bindings remain subordinate to this professional-stage spine and must not be promoted into parallel top-level stage steps.

Rules:

- Professional Stage ≠ Skill;
- Skill ≠ Tool;
- Tool ≠ METHOD;
- Tool availability ≠ Tool callability;
- Tool execution ≠ Artifact quality;
- Artifact quality ≠ Design KEEP.

No software product is a default design method.

## 34｜Canonical execution summary

The shared OLEANDER design-development relation is:

`Authority → Design Question → Knowledge / Evidence → Intent → Concept / Generative Principle → Experience / Human / Formal Development → Design Language / Style → Prototype / Compare → Detail / Craft → Adaptation / Meaning / Coherence → Native Execution → Multi-scale / Multi-state Actual Readback → Independent Design Review → Truth + Performance + Integration + Artifact Gates → KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD → Promotion / G9`.

The governing principle is:

> **OLEANDER must not treat correct process, correct evidence, consistent components, attractive styling or technical completion as substitutes for developed design. A promotable design must demonstrate intentional relations, coherent form/system behavior, credible human and contextual response, resolved detail at the claimed scale, appropriate adaptation, and independent design judgment at the actual artifact or experience state.**

## 35｜Implementation acceptance test

This specification is considered correctly implemented in a project only when the project can answer, at the applicable claim level:

1. **Intent** — what is the design trying to change, for whom, and why?
2. **Concept** — what generative relation actually shapes the design?
3. **Experience** — how does the design unfold through use, time, movement or reading?
4. **Form** — what controls hierarchy, composition, scale, proportion, figure-ground or mass-void?
5. **Human relation** — how does body, behavior, cognition or use materially affect the result?
6. **Sensory relation** — which sensory carriers are intentional and consequential?
7. **Design language** — which relations make the project recognizable without reducing it to a style recipe?
8. **Craft** — do details, edges, transitions and micro-relations sustain the larger intent?
9. **Experiment** — what was learned by making, comparing or prototyping rather than only by describing?
10. **Adaptation** — what remains invariant and what legitimately changes across states/scales/media?
11. **Meaning / memory** — what should be understood, associated with or remembered, and through which carrier?
12. **Coherence** — do intent, function, form, material, detail, experience and expression reinforce one another?
13. **Truth** — which claims are factual, inferred, assumed, predicted, proposed or verified?
14. **Performance** — what actually works at the current professional/technical claim ceiling and what remains open?
15. **Readback** — has the current artifact/experience been inspected at its real target condition rather than inferred from source intent?
16. **Decision** — what is KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD, why, and what exact change reopens the decision?

If these questions cannot be answered for the dimensions materially claimed by the project, the work may remain a useful candidate or exploration, but it is not Design Quality & Design Development PASS for that scope.
