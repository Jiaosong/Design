## 1｜Why this specification exists

OLEANDER already separates Knowledge, Project, Design Review, Artifact Review, technical gates, evidence and promotion. A further design-development specification is required because a project can still appear complete while remaining aesthetically generic, formally unresolved, experientially weak, over-dependent on presentation polish, under-developed at detail scale, or incoherent across media and disciplines.

Typical false positives include:

- a correct brief with no strong design proposition;
- a coherent grid with no visual ownership;
- a consistent component system with no authorship or distinctiveness;
- a moodboard with no generative design rules;
- an attractive render whose spatial/formal system is weak;
- a strong hero with generic supporting pages;
- polished typography masking weak content hierarchy;
- a concept explained in prose but not visible in form;
- a technically solved detail that destroys the project design language;
- a strong single state that collapses under responsive, operational, seasonal or lifecycle variation;
- a visual identity reduced to color and font tokens;
- a culturally themed design that copies motifs without translating underlying relations;
- a process receipt used as proof that the resulting design is professionally strong.

Therefore OLEANDER treats design quality as an independent, developed state that must be constructed, compared, read back and judged.

Canonical invariants:

`CORRECT CONTENT ≠ STRONG DESIGN`
`TECHNICAL PASS ≠ DESIGN QUALITY`
`CONSISTENCY ≠ DISTINCTIVENESS`
`STYLE ≠ DESIGN LANGUAGE`
`MOODBOARD ≠ DESIGN DIRECTION`
`COMPONENT SYSTEM ≠ VISUAL AUTHORSHIP`
`DETAIL DENSITY ≠ CRAFT`
`RENDER REALISM ≠ FORMAL QUALITY`
`ONE STRONG HERO ≠ SYSTEM QUALITY`
`PROCESS PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`

`Authority → Knowledge Resolution → Design Intelligence Packet → this Design Quality & Design Development Specification → triggered Professional Domain Process → Skill / Capability Resolution → Tool / Adapter → Native Artifact → Design Readback → Independent Design Review → R-D Design verdict`.

In parallel, owner-native Professional / Integration / Artifact / Technical / Evidence states remain independently visible. The Master Runtime / `R-J` compiles those states with the R-D verdict for Promotion eligibility; authorized human Promotion then precedes any `R-K` G9 return. R-D does not inherit or reissue those other owners' gate verdicts.

- `design-intelligence-routing-and-review-v1.0.md` — question, intent, knowledge routing and review-lens authority;
- `architecture-design-development-process-v1.0.md` — architecture-specific ADD stages;

- Production Asset Persistence — durable production artifact persistence.

No new top-level taxonomy is created by this document.

### 2.1｜Knowledge-input admission boundary

Design development may consume only knowledge that has been resolved through the current Knowledge system at the level required by the task and claim. Existing knowledge pages are not automatically trusted merely because they are `CURRENT`, well written, linked from a project, or already used by another stage.

Keep these states independent:

`CONTENT COMPLETE ≠ KNOWLEDGE INTEGRITY VERIFIED`

`KNOWLEDGE INTEGRITY VERIFIED ≠ OPERATIONALLY ELIGIBLE FOR EVERY CLAIM`

`OPERATIONALLY ELIGIBLE KNOWLEDGE ≠ DQ MATURITY`

`DQ MATURITY ≠ PROFESSIONAL-STAGE COMPLETION`.

The runtime admission owner is `knowledge-integrity-and-operational-mount-v1.0.md`. It mounts the existing canonical corpus without copying knowledge into `DD-*` or professional-stage structures. Professional stages reference canonical knowledge objects through task/claim-scoped operational eligibility, applicability, claim ceiling, freshness and `does_not_prove` boundaries.

Knowledge pollution discovered during design execution must be routed back to the appropriate Knowledge owner. A design team may record and respect the issue, but Design Quality review must not silently repair graph authority, duplicate canonical ownership, provenance, lifecycle or source identity by treating the design artifact as proof.

## 3｜Three independent design-result axes

Every material design result must keep three axes separate.

### A｜Truth Axis

Question: **Are the claims, sources, assumptions and represented states truthful to the declared claim ceiling?**

Typical owners: source/evidence governance, research, standards, field verification, claim ledger.

### B｜Performance Axis

Question: **Does the design work for its required use, system, human, technical and lifecycle conditions?**

Typical owners: professional domain process, specialist engineering, HCD/usability, accessibility, operations, simulation, prototype/test.

### C｜Design Axis

Question: **Is the design intentional, coherent, experientially resolved, formally strong, distinctive, crafted and appropriate to context?**

Typical owners: professional design review, discipline-specific design lens, art direction, visual/spatial/product/interaction design review.

Rules:

- none of the axes may self-certify another;
- one axis may be `PASS` while another remains `REVISE / HOLD`;
- a safety, truth or required-performance blocker cannot be averaged away by Design strength;
- strong compliance or evidence cannot be used to force `DESIGN KEEP`;
- Design Axis uses professional judgment with traceable reasons, not a universal numerical score.

## 4｜Shared Design Development Kernel

The following twelve dimensions form the shared design-making kernel. They are **responsibility dimensions**, not a mandatory checklist with equal weight.

### 4.0｜Local specification boundary and body-title contract

This local governance file owns the **shared execution contract**, not the canonical professional-knowledge body. It may define what a design-development record must contain, how it is titled, how it references knowledge, what readback is required, and when a decision becomes stale. It must not copy full Design Language, Human Factors, Visual Communication, Lighting, Material, Accessibility or other specialist knowledge bodies into `DD-*`.

Canonical reusable content remains in the existing Knowledge Architecture and is referenced through current canonical IDs plus operational eligibility. The governing relation is:

`LOCAL GOVERNANCE CONTRACT → CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE REF → PROJECT / PROFESSIONAL EXECUTION → NATIVE ARTIFACT → READBACK → DESIGN DECISION`.

For any material design-development body, **title and section structure are required**. A long untitled paragraph dump is not an acceptable professional body even when its content is technically correct.

Minimum semantic heading responsibilities, when applicable, are:

1. `Title / Identity` — the actual design question, object, stage or decision being developed;
2. `Definition / Scope` — what this body owns and what it does not own;
3. `Current Condition / Problem` — the project-specific condition that triggered development;
4. `Canonical Knowledge / Evidence Inputs` — referenced owners, operational eligibility, applicability and claim ceiling;
5. `Design Intent / Criteria` — the relation to be produced, protected or changed;
6. `Development / Mechanism / Workflow` — the actual design move, comparison, transformation or professional action;
7. `Native Artifact / Readback` — what was made and how the current state is inspected;
8. `Failure / Counterexample / Open Item` — what failed, remains unresolved or limits the current claim;
9. `Decision / Maturity / Claim Ceiling` — what can be kept, revised, rejected or held at the present state;
10. `Reopen Trigger / Next Development Action` — what future change invalidates the current decision and what must happen next.

These are **semantic heading responsibilities**, not one compulsory visible wording template. Professional domains may rename, combine or reorder headings when their native documentation practice requires it, but the resulting body must remain visibly structured and recoverable. Decorative headings with no substantive body do not satisfy the contract.

The local specification may carry concise definitions, invariants, failure conditions and runtime fields for DD-01…DD-12. Detailed theory, method procedure, source research, evidence synthesis, case analysis and reusable Practice lessons remain with their canonical Knowledge owners.

`BODY STRUCTURE REQUIREMENT ≠ LOCAL KNOWLEDGE DUPLICATION`.

`CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE REF ≠ COPIED CANONICAL BODY`.

#### R-I review consumption boundary

The design-development body record is a locator/index consumed by `R-I Actual Readback & Review` when the body is material to the claim. R-I must reopen the referenced native artifact/readback rather than treating the body prose, title or section coverage as evidence that implementation occurred.

At review time, the applicable body record must still point to the Current native/readback identity used by the review. A stale, missing or mismatched locator is a review-evidence gap and must be routed back to the R-D/R-H owner as applicable; R-I does not silently rewrite the design body or canonical Knowledge source to make the review pass.

`BODY COMPLETE ≠ NATIVE IMPLEMENTATION VERIFIED ≠ ACTUAL READBACK PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

### 4.1｜DD knowledge-mount routing contract

Each triggered DD responsibility resolves knowledge by **decision question**, not by folder name, historical association or object title. The local DD contract records the required owner classes and the task-scoped mount; it does not duplicate the owner body.

Minimum DQ-side record for a consequential mounted input:

```yaml
mount_record_ref:          # R-B owner-issued task/claim mount record
mount_record_revision_or_hash:
knowledge_ref:
use_role: PRIMARY | SUPPORTING | CONDITIONAL | CONTEXT | COUNTEREVIDENCE
eligibility_snapshot: OE1 | OE2 | OE3
claim_ceiling:
applicability:
conditions:
does_not_prove:
freshness_or_revalidation_trigger:
review_basis:
```

`mount_record_ref` points to the R-B owner-issued Operational Mount. The remaining fields are a **non-authoritative reproducibility snapshot** of the boundary consumed by the DQ decision; they do not let R-D reissue or upgrade OE. Before consequential Current reuse, dereference the Current mount record and compare its revision/hash, eligibility, applicability, conditions and claim ceiling. A historical DQ receipt may preserve the snapshot it actually consumed even when that mount later changes.

Only an R-B mount currently resolved to `OE2 / OE3` may support a consequential in-scope design decision, and every `OE2` condition must travel with the decision. `SUPPORT` objects may be valid bounded inputs when task-scoped eligibility permits; lifecycle label alone neither grants nor removes decision authority.

`DQ ELIGIBILITY SNAPSHOT ≠ R-B OE AUTHORITY`.

Default owner routing by DD responsibility:

| DD responsibility | Typical canonical owner classes to resolve when material | Local DD does not own |
|---|---|---|
| `DD-01 Intent` | Current project authority, brief / decision sources, stakeholder or contextual evidence, applicable design-strategy methods | source truth, stakeholder authority, reusable intent theory |
| `DD-02 Concept / Generative Principle` | design-method owners, contextual / cultural / technical sources, precedents / cases, applicable theory | full theory, precedent body, cultural-source research |
| `DD-03 Experience Architecture` | experience / interaction / service / spatial-sequence methods, user/task evidence, relevant Practice | canonical HCD/experience methods and user evidence |
| `DD-04 Form / Composition` | form/composition/perception methods, visual/spatial frameworks, domain-native form-language knowledge | visual/perceptual theory body or professional form method |
| `DD-05 Human Relation` | Human Factors / ergonomics, accessibility, anthropometric/task evidence, observed-use Practice | human-factors standards, accessibility authority, user-performance evidence |
| `DD-06 Sensory Design` | lighting, acoustics, material, haptics and other triggered sensory owners plus performance evidence | specialist performance methods, calculations or certifications |
| `DD-07 Design Language / Style` | Current Design Language / Form Language / Visual Communication owners and project Design DNA authority | canonical grammar/method bodies or reusable visual knowledge |
| `DD-08 Detail / Craft` | construction/detailing, fabrication, surface/material, interaction/editorial production methods and relevant evidence | specialist detailing/fabrication methods and production authority |

The table defines **routing responsibility**, not a fixed object inventory. A concrete project must resolve live canonical IDs and current states at runtime. Do not hardcode a SUPPORT candidate, legacy page or stale method as a permanent DD owner merely because it once supplied useful content.

`OWNER CLASS ROUTING ≠ FIXED KNOWLEDGE INVENTORY`.

### DD-01 Intent

Resolve why the design exists, what change it should make, for whom, and what it explicitly does not seek to solve.

Required as applicable:

- design question;
- desired change;
- desired experience;
- non-goals;
- stakes;
- claim ceiling;
- success/failure condition.

Hard failure: visual/formal activity proceeds without a resolvable design question or consequential intent.

### DD-02 Concept / Generative Principle

Concept must generate or constrain design, not merely explain it after the fact.

Record:

- conceptual source;
- abstraction step;
- generative principle;
- transformation rule;
- invariants;
- controlled variables;
- rejected literal/metaphoric shortcuts;
- visible consequence in form/system/behavior.

`CONCEPT STORY ≠ GENERATIVE DESIGN RULE`.

### DD-03 Experience Architecture

Resolve the experience through time, space, use or interaction rather than as a static object only.

Possible sequence carriers include:

`approach → entry → orientation → engagement → intensification → pause → transition → return / exit → memory`.

Record experience moments only when applicable to the medium and task.

### DD-04 Form / Composition

Resolve primary formal relations such as:

- hierarchy and dominant field;
- figure / ground;
- scale and proportion;
- balance and tension;
- axis / alignment;
- cluster / isolation;
- overlap / crop / bleed / edge pressure;
- negative space;
- repetition / rupture;
- sequence / rhythm;
- mass / void / geometry for spatial work.

The grid, component library or CAD/BIM framework may support composition but may not become the design concept by default.

### DD-05 Human Relation

Where people materially use, inhabit, operate, read or encounter the design, resolve applicable relations such as:

- body / posture / reach;
- movement;
- gaze and field of view;
- proximity / territory / privacy;
- effort;
- affordance;
- control / feedback;
- social relation;
- accessibility / inclusion interfaces;
- comfort and cognitive demand.

A human silhouette, persona or accessibility label is not evidence that human relation has been designed.

### DD-06 Sensory Design

Resolve relevant sensory carriers rather than treating experience as visual-only.

Possible carriers:

- light and shadow;
- color;
- texture / touch;
- weight / resistance;
- temperature;
- sound / acoustics;
- air / movement;
- material reflectivity / transparency;
- smell or other project-relevant sensory conditions.

Not every project needs every carrier. Unused carriers should not be fabricated for completeness.

### DD-07 Design Language / Style

Resolve the reusable formal grammar that produces recognizable project character.

Design Language may include:

- geometry grammar;
- proportion grammar;
- edge / termination grammar;
- surface / depth grammar;
- composition grammar;
- color character;
- typography character;
- image language;
- material language;
- light language;
- motion character;
- spatial grammar;
- detail / joint language;
- signature moves;
- variation envelope;
- forbidden moves.

`STYLE` is the perceived result; `DESIGN LANGUAGE` is the rule system capable of producing and adapting that result. `ART DIRECTION` is the project-specific orchestration of image, framing, material, light, color, typography, sequence and expressive intensity that makes the language perceptible in a given medium or experience. Art Direction may vary by project and medium but must remain traceable to the Current Design DNA when one exists.

### DD-08 Detail / Craft

Resolve close-range and transition conditions appropriate to the design medium.

Review as applicable:

- edge;
- joint;
- seam;
- gap;
- termination;
- opening;
- transition;
- fastening / assembly expression;
- tolerance;
- touch point;
- finish;
- micro hierarchy;
- micro interaction;
- caption/legend/line/spacing finish;
- construction or production consequence.

The detail should not contradict the project-level concept unless the contradiction is an explicit design decision.

### DD-09 Prototype / Design Experiment

Prototypes may serve different roles and must be typed accordingly:

- `DISCOVERY`;
- `COMPARISON`;
- `VALIDATION`;
- `COMMUNICATION`;
- `PRODUCTION`.

For design experiments record:

`question → variable → fixed conditions → candidates → observation → unexpected behavior → design consequence → validation ceiling`.

`PROTOTYPE EXISTS ≠ DESIGN VALIDATED`.

### DD-10 Adaptation / Variation

Test whether the design survives legitimate variation.

Possible stress states:

- large / small;
- sparse / dense;
- desktop / mobile;
- day / night;
- individual / crowd;
- normal / degraded / closed;
- seasonal / weather state;
- new / aged;
- concept / production;
- different content lengths / languages;
- different room/use states.

Record invariants, variables, ranges, context rules, exceptions and failure boundaries.

### DD-11 Meaning / Memory / Cultural Reading

Where relevant, define what should be recognized, associated, remembered or culturally legible.

Record:

- intended meaning;
- cultural/context basis;
- design carrier;
- literal vs abstract translation;
- possible conflicting reading;
- misuse/cliché risk;
- memory carrier;
- repetition rule;
- transfer boundary.

`CULTURAL REFERENCE ≠ LICENSE TO COPY MOTIF`.

### DD-12 Integration / Coherence

Review whether the project operates as one design rather than a set of individually polished parts.

Check relations among:

`Intent ↔ Concept ↔ Form ↔ Function ↔ Human Use ↔ Material ↔ Detail ↔ Experience ↔ Expression ↔ Technical System`.

`LOCAL QUALITY ≠ SYSTEM COHERENCE`.

### 4.3｜DD operating criteria, evidence floor and reopen threshold

`DD-01 ... DD-12` are not labels to attach after the design is produced. For every triggered dimension, the project must be able to identify the **design relation being changed**, the evidence used to judge that relation, the minimum condition for retaining it, the downstream consumer that depends on it, and the condition that would reopen it.

The following table defines the minimum professional substance expected from each shared DD responsibility. Domain processes may add stronger criteria; they must not replace these relations with reporting mechanics.

| DD responsibility | Minimum design substance | Evidence / readback floor | KEEP / closure threshold | Typical handoff / reopen trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DD-01 Intent` | explicit user/context change, competing priorities, non-goals and trade-off position | current brief/authority + bounded user/context evidence + visible consequence in the design | the intended change can be traced to at least one material design decision and one explicit failure condition; slogans alone fail | hand off intent/criteria to every consuming domain; reopen on brief, user, authority, claim-ceiling or priority change |
| `DD-02 Concept` | generative relation that actually constrains form/system/behavior and excludes alternatives | materially different option or transformation evidence showing the rule produces observable consequences | retained design still follows the generative relation under the states/scales in claim; post-rationalizing an already chosen form fails | hand off invariants/controlled variables to Design DNA and domain owners; reopen when a retained move requires a contradictory rule or exception proliferation |
| `DD-03 Experience` | sequence, transition, orientation, action, pause, recovery/return and state change as applicable | walkthrough, storyboard, route/time-state model, interactive prototype or equivalent actual sequence readback | critical experience moments connect without hidden discontinuity and the intended experiential consequence survives actual use/state readback | hand off sequence requirements to spatial, interaction, lighting, sound, operations and wayfinding owners; reopen on route/state/timing/use change |
| `DD-04 Form / Composition` | dominance, mass/void, alignment, proportion, hierarchy, rhythm and spatial/visual relation at relevant scales | plan/section/elevation/view/frame readback at whole/working/detail scales, with labels/effects reduced where useful | the intended relation remains legible without explanatory rescue and does not contradict function, evidence, accessibility or technical constraints | hand off governing geometry/proportion to native production owners; reopen on major geometry, crop, viewport, component, section or system-zone change |
| `DD-05 Human Relation` | body/task/reach/movement/gaze/privacy/control/social/access relation using real or bounded human-use assumptions | dimensioned geometry, ergonomic/anthropometric source where needed, task simulation, furnished/equipped plan, prototype or observed use | every in-claim critical task can be performed with credible clearance, access, control and cognitive/physical demand at the declared ceiling | hand off human-use envelopes to Architecture/Interior/HCD/MEP/Accessibility as applicable; reopen on user group, equipment, posture, route, interface or accessibility requirement change |
| `DD-06 Sensory` | intentional light, sound, thermal, tactile, air, material, color or other relevant sensory condition and its source/path/receiver relation | material sample/mockup, acoustic/light/environmental analysis, calibrated render only where appropriate, prototype or in-use sensory readback | claimed sensory quality is supported by a controllable design variable and does not depend on a presentation effect absent from the real medium | hand off target conditions and tolerances to material/lighting/acoustic/MEP/envelope owners; reopen on material, source, geometry, environment, operation or control change |
| `DD-07 Design Language / Style` | repeatable grammar with explicit invariants, permitted variation, signature roles, exceptions and forbidden moves | cross-artifact/state readback demonstrating the grammar in more than one relevant application | identity survives legitimate variation without relying on logo/name or one hero artifact, and exceptions remain bounded | hand off Current Design DNA rules to consuming artifacts/domains; reopen on rule replacement, uncontrolled exception growth or medium/state failure |
| `DD-08 Detail / Craft` | edge/joint/transition/termination/assembly/tolerance/finish behavior that preserves concept, performance and maintenance logic | enlarged native detail, prototype/mockup, fabrication/assembly logic, material sample or close-range readback as applicable | critical transitions have intentional geometry/material/assembly/tolerance consequences and do not hide unresolved production or maintenance failure | hand off critical details and tolerances to production/fabrication/construction owners; reopen on material, supplier, tolerance, assembly, maintenance or adjacent-system change |
| `DD-09 Prototype / Experiment` | a falsifiable design question with controlled variables and an explicit observation method | actual prototype/test result, not only a rendered proposal; failed/unexpected behavior retained | the experiment changes, confirms or rejects a design relation at its declared validation ceiling; “prototype completed” alone never closes the question | hand off observed consequence to the owning design/professional relation; reopen when context/variable changes outside tested envelope or contradictory evidence appears |
| `DD-10 Adaptation / Variation` | invariant plus variable range, context-selection rule, coupled variables, degraded/edge states and exception boundary | boundary-state and adverse-state readback, not only nominal-state success | the design remains identifiable, usable and professionally coherent at required extremes; hidden new rules or interface breakage fail the envelope | hand off allowed ranges and failure boundaries to implementation owners; reopen when range, context, lifecycle state, content, occupancy or responsive/operational condition expands |
| `DD-11 Meaning / Memory / Cultural Reading` | intended reading, contextual basis, abstraction/translation method, conflicting readings, cliché/misuse risk and memory carrier | source/context evidence + independent or user/context readback appropriate to the claim; visual resemblance alone is insufficient | the intended relation is legible without false authorship, tokenism or literal copying and competing readings remain understood/bounded | hand off protected meaning/carriers to art direction, content, spatial/material or interaction owners; reopen on audience/context/source/rights/interpretive contradiction change |
| `DD-12 Integration / Coherence` | explicit relation among intent, function, human use, form, material, technical systems, interfaces and design language | cross-domain/native readback of the actual combined state, including strongest contradiction | no material in-claim subsystem succeeds by breaking another required relation; remaining tensions are deliberate, bounded and owned | hand off coherent controlled variables to Integration/Master review; reopen on any material upstream change whose consequence propagates across two or more design relations |

For quantitative work, `threshold` means a **current project/professional criterion with source, unit, applicability and tolerance**, not a generic number invented by this shared specification. For qualitative work, the threshold must still be falsifiable enough to distinguish `KEEP` from `REVISE`; terms such as “good”, “premium”, “comfortable”, “dynamic” or “resolved” without observable consequences are not sufficient.

Every triggered dimension should therefore be reviewable through:

`criterion / intended relation → native or experiential carrier → actual readback → observed success + strongest contradiction → bounded disposition → handoff owner → reopen trigger`.

`DD COVERAGE ≠ DD SUBSTANCE`.

`CRITERION WITHOUT READBACK ≠ DESIGN EVIDENCE`.

`READBACK WITHOUT A RETENTION / FAILURE THRESHOLD ≠ DESIGN DECISION`.

## 6｜Design Direction & Aesthetic Position

Design direction must translate qualitative intent into visible or experiential consequences.

Weak form:

`calm / premium / natural / modern / poetic`.

Required form:

`desired quality → formal consequence → allowed range → exception → failure sign`.

Example:

`quiet → low competing salience + longer pauses + restrained contrast → hero moments may intensify → fail when every module competes equally`.

Useful aesthetic tensions may include, when relevant:

- controlled ↔ expressive;
- minimal ↔ dense;
- quiet ↔ dramatic;
- precise ↔ organic;
- formal ↔ informal;
- tactile ↔ immaterial;
- static ↔ dynamic;
- monumental ↔ intimate;
- polished ↔ raw;
- consistent ↔ varied;
- familiar ↔ novel;
- contextual ↔ iconic.

For each material tension record:

`chosen position / reason / design consequence / accepted cost / allowed variation / reopen condition`.

Do not reduce these to automated 1–10 aesthetic scores.

## 7｜Design Language / Formal Grammar Contract

Where a repeated design language is material, resolve the minimum sufficient grammar rather than collecting style attributes.

### 7.1 Geometry grammar

Examples of variables: rectilinear/curved, orthogonal/oblique, continuous/fragmented, regular/irregular, sharp/soft, monolithic/articulated.

For each governing rule record `scope / reason / invariant / variable / exception / failure condition`.

### 7.2 Proportion grammar

Record recurring proportion behavior such as slender/wide, vertical/horizontal, compressed/open, deep/flat, large-scale jump vs close scale family.

### 7.3 Edge and termination grammar

Record rules for hard edge, radius, bevel, cut, fold, frame, open edge, bleed, reveal, joint and terminal conditions.

### 7.4 Surface and depth grammar

Record whether the project depends on flat fields, shallow layering, transparency, reflection, texture, deep spatial hierarchy, overlap, shadow or other depth relations.

### 7.5 Composition grammar

Record only project-relevant relations such as dominance, balance, tension, axis, field, cluster, isolation, overlap, crop, bleed, edge pressure, void, rhythm, repetition, rupture and scale jump.

### 7.6 Chromatic character

Separate semantic color roles from aesthetic color character. When material, record:

- temperature;
- saturation range;
- contrast range;
- lightness range;
- neutral bias;
- accent frequency;
- color field scale;
- color adjacency;
- material/image color relationship.

### 7.7 Typographic character

In addition to functional type roles, record project-specific character when material:

- voice;
- scale contrast;
- weight behavior;
- width/density;
- case behavior;
- spacing character;
- alignment;
- editorial behavior;
- numeric/technical treatment;
- CJK/Latin relationship.

### 7.8 Image language

Record applicable camera/art-direction relations:

- camera distance / height;
- viewpoint behavior;
- subject framing;
- people presence/absence;
- movement;
- depth;
- time / weather / light;
- contrast / focus / grain;
- crop behavior;
- documentary ↔ staged;
- literal ↔ abstract;
- whole ↔ detail sequence.

### 7.9 Material / sensory language

Record relevant relations among mass, weight, hardness, softness, roughness, reflectivity, transparency, aging, weathering, sound, touch, joint and light response.

### 7.10 Light language

Record relevant directionality, softness, contrast, shadow depth, ambient condition, highlight/reflection behavior, temperature relation and temporal change.

### 7.11 Motion character

Separate motion semantics from motion character. When material, record tempo, weight, inertia, continuity, elasticity, abruptness, delay, rhythm, choreography and frequency.

## 8｜Visual Ownership and Dominant Relation

For every material frame, page, board, screen, scene, view or comparable composition, resolve the intended primary visual owner where useful.

Candidate owner types include:

`IMAGE / FORM / TYPE / DATA / SPACE / MATERIAL / DIAGRAM / VOID / MOTION`.

The owner is not a mandatory metadata field for every artifact. It is a diagnostic when hierarchy is weak or multiple modules compete equally.

### 8.1｜What ownership means

Visual ownership answers a bounded perceptual question: **what relation should control the first meaningful read of this state, and which elements must remain subordinate so that the intended claim is not diluted?** It is not the same as visual size, decorative prominence, brand style, evidence authority or content importance.

The minimum reasoning chain is:

`design question / communication role → intended first-read relation → candidate owner → supporting hierarchy → actual readback → observed competition / clarity → KEEP / REVISE`.

Inputs may include the current Design Question, Project Design DNA, content/evidence role, target medium and reading condition, required figure/text relations, accessibility constraints and any professional/native geometry that may not be visually distorted for presentation convenience.

### 8.2｜Owner selection and support hierarchy

Choose the owner from the relation that must survive a fast first read, not from the element that is easiest to enlarge. Then identify which elements are:

- **OWNER** — carries the primary perceptual/design relation;
- **CO-DEPENDENT** — must remain perceptually linked to the owner for the claim to remain truthful;
- **SUPPORTING** — explains, qualifies or locates the owner without competing with it;
- **BACKGROUND / INFRASTRUCTURE** — necessary for navigation, continuity or production but not intended to become the first read;
- **CONSTRAINT** — authoritative geometry, evidence, accessibility, safety or other information that may cap the owner treatment even when it reduces visual drama.

More than one strong element is allowed when the intended relation is explicitly comparative, dialogic or dual-focus. In that case the **relationship between them** becomes the owner. Do not force a single-object hierarchy when the design question is genuinely about tension, comparison, before/after, cause/effect or two-part spatial framing.

### 8.3｜Readback evidence

Where ownership is material, review the actual target artifact or a faithful target-state readback using the tests that fit the medium, such as:

- first-read / five-second description;
- thumbnail or distance read;
- grayscale or reduced-detail read when color/decorative effects may be carrying false hierarchy;
- label-off or copy-reduction test when explanatory text may be rescuing weak form;
- page-to-page / state-to-state sequence read when ownership intentionally changes;
- plan/section/view cross-read where a spatial owner must remain coherent across representations;
- accessibility or information-priority check where perceptual emphasis could conflict with required readability or safety information.

The evidence should state **what was observed**, not merely that the producer intended one owner. Eye-tracking, biometric or quantitative saliency evidence is optional and may support the review where genuinely available; it is not a universal requirement and does not replace professional judgment.

### 8.4｜Failure and reopen conditions

Hard warning conditions:

- repeated `headline + equal cards + small image` without project-specific reason;
- every page has equal visual mass;
- visual hierarchy is achieved only by changing font size;
- decorative effects create the only first-read signal;
- supporting evidence dominates the primary design claim;
- the designated owner is weak while secondary modules are louder.

Also reopen ownership when a material crop, viewport, language expansion, content-density change, interaction state, image substitution, geometry revision or accessibility requirement changes the first-read relation. A local export or responsive repair may remain local only when actual readback shows the owner/support hierarchy and required evidence relations are unchanged.

`DECLARED OWNER ≠ OBSERVED OWNER`.

`VISUAL DOMINANCE ≠ AUTHORITY`.

`ONE OWNER ≠ ONE OBJECT`.

## 9｜Signature, System, Exception and Experiment

Classify the **project design-language role** of each material Current relation as one of:

- `SYSTEM_RULE` — recurring base grammar;
- `SIGNATURE_MOVE` — limited, memorable, high-recognition move;
- `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` — context-specific departure;
- `EXPERIMENT` — unpromoted candidate behavior.

This role classification is project design-language governance, not a lifecycle or Knowledge-state replacement. `SYSTEM_RULE / SIGNATURE_MOVE / LOCAL_EXCEPTION / EXPERIMENT` answer **how the relation is allowed to behave inside the current Project Design DNA**. Currency/supersession is tracked separately through the existing R-A / owner-native Current-Supersession boundary; Knowledge `CURRENT / SUPPORT / PROVENANCE / EXCLUDED` remains a different corpus/retrieval family. A prior design-language relation may therefore be historical/provenance/superseded without `LEGACY / SUPERSEDED` becoming a fifth design-language role.

### 9.1｜Decision criteria

Classify a move by **scope, repeatability, authority and failure consequence**:

| Class | Appropriate when | Minimum evidence before relying on it | What it must not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SYSTEM_RULE` | the relation should recur across the declared system scope | deliberate Project Design DNA decision + successful application/readback across the relevant element/state range | universal reusable knowledge |
| `SIGNATURE_MOVE` | a limited high-recognition relation should remain special | exact intended locations/conditions + readback showing recognizability without system damage | permission to repeat everywhere |
| `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` | a bounded context legitimately overrides the base grammar | explicit reason, boundary and compatibility with adjacent system relations | silent mutation of the base rule |
| `EXPERIMENT` | the relation is being tested and has not been adopted | test question, bounded prototype/native artifact, actual readback and disposition | Current system authority or Promotion evidence |

When a prior relation no longer writes Current project state, retain its predecessor identity, role-at-the-time and provenance through the existing supersession/history mechanism. That historical status does not grant deletion authority and must not leave the predecessor as a parallel Current grammar rule.

### 9.2｜Transition and authority

A recurring pattern does not promote itself from `EXPERIMENT` to `SYSTEM_RULE`. Adoption requires the existing project/design authority to make a bounded Project Design DNA decision after comparison/readback at the relevant claim ceiling. A successful `SIGNATURE_MOVE` may remain a signature indefinitely; recurrence alone is not evidence that its role should change.

When a system rule is replaced, use the existing Current/Supersession boundary: close/demote the predecessor from live Current use, preserve its provenance, update only affected consumers, and re-read the successor where the changed relation is in claim. Do not keep old and new grammar rules co-equal merely because both still exist in files or prior artifacts.

Rules:

- a successful signature move must not automatically become a system rule;
- repeating a signature everywhere may reduce it to a gimmick;
- a local exception must not silently mutate the global Design DNA;
- an observed recurring implementation pattern is not `SYSTEM_RULE` without authority or deliberate promotion;
- legacy patterns remain provenance, not parallel Current design language.

Failure / reopen conditions include uncontrolled exception growth, an experiment leaking into production as if approved, a signature repeated until it destroys hierarchy, a system rule that fails materially at required scale/state, or a successor Design DNA decision that changes the permitted role of an existing move.

`REPEATED USE ≠ SYSTEM AUTHORITY`.

`PROJECT SYSTEM RULE ≠ REUSABLE KNOWLEDGE RULE`.

## 10｜Variation Envelope

Every material system rule should identify, where applicable:

```yaml
invariant:
variable:
allowed_range:
context_rule:
exception:
failure_boundary:
```

The design system should preserve identity without forcing uniformity.

### 10.1｜Operational meaning

The Variation Envelope defines the region in which a design relation may adapt **without becoming a different relation or breaking a required performance/experience/identity condition**. It is therefore a constraint on controlled change, not a library of decorative variants.

For each material rule, resolve where applicable:

- the invariant relation that must survive;
- the variable(s) intentionally allowed to change;
- allowed range or discrete alternatives, including units when quantitative;
- context rule that selects or modulates the variable;
- explicit exceptions and their owner;
- coupled variables that must change together;
- failure boundary and observable failure signal;
- the native artifact/state in which the variation is implemented;
- the readback conditions that test the envelope;
- the authority/reopen trigger when the allowed range itself changes.

If the range is not yet known, record it as an assumption or experiment. Do not fabricate a precise range merely to make the system appear parametric.

Example:

`invariant = one dominant field`
`variable = owner may be IMAGE / TYPE / DIAGRAM`
`failure_boundary = two or more equal dominant fields make the reading hierarchy ambiguous`.

### 10.2｜Envelope validation

Test the states most likely to expose collapse rather than only the nominal middle state. Depending on the design, this may include minimum/maximum content, smallest/largest viewport, empty/error/loading state, highest/lowest occupancy, day/night, seasonal condition, material aging, accessibility mode, long/short language, extreme component count, or a spatial/programmatic edge case.

Useful validation sequence:

`nominal state → boundary state → adverse/edge state → observed invariant → observed failure/robustness → revise range or rule`.

The envelope fails when a supposedly allowed variation requires a hidden new rule, breaks a coupled interface, changes the design identity beyond the stated claim, makes required information unreadable, or creates a professional/technical contradiction. A failure at one edge does not automatically invalidate all variants; narrow the allowed range or reopen the controlling relation at the smallest truthful scope.

`VARIATION ≠ EXCEPTION`.

`PARAMETRIC POSSIBILITY ≠ DESIGN-PERMITTED RANGE`.

`ONE VALID STATE ≠ ENVELOPE VALIDATED`.

## 11｜Style Intensity and Aesthetic Rhythm

When a project contains several pages, spaces, screens, scenes or states, control expressive intensity rather than making every moment equally loud.

Optional style-intensity states:

- `S0 INFRASTRUCTURAL / NEUTRAL`;
- `S1 RESTRAINED`;
- `S2 CHARACTERISTIC`;
- `S3 EXPRESSIVE`;
- `S4 SIGNATURE`.

These are local project control states, not universal quality levels.

The states describe **relative expressive load inside one project/system**. `S4` is not better than `S1`; a restrained state may be the correct design decision for wayfinding, dense evidence, recovery, maintenance, background circulation or a deliberate pause. Likewise, a signature state that appears everywhere is usually no longer functioning as a signature.

Possible aesthetic sequence:

`quiet → dense → release → focus → rupture → return`.

Spatial equivalent may be:

`compression → threshold → expansion → pause → framed view → release`.

The exact rhythm is project-specific. Do not impose fixed pacing formulas.

### 11.1｜Intensity allocation contract

When rhythm is material, identify:

`sequence / state → intended experiential or communication role → local intensity → dominant carrier → preceding/following contrast → invariant Design DNA relation → failure condition`.

Intensity may be carried by scale, contrast, density, motion, light, material, sound, spatial compression/expansion, image treatment, typography, data density or other domain-native means. Do not equate intensity with saturation, animation, large type or visual noise.

### 11.2｜Readback and failure

Review the whole sequence as well as individual moments. Check whether quiet states remain intentionally resolved, whether transitions have enough contrast to be perceived, whether repeated peaks flatten into noise, and whether low-intensity states still retain project identity and required information.

Reopen rhythm when a sequence is materially reordered, a major state/page/space is added or removed, an interaction mode changes, a lighting/media condition changes, or responsive/operational adaptation causes peaks and pauses to collapse. The appropriate repair may be to lower an over-expressive state rather than increase every weak state.

`STYLE INTENSITY ≠ DESIGN MATURITY`.

`MORE EXPRESSIVE ≠ BETTER`.

`LOCAL PEAK ≠ GLOBAL SYSTEM RULE`.

## 12｜Content, Information and Design Communication Binding

Design development must preserve the distinction among:

### Content Governance

What can be claimed, what evidence supports it, what remains uncertain and what must be retained.

### Information Architecture

What belongs together, hierarchy, order, canonical home, navigation, cross-reference and progressive disclosure.

### Language / Terminology

Wording, naming, technical precision, bilingual equivalence, voice profile and status/action language.

### Visual Communication

Hierarchy, composition, typography, image relation, diagram relation, color, density, reading distance and visual craft.

### Editorial / Publication

Long-form sequence, page/spread role, pacing, body measure, captions, folios, appendix/reference behavior and publication readback.

### Presentation / Projection

Audience, decision context, shown vs spoken, Claim–Proof relation, medium, density and release context.

### Interaction / Temporal Communication

State, action, consequence, feedback, recovery, motion and re-entry.

### Production / Delivery

Native source, editable master, export, delivery conditions, reproducibility and artifact persistence.

Under v2.1 this responsibility is governed by the cross-cutting File & Artifact Management Control Domain rather than by presentation convention. Material design artifacts must preserve `one logical artifact → one Current revision → N justified representations`, with native/editable authority, derivation lineage, current location and readback remaining explicit. `PREVIEW / SCREENSHOT / PACKAGE ≠ NATIVE AUTHORITY`, and `UPLOAD / COMMIT ≠ SYNCED` until the applicable persistence/sync owner completes target readback.

These systems may be triggered independently. A presentation artifact does not automatically require every editorial or interaction rule.

The binding question is not “which communication format are we making?” but:

```text
WHAT DECISION / CLAIM MUST SURVIVE
→ WHO MUST UNDERSTAND / USE IT
→ WHICH AUTHORITY / EVIDENCE BOUNDS IT
→ WHICH INFORMATION RELATIONS MUST REMAIN VISIBLE
→ WHICH CARRIER CAN EXPRESS THEM
→ WHAT ACTUAL READBACK PROVES THE PROJECTION WORKS
```

Each triggered communication system owns a different professional problem:

| System | Primary design problem | Must not silently become |
|---|---|---|
| Content Governance | truth, claim, evidence, uncertainty, retention | copywriting polish |
| Information Architecture | relation, hierarchy, grouping, sequence, retrieval | page layout only |
| Language / Terminology | precise naming, equivalence, action/status language | tone styling only |
| Visual Communication | attention, hierarchy, comparison, visual explanation | decoration |
| Editorial / Publication | sustained reading and sequence across pages/spreads | slide deck logic |
| Presentation / Projection | time-bounded audience decision and proof | shortened report |
| Interaction / Temporal Communication | state/action/feedback/recovery over time | static screen styling |
| Production / Delivery | editable source, export fidelity, reproducibility, persistence | final screenshot/PDF existence |

A material design communication body should therefore preserve four layers even when they are carried by different artifacts:

```text
SOURCE / AUTHORITY LAYER
→ INFORMATION / RELATION LAYER
→ COMMUNICATION / EXPERIENCE LAYER
→ PRODUCTION / READBACK LAYER
```

Failure in a downstream communication layer must not be repaired by changing upstream truth without authority. Conversely, a correct upstream source does not excuse an unreadable, misleading or professionally unfinished projection.

When design communication consumes external files or canonical Knowledge, v2.1 Reader/File handling applies: use the minimum sufficient `LIST / SEARCH / FIND / READ / MATERIALIZE / PARSE_RENDER / READBACK` depth, preserve source/revision identity, and route consequential knowledge use through R-B Operational Mount. A readable search hit, Reader result or materialized copy does not gain Knowledge authority, KI/OE eligibility or mutation permission merely because it is accessible.

`CONTENT CORRECT ≠ INFORMATION STRUCTURED ≠ COMMUNICATION EFFECTIVE ≠ PRODUCTION COMPLETE`.

## 13｜Content Projection / No-loss Contract

When source content is projected into a board, deck, page, web screen, film, diagram or other communication surface, OLEANDER permits:

- reorder;
- summarize;
- layer;
- collapse;
- cross-reference;
- move depth to appendix/support;
- adapt wording to audience and medium without changing truth.

OLEANDER forbids:

- deleting a qualification that materially changes the claim;
- upgrading claim strength;
- removing contradictory evidence in a way that makes the projected claim misleading;
- erasing uncertainty or OPEN/HOLD state;
- changing source meaning;
- converting a proposal into implemented condition;
- converting simulation into field truth;
- using a decorative visual as evidence.

For consequential projection, classify information where useful as:

- `MUST_SHOW`;
- `MAY_COLLAPSE`;
- `MUST_RETAIN`;
- `MUST_COLOCATE`.

Typical `MUST_COLOCATE` pairs:

- claim ↔ critical limitation;
- number ↔ unit;
- requirement ↔ authority;
- figure ↔ legend;
- decision ↔ unresolved blocker;
- status ↔ reason;
- evidence result ↔ `does_not_establish` when omission would mislead.

No-loss is semantic, not geometric. The projected artifact does **not** need to preserve the source document's paragraph order, page count, visual weight or literal wording. It must preserve the relations needed to prevent a materially different reading.

For consequential projection, use this development loop:

```text
authoritative source state
→ identify decision / audience / medium
→ classify MUST_SHOW / MAY_COLLAPSE / MUST_RETAIN / MUST_COLOCATE
→ transform / reorder / layer
→ preserve source locators and claim boundary
→ read back at actual medium / scale / state
→ compare projected reading with source meaning
→ repair or HOLD
```

When compression is material, the projection should be recoverable enough to answer:

- what source state / baseline was projected;
- what was retained verbatim because wording is authority-sensitive;
- what was summarized or visually encoded;
- what was moved to support / appendix / interaction depth;
- what was omitted and why omission does not change the in-claim reading;
- which qualification / uncertainty / counterevidence remains coupled to the claim;
- where the reader can recover deeper evidence or method;
- what actual readback was performed on the projected artifact.

Do not use “available elsewhere” as a generic excuse for hiding a material limit. A qualifier may move out of the primary reading layer only when the primary claim remains bounded without it or the route to that qualification is immediate and unambiguous for the decision context.

Projection failure signals include:

- a source `HOLD / OPEN / ASSUMED / PREDICTED` state reading as settled fact;
- one option receiving materially richer evidence than comparison alternatives without an explicit reason;
- a number surviving while its unit, population, period, scenario or uncertainty disappears;
- a visual retaining persuasive impact after its evidence status or source context has been removed;
- a critical limitation being available only in speaker notes, hover state or inaccessible appendix;
- a responsive/adaptive variant dropping required relations rather than re-composing them.

`NO-LOSS PASS ≠ SAME LAYOUT`.

`TRACEABLE SOURCE ≠ SUCCESSFUL PROJECTION`.

## 14｜Design Content Depth and Density

Do not govern content by universal word counts.

Optional content-depth classes:

- `CD0 LOCATOR` — identity, location, status;
- `CD1 SUMMARY` — question, main claim, boundary;
- `CD2 OPERATIONAL` — inputs, method, output, conditions;
- `CD3 PROFESSIONAL` — mechanism, evidence, failure, limits, transfer, validation;
- `CD4 RESEARCH / EVIDENCE DEEP` — protocol, data, bias, uncertainty, counterevidence, reproducibility.

Optional reading-density classes:

- `DENSITY-A SIGNAL` — immediate conclusion;
- `DENSITY-B SCAN` — relation understandable by scanning;
- `DENSITY-C READ` — continuous explanatory reading;
- `DENSITY-D STUDY` — professional comparison/annotation;
- `DENSITY-E REFERENCE` — lookup-oriented detail.

These classes describe intended reading behavior. They do not create universal maximum word counts, character counts or page density scores.

Depth and density are separate controls:

```text
DEPTH = how much professional reasoning / evidence / boundary the artifact must carry
DENSITY = how much of that depth is simultaneously exposed in the current reading surface
```

A high-depth object may legitimately use a low-density primary layer if deeper material remains recoverable. A low-depth object does not become professionally deep by filling the surface with text, annotations or diagrams.

Choose required depth from the decision, not from the available page area. Increase depth when one or more of these rise:

- consequence of error;
- uncertainty or contested evidence;
- number of coupled systems/interfaces;
- irreversibility / cost of the decision;
- novelty or departure from established practice;
- need for professional handoff, audit or later reuse;
- claim strength;
- distance between source evidence and design consequence.

Then design density from the reading task:

- what must be perceived immediately;
- what must be compared without memory burden;
- what requires sustained reading;
- what is reference-only;
- what can be progressively disclosed without breaking no-loss;
- what must remain co-located despite visual cost.

Recommended development sequence:

```text
decision / claim
→ minimum semantic payload
→ required depth class
→ reading task / density class
→ hierarchy + grouping + disclosure
→ actual-size / actual-state readback
→ overload / under-specification finding
→ revise
```

False density solutions include shrinking text until it technically fits, converting explanation into unexplained iconography, replacing evidence with decorative diagrams, or spreading one inseparable comparison across surfaces that cannot be read together.

`MORE CONTENT ≠ MORE DEPTH`.

`LESS TEXT ≠ BETTER COMMUNICATION`.

`VISUAL SIMPLICITY ≠ SEMANTIC SIMPLICITY`.

## 15｜Content Role Profiles

When a design artifact contains formal body content, route it to the appropriate profile rather than one universal text template.

### 15.0｜Canonical-body ownership boundary

The profiles below define **what semantic responsibilities must survive when knowledge or professional content is used inside a design artifact**. They do not move ownership of the canonical body into this local Design Quality specification.

Canonical THEORY / FRAMEWORK / METHOD / SOURCE / EVIDENCE / CASE / PRACTICE / TOOL / STANDARD bodies remain governed by the Professional Knowledge Content & Research Standard and their current Knowledge Architecture owners. Their own titles, section hierarchy, mechanism/evidence/failure content, bilingual parity, research status, maturity and review state must be repaired at the canonical source rather than recreated here.

When such content is projected into a project board, report, presentation, interface or design-development record, this specification governs **projection, hierarchy, visual communication, no-loss, readback and design quality**. It must reference the canonical object rather than silently creating a second local body.

`CONTENT ROLE PROFILE ≠ CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE BODY`.

### 15.1 THEORY / FRAMEWORK

Minimum applicable responsibilities:

`Problem / Phenomenon → Definition → Conceptual Model → Key Constructs → Relations → Mechanism → Supporting Evidence → Competing Explanation → Boundary Conditions → Criticism / Failure → Design Interpretation → Misuse / Non-transfer → Validation / Maturity → Open Questions`.

### 15.2 METHOD

`Purpose → When to Use → When Not to Use → Preconditions → Inputs → Required Evidence → Roles → Procedure → Decision Points → Branch Conditions → Outputs → Validation → Failure Modes → Common Misuse → Tool/Capability Dependencies → Professional Judgment Boundary → Practice Example → Reopen Conditions`.

### 15.3 SOURCE

`Identity → Issuer/Author → Version/Edition/Date → Official Status → Scope → Jurisdiction/Applicability → Contents → What It Supports → What It Does Not Support → Relevant Locator → Freshness/Supersession → Access/Rights → Current OLEANDER Use`.

### 15.4 EVIDENCE

`Evidence Question → Provenance → Generation Method → Sample/Instrument/Model → Conditions → Raw/Derived Distinction → Result → Uncertainty → Quality/Bias → Reproducibility → Supports → Contradicts → Does Not Establish → Validation State → Availability`.

### 15.5 CASE / PRECEDENT

`Problem → Context → Selection Rationale → Source Hierarchy → Documented Facts → Design Move → Mechanism → Outcome → Failure/Limitation → Transferable Principle → Non-transferable Condition → Current Project Binding`.

Always distinguish:

`WHAT HAPPENED ≠ WHY IT WORKED ≠ WHAT WE SHOULD DO`.

### 15.6 PRACTICE

`Task → Initial State → Method Applied → Actual Execution → Native Artifact → Deviation → Result → Failure → Readback → Reviewer Finding → Lesson → Reuse Boundary`.

### 15.7 TOOL

`Identity → Version → Capability → Intended Task → Inputs → Outputs → Native Format → Exchange Format → Automation Surface → Runtime Requirements → Known Loss → Failure Conditions → Validation/Readback → Claim Ceiling → Alternatives → Deprecation/Freshness`.

### 15.8 STANDARD / CODE

`Authority → Jurisdiction → Edition → Effective Date → Scope → Relevant Clauses → Mandatory/Advisory Distinction → Interpretation Boundary → Project Applicability → Conflict/Precedence → Verification Method → Supersession/Amendment`.

### 15.9 PROJECT DECISION

`Question → Context → Authority → Alternatives → Evaluation Criteria → Evidence → Trade-offs → Selected Decision → Rejected Alternatives → Accepted Cost → Dependencies → Affected Artifacts → Affected Interfaces → Assumptions → Open Items → Reopen Condition → Reviewer → Date`.

### 15.10 DESIGN DEVELOPMENT BODY

`Design Question → Existing Condition → Evidence/Constraint → Design Intent → Design Criteria → Alternatives → Selected Move → Formal/Spatial/Functional Mechanism → Human Experience Consequence → Technical Consequence → Cross-disciplinary Impact → Trade-off → Risk → Assumption → Unresolved Item → Validation → Next Development Action`.

Profiles are semantic responsibilities, not mandatory visible headings.

When more than one role is present in one artifact, do not merge their authority semantics. For example, a `CASE / PRECEDENT` may contain `EVIDENCE`, but precedent transfer remains an interpretation; a `PROJECT DECISION` may cite a `STANDARD / CODE`, but the project record does not become the standard's canonical owner; a `DESIGN DEVELOPMENT BODY` may use a `METHOD`, but current execution findings do not rewrite the reusable method body.

Use the narrowest role that explains why the content exists in the current artifact, and retain explicit refs to other canonical roles when they supply authority or evidence.

## 16｜Paragraph and Sentence Truth Patterns

Preferred professional paragraph patterns include:

- `Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Boundary`;
- `Condition → Mechanism → Consequence → Exception`;
- `A ↔ Criterion ↔ B → Difference → Consequence`;
- `Question → Option → Trade-off → Decision → Reopen Condition`;
- `Known → Unknown → Why → Impact → Next Validation`.

Avoid:

- generic AI filler;
- decorative headings without substantive content;
- one-sentence paragraph spam;
- dense paragraphs containing several unrelated semantic moves;
- citation lists without synthesis;
- unsupported evaluative language;
- generic `Benefits / Challenges / Future Outlook` structure where it does not fit the object role.

Controlled strong terms require evidence at the claimed level:

`VALIDATED / VERIFIED / EVIDENCE-BASED / USER-TESTED / FIELD-VERIFIED / COMPLIANT / SAFE / OPTIMAL / PROVEN / BEST PRACTICE`.

When evidence is weaker, use bounded language such as:

`SUPPORTED BY / INDICATED BY / PRELIMINARY / PREDICTED / REFERENCE / CANDIDATE / ASSUMED / VALIDATION OPEN`.

Paragraph structure should make the direction of inference inspectable. The reader should be able to tell whether a sentence is:

- reporting an observed/source condition;
- interpreting that condition;
- making a design decision;
- predicting a consequence;
- recording an unresolved question;
- defining a boundary on what the evidence proves.

Do not compress those functions into one authoritative-sounding sentence when they have different evidence states.

For consequential claims, prefer a local evidence locator near the claim rather than a distant undifferentiated bibliography. A citation proves provenance of the cited source; it does not by itself prove that the current interpretation, transfer or design decision is valid.

Sentence-level failure signals include:

- an adjective such as `better`, `appropriate`, `premium`, `intuitive`, `safe` or `efficient` with no criterion;
- causal language where only correlation, precedent or simulation exists;
- “the design ensures…” when only intent or proposal exists;
- plural/general claims derived from one case or one user;
- a future or desired state written in present tense as implemented fact;
- passive wording that hides the professional decision owner;
- status language without the condition or scope that makes the status valid.

`CLEAR WRITING ≠ STRONGER CLAIM`.

Clarity must expose uncertainty and trade-off, not edit them away.

```text
→ INTERPRETATION / DESIGN CONSEQUENCE
```

The figure's authority cannot exceed the authority of its source and derivation. In particular:

- a render can demonstrate intended appearance, camera composition or modeled spatial relation; it does not prove field condition, photometric performance, material performance or constructability;
- a diagram can explain a relation or hypothesis; it does not prove the relation exists in the native artifact unless bound to real data/geometry/state;
- a drawing can carry professional design information only at the scale, issue status and responsibility boundary actually developed;
- a screenshot can document observed software state; it does not replace the editable/native source when editability or underlying data is part of the claim;
- an AI-generated or illustrative image may communicate a bounded concept/reference but must not be presented as measured, observed, constructed or source-faithful evidence unless that relation is independently established.

Where geometry, quantity or performance is claimed, the visual should expose enough scale/state information to interpret the claim: dimension, scale bar, grid, coordinate, legend, unit, time/state, scenario or other appropriate locator. Do not add fake precision merely to make a visual look technical.

Technical and analytical figures must survive actual-use readback:

- lines, symbols and labels remain distinguishable at delivery size;
- comparison states use consistent enough framing/scale to support the intended comparison;
- critical annotations are not clipped, occluded or dependent on inaccessible zoom;
- legend/color/line-style meaning survives grayscale, print, projection or accessibility conditions when those are in scope;
- vector/live text and editable geometry are retained in the native master where downstream editing, scaling or technical reuse is required;
- raster export, preview or screenshot is treated as readback/delivery evidence, not as automatic replacement of the editable master.

A caption should add the missing interpretive or authority layer rather than repeat the title. For evidence/technical figures, include the decisive condition, source/derivation and limitation when those are required to prevent a false reading.

`FIGURE EXISTS ≠ FIGURE PROVES`.

`REALISTIC IMAGE ≠ REALITY EVIDENCE`.

`TECHNICAL LOOK ≠ TECHNICAL AUTHORITY`.

## 18｜Typography, Reading Distance and Bilingual Design

Typography must be role-based rather than an arbitrary heading-size ladder.

Typical roles include:

`DISPLAY / SECTION / CLAIM / BODY / EVIDENCE / CAPTION / SOURCE / LABEL / STATUS / NUMERIC / TECHNICAL / CODE-ID`.

For physical or large-format outputs, consider reading layers where applicable:

- `FAR` — dominant object / headline / major section;
- `MID` — claim / diagram / annotation;
- `NEAR` — body / caption / evidence;
- `DETAIL` — source / dimension / technical metadata.

Bilingual design requires more than semantic translation parity. Where applicable check:

- semantic parity;
- terminology parity;
- hierarchy parity;
- reading-weight parity;
- line-break quality;
- reference parity;
- numbers / units / dates / versions;
- mixed-script rhythm.

Equal semantic role does not require equal literal line count or equal font size across scripts.

Typography is part of the information model. A change of type role, weight, scale, alignment, spacing or placement changes perceived hierarchy and therefore can change the effective claim structure even when words are unchanged.

Do not set universal point sizes as OLEANDER truth. Resolve typography against:

- physical/display dimensions;
- expected viewing distance and viewing time;
- medium and pixel/print resolution;
- ambient/contrast conditions where material;
- typeface metrics and script coverage;
- language/script length behavior;
- audience and accessibility needs;
- information density and competing visual hierarchy.

For consequential physical or presentation artifacts, validate at the intended output size rather than only at editor zoom. A technically present source line that cannot be read in the real viewing condition is not a successful visible source layer.

Bilingual composition must preserve semantic **role** and reference coupling, not force mechanical mirroring. Acceptable differences may include line count, line breaks, measure, local spacing and scale where needed for equivalent hierarchy/readability. Unacceptable differences include stronger claim language in one language, missing qualifications, mismatched figure/source references, inconsistent numbers/units or one language being visually demoted below the role it is supposed to share.

Check mixed-language artifacts for:

- script-specific punctuation and spacing;
- numeral/unit consistency;
- date/version ambiguity;
- acronym expansion and stable terminology;
- baseline/vertical alignment where scripts differ materially;
- line-breaks that separate a qualifier, unit or reference from the content it governs;
- fallback-font substitution in export/delivery;
- text remaining editable/vector in the native production master when later revision or high-quality scaling is required.

`TRANSLATED ≠ BILINGUALLY DESIGNED`.

`LEGIBLE IN EDITOR ≠ LEGIBLE IN DELIVERY CONTEXT`.

## 19｜Editorial / Publication and Presentation Separation

### Editorial / Publication

Owns long-form reading systems such as report, book, catalog, manual and long-form case study.

Typical control objects:

`content inventory → flat plan → page/spread role → master/grid options → type/image/caption/folio → pacing → production conditions → full-sequence readback`.

### Presentation / Projection

Owns time-bounded or audience-specific communication such as live review, pitch, jury, roadshow or decision deck.

For material presentation pages use, where helpful:

`Audience Question → Primary Claim / Decision → Primary Proof → Visual Relation → Spoken Context → Next Narrative Step`.

`REPORT / DOCUMENT ≠ LIVE PRESENTATION`.

Presentation compression may route depth to support/appendix but must preserve no-loss traceability.

Editorial and presentation artifacts may share the same canonical content and design language, but they solve different time structures:

```text
EDITORIAL / PUBLICATION
reader controls pace
→ navigation + sustained argument + recoverable reference depth

PRESENTATION / PROJECTION
presenter / event controls pace
→ immediate orientation + decision sequence + visible proof + controlled spoken context
```

Do not create a presentation by cropping report pages, or a report by stacking presentation slides, unless actual readback proves the resulting reading system works. The source relation may be shared; sequence, density, repetition and navigation must be redesigned for the carrier.

For live presentation, critical claim boundaries must remain visible enough that the spoken track is not the sole carrier of a limitation, status or uncertainty. Speaker notes may add context; they must not hide information required to keep the visible claim truthful.

For editorial/publication, full-sequence review must test more than individual page quality:

- entry and orientation;
- chapter/section rhythm;
- continuity of terminology and references;
- repetition vs necessary re-orientation;
- spread/page turns and comparison integrity;
- figure/caption/source continuity;
- appendix/support recoverability;
- closing synthesis and return path;
- export/print/digital production behavior.

For presentations, full-sequence review should test:

- audience question at each step;
- one primary decision/claim relationship per moment unless intentional comparison requires otherwise;
- evidence appearing before or with the claim it must support;
- transition logic and memory burden;
- timing against actual speaking/decision conditions;
- fallback when media, animation or interaction fails;
- silent/read-alone replay when the artifact is expected to circulate after the event.

`GOOD PAGE ≠ GOOD SEQUENCE`.

`GOOD REPORT ≠ GOOD PRESENTATION`.

## 20｜Design Resolution Scale

Review design at the scales relevant to the medium:

- `R0 SYSTEM`;
- `R1 WHOLE`;
- `R2 ZONE / CHAPTER / SUBSYSTEM`;
- `R3 ELEMENT`;
- `R4 COMPONENT`;
- `R5 DETAIL`;
- `R6 MICRO / FINISH`.

Examples:

- architecture: campus → building → room → wall/opening → component → joint → finish;
- editorial: publication → spread → page → module → element → typographic/graphic detail;
- product: family → product → assembly → part → interface → surface → finish.

A strong R1 hero does not compensate for failed R3–R6 resolution where those scales are in claim.

### 20.1｜Scale-selection rule

`R0...R6` is a **review-resolution vocabulary**, not a universal physical-size classification. Each project or medium maps the levels to its own object hierarchy and identifies which levels are materially in claim for the current decision.

Choose scales from the failure mode the design claim could hide. A whole-system question may require R0/R1 only; a detailing/craft claim may require R3–R6; an integration claim may require a cross-scale path because a relation that looks coherent at R1 may fail at a penetration, component, state or finish transition.

For every material review round, resolve where applicable:

`design question → in-claim resolution scales → native artifact/view at each scale → target readback condition → observed relation → defect / decision → affected higher/lower scale`.

The scale list in a DQ receipt records **what was actually reviewed**, not what exists in the project. Do not list R4–R6 merely because a model contains small geometry or a publication contains fine type.

### 20.2｜Cross-scale continuity

A design relation should survive the scale transitions that materially support the claim. Review at least the following questions where applicable:

- does R0/R1 intent remain visible in R2/R3 organization rather than existing only in a hero explanation?
- do R3/R4 components reinforce rather than contradict the larger hierarchy/form/experience?
- do R5/R6 details, joints, typography, transitions, finishes or micro-interactions sustain the claimed craft and not expose generic/default behavior?
- does local repair at a lower scale create a higher-scale rhythm, alignment, interface or performance defect?
- does a high-level redesign invalidate downstream detail readbacks that were based on the prior geometry/state?

When a changed relation crosses scales, reopen only the dependent scale chain. A revised component does not automatically invalidate the whole system; a changed system geometry cannot leave dependent components falsely Current.

### 20.3｜Readback evidence and machine boundary

Each in-claim scale needs an actual readback appropriate to that medium. The readback may be a native model/drawing view, browser/runtime state, physical mockup/test, print proof, full-resolution image, actual-size detail, or other owner-native evidence. A screenshot may document a readback but cannot replace the authoritative source where the claim depends on editable geometry/data/state.

For a final `KEEP`, every listed `artifact_ref` must participate in at least one declared `readback_condition`, and each in-scope readback condition supporting that KEEP must be `PASS`. The validator may check this structural consistency; it still cannot decide whether the design is aesthetically or professionally good.

`MODEL CONTAINS DETAIL ≠ DETAIL REVIEWED`.

`R1 KEEP ≠ R5 KEEP`.

`MORE RESOLUTION LEVELS ≠ HIGHER DESIGN QUALITY`.

## 21｜Design Maturity States

Design maturity is separate from Project Status, Evidence Code, professional stage and artifact review state.

Use when helpful. `DQ*` is reserved inside this specification as a **Design Quality maturity prefix only**; it is not a Project Axis, Knowledge level, Delivery Priority, Evidence Code, professional stage, Gate family or Case identifier.

States:

- `DQ0 UNDEFINED` — no meaningful design position yet;
- `DQ1 DIRECTION` — intent / direction exists but is weakly instantiated;
- `DQ2 COHERENT CANDIDATE` — core relations are visible and internally coherent;
- `DQ3 DEVELOPED SYSTEM` — design survives multiple elements/states/scales with detail development;
- `DQ4 DISTINCTIVE RESOLVED SYSTEM` — coherent, crafted, project-specific and professionally reviewable at intended scope;
- `DQ5 PROVEN CROSS-CONTEXT LANGUAGE` — repeated real application/readback demonstrates the language can adapt without losing identity.

### 21.1｜Maturity transition evidence

The maturity state is assigned from the strongest level whose defining evidence is actually present at the declared scope. It is not a checklist score and it does not advance because time, stage number, document count or model detail increases.

Typical transition evidence is:

| Transition | Evidence required at the Design claim ceiling | Typical reason to remain lower |
|---|---|---|
| `DQ0 → DQ1` | explicit intent/direction plus at least one instantiated relation that can be reviewed | only adjectives, mood references or unresolved brief |
| `DQ1 → DQ2` | core relations visible in a coherent candidate, with contradictions/open items explicit | direction exists but elements/states still conflict |
| `DQ2 → DQ3` | system survives the materially required scales/states/variants and has developed detail/craft | strong single state/hero but weak system behavior |
| `DQ3 → DQ4` | resolved project-specific system, strong cross-scale coherence, independent design judgment and no in-claim hard Design FAIL | competent system but generic, under-crafted or unresolved in a material state |
| `DQ4 → DQ5` | more than one materially different real context/application read back successfully with the identity/grammar relation preserved and transfer limits explicit | only one project/context, copied repetition, or transfer inferred from similarity |

`DQ5` remains a Design Quality maturity claim. It does **not** create canonical reusable Knowledge, award `KI4`, award `OE3`, or prove a universal rule. Any reusable method/pattern/language candidate still follows the existing G9 → Knowledge validation/lifecycle route.

### 21.2｜Hold, downgrade and reopen

Maturity may remain stable while the Project Status changes, and Project Current may legitimately carry DQ0–DQ3 when the claim ceiling says so. Conversely, a material contradiction, superseding Design DNA decision, failed actual readback or changed authority can require a lower current maturity claim or a reopened maturity decision even if a prior receipt recorded a higher state for an earlier baseline.

Do not rewrite the old receipt. Preserve its historical maturity judgment against its original baseline and emit a successor/revalidation receipt for the new Current relation.

A DQ maturity change should state:

`prior maturity + baseline → changed relation/evidence → affected scope → current observed condition → new bounded maturity → required next action / reopen trigger`.

### 21.3｜Machine-checkable limits

Machines may reject structurally impossible combinations such as `KEEP + stale=true`, `KEEP` with non-PASS required readback, DQ4/DQ5 without independent reviewer independence, or a missing body/native/readback locator. Machines may not infer DQ3–DQ5 from artifact count, elapsed stage, score averages, rendered quality, model complexity or process completion.

Rules:

- DQ maturity is not a statutory, field, engineering or evidence level;
- machines may validate presence/consistency of declared maturity fields but may not auto-award DQ3–DQ5;
- DQ4/DQ5 require independent design judgment;
- a project may remain `CURRENT` while design maturity is intentionally lower if Current Authority and claim ceiling say so.

`DQ MATURITY ≠ PROJECT STATUS`.

`DQ MATURITY ≠ PROFESSIONAL-STAGE COMPLETION`.

`DQ5 ≠ CURRENT REUSABLE KNOWLEDGE`.

## 22｜Design Review Lens

When Design Quality is material, review applicable questions from the following lens. Do not average into a total score.

### DQR-01 Concept-to-form

Does the visible/spatial/interactive form actually embody the stated concept?

### DQR-02 Visual / Spatial Ownership

Is there deliberate hierarchy and ownership, or only equally weighted modules/elements?

### DQR-03 Composition Strength

Do relations of dominance, balance, tension, void, rhythm and sequence produce a convincing whole?

### DQR-04 Proportion / Scale

Are macro, meso and micro proportions intentional and contextually appropriate?

### DQR-05 Figure–Ground / Mass–Void

Is the primary perceptual structure legible and controlled?

### DQR-06 Experience Sequence

Does temporal/spatial/user sequence support the stated experience?

### DQR-07 Human Relation

Does body, behavior, use, control or reading remain credible?

### DQR-08 Sensory Quality

Are light/material/sound/touch/etc. aligned with the design intent where relevant?

### DQR-09 Color Character

Does color operate as a coherent semantic and aesthetic system rather than decoration?

### DQR-10 Typography Character

Does type simultaneously support role, readability, identity and medium?

### DQR-11 Image / Camera Direction

Does image selection/framing/crop/light/sequence carry the intended design relation?

### DQR-12 Material / Detail / Craft

Do edges, joints, transitions and finishes sustain the project language at close range?

### DQR-13 Signature / Memorability

Is there a justified reason the work is recognizably this project rather than a generic template?

### DQR-14 Variation Control

Can the design vary without either flattening into sameness or losing identity?

### DQR-15 Cross-media / Cross-state Translation

Does the same design logic survive different media, responsive states, operational conditions or scales where required?

### DQR-16 Genericity / Cliché Attack

Attack with at least these questions when relevant:

1. If project name, logo and project-specific imagery are removed, is meaningful project-specific design logic still visible?
2. Is distinctiveness carried only by font/color choice?
3. If decorative effects are removed, does the composition/form still work?
4. Is the design relying on a common template pattern because no stronger project-specific relation was resolved?
5. Is a cultural/style reference being imitated rather than transformed through project logic?

### DQR-17 Coherence

Do intent, concept, function, form, material, detail, experience and expression support rather than undermine each other?

### DQR-18 Final Craft

At the intended final viewing/use condition, are spacing, alignment, edge, crop, caption, transition, line, material, motion and other last-mile relations professionally resolved?

### 22.1｜Lens evidence and decision thresholds

The review lens is only useful when each triggered question has a **decision threshold**. A reviewer should not stop at “looks weak/strong”; the finding should name the relation, evidence and repair consequence.

| Lens | Evidence expected when material | REVISE / HOLD threshold |
|---|---|---|
| `DQR-01 Concept-to-form` | current concept rule + native artifact/readback showing the rule's actual consequence | major visible/spatial/interactive decisions cannot be derived from or are contradicted by the claimed generative rule |
| `DQR-02 Ownership` | first-read/distance/state comparison with supporting hierarchy visible | two or more unintended equal owners compete, or the declared owner only exists through decorative emphasis |
| `DQR-03 Composition` | whole-state and cropped/detail readbacks across relevant scales | balance/tension/void/rhythm fails at the actual viewing/use state or is rescued only by labels/styling |
| `DQR-04 Proportion / Scale` | dimensioned or comparable scale evidence tied to body/context/medium | material macro/meso/micro proportions conflict with intended hierarchy, use, reading distance or neighboring systems |
| `DQR-05 Figure–Ground / Mass–Void` | reduced-detail/plan/section/frame readback | primary structure becomes ambiguous when color, labels or effects are reduced |
| `DQR-06 Experience Sequence` | ordered route/state/time readback including transitions and recovery | a critical moment is unreachable, confusing, discontinuous, mistimed or contradicts the intended sequence |
| `DQR-07 Human Relation` | task/body/clearance/control/readability evidence | required use depends on implausible posture, reach, clearance, control, cognitive load or excluded user condition |
| `DQR-08 Sensory Quality` | material/light/sound/thermal/tactile/environmental evidence appropriate to claim | claimed sensory effect is unsupported by the real carrier or conflicts with comfort, use, maintenance or technical condition |
| `DQR-09 Color Character` | system/state/context readback including adjacent materials/images/data | color is decorative only, destroys hierarchy/legibility, or semantic roles and aesthetic roles conflict materially |
| `DQR-10 Typography Character` | target-size/distance/language readback with real content extremes | required text hierarchy/readability fails, or character depends on a type treatment unusable in actual content/language states |
| `DQR-11 Image / Camera` | source-aware sequence/crop/viewpoint comparison | framing/crop/light/sequence changes the claim, hides evidence, or produces no coherent project-specific image logic |
| `DQR-12 Material / Detail / Craft` | close-range native detail/sample/mockup/readback | edge/joint/finish/transition remains unresolved at the final claimed scale or contradicts assembly, maintenance or system language |
| `DQR-13 Signature / Memorability` | name/logo-off and cross-state recognition test | distinctiveness collapses when branding/decorative assets are removed, or the signature is repeated until hierarchy disappears |
| `DQR-14 Variation Control` | nominal + boundary/adverse-state readbacks | an allowed variation breaks identity, use, interface or professional constraint, or needs an undeclared new rule |
| `DQR-15 Cross-media / Cross-state` | same relation checked in every required medium/state | translation changes the governing relation, hides required evidence, or creates a separate style/system with no explicit exception |
| `DQR-16 Genericity / Cliché` | comparison to common template pattern plus project-specific relation evidence | the work remains materially interchangeable with a generic template after project labels/assets are removed |
| `DQR-17 Coherence` | cross-domain/native whole-state review and contradiction list | one locally successful system materially damages another required design relation without an explicit accepted trade-off |
| `DQR-18 Final Craft` | final output/use-condition readback, not editor/source view only | visible last-mile defects, inconsistent edges/spacing/transitions, or production artifacts remain at the claimed final condition |

The threshold is scoped to the current design claim. A `REVISE` finding must name the **dominant relation to repair** and the smallest truthful reopen scope; a `HOLD` should identify the missing evidence/authority/readback preventing a judgment. `REJECT` is appropriate when the current route is not worth repairing within the stated intent/constraints, not as a synonym for low polish.

When two lenses conflict, do not average them. Record the trade-off and identify which criterion has controlling authority for the present claim. Examples include hierarchy vs accessibility, transparency vs privacy, minimal detail vs maintainability, openness vs acoustic separation, visual lightness vs structural/service depth, or expressive motion vs interaction stability.

The lens is a review instrument, not a mandatory eighteen-part scorecard. Trigger only the questions materially relevant to the design claim, but do not omit a weak dimension merely because it is inconvenient to present.

For each triggered lens, review through the same evidence discipline:

```text
stated intent / criterion
→ current native artifact or experience state
→ actual readback at relevant scale/state/medium
→ observed relation
→ strongest evidence of success
→ strongest contradiction / weakness
→ professional interpretation
→ KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD at bounded scope
```

The reviewer should distinguish at least four kinds of statement:

- **observed** — directly visible/experienced in the current readback;
- **inferred** — professional interpretation of the observed relation;
- **expected** — predicted consequence not yet observed at the claimed condition;
- **required** — criterion or authority that the design must meet.

Do not convert “I can see the designer's intention” into “the design succeeds.” Intent recognition is only one input. Review the actual consequence.

A useful independent review should identify both the strongest resolved relation and the weakest consequential relation. A review containing only praise, only defects, or only checklist completion is insufficient when it does not reveal what controls the next design decision.

`LENS COVERAGE ≠ DESIGN QUALITY SCORE`.

`REVIEWER AGREEMENT ≠ EVIDENCE OF FIELD / TECHNICAL PERFORMANCE`.

## 23｜Comparison-First Design Development

When a material design question is genuinely open, use comparison as a selection mechanism.

Default logic:

`Question → design hypothesis → materially distinct candidate A/B/... → fixed comparison condition → whole-frame / relevant-scale readback → attack → select / reject / branch → develop`.

Rules:

- changing only font size, spacing, color, corner radius, shadow or another micro-parameter does not create a materially different composition when the structural question remains unchanged;
- comparison count is not a quality metric;
- a single option is acceptable when authority/technical conditions have already removed the meaningful alternative space;
- keep rejected alternatives when their failure materially improves future reasoning;
- if no candidate improves the named root cause, retain `REVISE` and reopen the design hypothesis.

Comparison is valid only when the comparison condition is explicit enough to prevent a false winner. Before choosing among candidates, state:

- the design question being tested;
- which variables intentionally differ;
- which variables are held sufficiently constant for the decision;
- which constraints/authority conditions every candidate must respect;
- the readback view/scale/state used for comparison;
- the criteria that can legitimately decide the question;
- which criteria remain outside the comparison claim.

Do not compare a fully developed candidate against a schematic candidate and attribute the difference to the design hypothesis itself. Do not give one candidate better imagery, annotation, camera, content depth or technical resolution unless that difference is itself the variable under test.

Where the question is temporal, spatial, interactive or multi-state, comparison must include the relevant sequence/state set rather than only a favorable hero frame.

Candidate development should preserve decision lineage:

```text
candidate
→ what relation it changes
→ why that relation might solve the question
→ what readback supports / contradicts it
→ why it is retained, rejected or branched
→ what survives into the next cycle
```

`MORE OPTIONS ≠ BETTER EXPLORATION`.

`VISIBLE DIFFERENCE ≠ MATERIAL DESIGN DIFFERENCE`.

`COMPARISON ARTIFACT EXISTS ≠ COMPARISON IS FAIR`.

## 24｜Root Cause and Convergence

After an actual Design `REVISE`, identify the dominant root cause before producing another version.

Possible root-cause layers include:

- design question / intent;
- concept;
- content / evidence;
- information architecture;
- composition architecture;
- spatial relation;
- asset/image quality;
- design language;
- typography;
- material/detail;
- interaction/state;
- professional interface;
- technical constraint;
- execution/tool limitation.

If the same dominant root cause survives two consecutive revisions, stop micro-tuning and reclassify the problem layer before another candidate.

`ITERATION COUNT ≠ CONVERGENCE`.

Treat the visible defect as a symptom until the controlling relation is identified. A root-cause statement should be specific enough to select the next kind of design work.

Weak diagnosis:

`page feels busy`.

Stronger diagnosis pattern:

```text
observed failure
→ affected user/design consequence
→ controlling relation / layer
→ evidence that the relation causes or materially contributes to the failure
→ repair hypothesis
→ expected readback change
```

Examples of layer mismatch to avoid:

- fixing weak hierarchy by changing only color when the real problem is information architecture;
- fixing poor circulation legibility by polishing graphics when the spatial topology is unresolved;
- fixing genericity by adding more signature decoration when the generative relation is generic;
- fixing a detail conflict by redrawing the detail while the upstream system boundary remains contradictory;
- fixing bilingual overflow by shrinking one language when the actual problem is content priority or layout structure.

If a root cause sits upstream of the current artifact, reopen that upstream decision rather than forcing a local patch. If several symptoms share one root cause, repair the root once and re-read all dependent states instead of applying unrelated cosmetic fixes.

Convergence means the dominant in-claim contradictions reduce while the intended relations remain or strengthen. A version that merely looks different is not evidence of convergence.

## 25｜Hard FAIL / Mandatory REVISE Conditions

The following cannot be averaged away by attractive styling, process completeness or technical artifact quality:

- concept exists only as explanatory prose with no visible/operational consequence;
- a project-specific design-quality claim relies only on generic template structure;
- primary hierarchy is unresolved and multiple elements compete equally without intent;
- critical content/evidence is visually suppressed to strengthen the narrative;
- style reference is copied as surface identity rather than translated through project logic;
- project-specific Design DNA is contradicted by the promoted artifact without a recorded exception/revision;
- signature move is repeated mechanically until it overrides function/clarity;
- local polish masks weak whole-system composition;
- strong hero state is promoted while required supporting states/pages/spaces collapse;
- detail resolution contradicts function, construction, accessibility, interaction or professional truth;
- a responsive/adaptive state loses required hierarchy or identity;
- a technical repair materially damages the design intent and no redesign/readback follows;
- decorative effect is presented as evidence;
- a design claim exceeds the actual prototype/readback/evidence ceiling;
- current artifact has not been reviewed at its intended size/state/medium;
- old Design Review receipt is reused after a material change to content, geometry, composition, Design DNA, interface or native asset.

These conditions define a lower bound on professional response; they do not automatically determine whether the correct disposition is `REVISE`, `REJECT` or `HOLD`. That disposition depends on scope, severity, authority, recoverability and whether the defect can be repaired inside the current design hypothesis.

Use `HOLD` when a consequential judgment cannot yet be made because a required source, native state, interface, readback or authority condition is missing. Use `REVISE` when the design question remains valid but the current resolution is inadequate. Use `REJECT` when the candidate/design move should not continue within the current bounded question. These meanings must not be collapsed into “failed score”.

A hard Design failure may be local in scope. Do not automatically invalidate unrelated resolved work; mark the affected relation, artifact/state and downstream dependencies. Conversely, do not label a whole artifact `KEEP` when a local failure sits inside the promoted claim.

## 26｜Reopen / Change Propagation

Material changes must reopen the affected design scope.

Typical triggers include:

- design question / brief / audience change;
- material program/use/operational change;
- major geometry / composition / hierarchy change;
- new or replaced primary asset;
- Design DNA or brand authority change;
- change to signature move or formal grammar;
- change to material / color / typography that alters identity or readability;
- major interface/technical repair;
- new contradictory evidence;
- change in target medium / size / viewport / production condition;
- major language/localization expansion;
- failed prototype/usability/field/readback evidence;
- new lifecycle/maintenance/aging condition;
- change to a controlled variable from another professional domain.

The **Current consumer/claim binding to the previous receipt becomes stale at the affected scope** until re-readback and review. Do not mutate the historical receipt to a newly invented `STALE_AT_AFFECTED_SCOPE` state; preserve the receipt under its original baseline and express Current staleness/reopen through the existing dependency, receipt-stale/successor and review mechanisms.

Reopen is dependency-driven rather than version-count-driven. Use the smallest affected scope that remains truthful, then propagate outward wherever another decision consumes the changed relation.

Canonical change path:

```text
material change
→ changed authority / relation / controlled variable
→ direct design decisions affected
→ native artifacts / states affected
→ professional / integration interfaces affected
→ readbacks invalidated
→ Current consumption of affected receipts / review findings marked stale or reopened at affected scope
→ targeted redevelopment
→ new actual readback
→ new bounded review / receipt
```

Preserve prior evidence as provenance; do not rewrite history to make the new version appear inevitable. A stale receipt can remain useful evidence of what was previously reviewed, but it cannot authorize the changed scope.

Not every edit requires whole-project reopen. Examples that may remain local when dependency review confirms no wider effect include spelling correction, non-semantic metadata correction, or export repair that does not change content, geometry, hierarchy, state or authoritative native source. The burden is to show that the change is non-material, not to assume every “small edit” is harmless.

Where a changed controlled variable crosses professional domains, Design Quality reopen and Integration/professional reopen are separate operations. One cannot silently close the other.

`NEW VERSION ≠ AUTOMATIC REOPEN OF EVERYTHING`.

`SMALL FILE DIFF ≠ NON-MATERIAL DESIGN CHANGE`.

## 27｜Design Quality & Development Receipt

For material design-quality promotion, emit `DESIGN_QUALITY_DEVELOPMENT_RECEIPT`.

Minimum fields:

- `project_id`;
- `body_structure` — body identity/title plus normalized semantic heading coverage;
- `design_question`;
- `design_intent`;
- `claim_ceiling`;
- `triggered_design_dimensions`;
- `body_structure.knowledge_mount_refs` when canonical knowledge/evidence inputs materially support the decision — owner-issued R-B mount-record refs, not bare Knowledge page refs;
- `knowledge_mounts` only as bounded **non-authoritative snapshots** of the R-B mount boundary actually consumed, with mount-record identity/revision traceable; historical unbound snapshots may remain immutable evidence but cannot by themselves establish Current OE;

Machine-readable schema family: `schemas/design-quality-development-receipt.v1.schema.json`.

The v1 family is versioned prospectively rather than rewriting historical evidence:

- `schema_version = 1.0` remains a read-compatible historical carrier. Existing immutable receipts may contain the earlier `OPEN / IN_PROGRESS` dimension values and legacy `operational_eligibility` snapshot field. Their historical validity is not upgraded into a Current Design claim merely because the current validator can still read them.
- `schema_version = 1.1` is the Current emission contract. New receipts use pure Design-review dispositions for dimension fields, owner-issued R-B mount bindings, exact independent-review input bindings when consequential review is required, and `in_claim_blocking_design_fail_count` for machine closure.
- a v1.0 receipt is never rewritten in place to impersonate v1.1. If its evidence is still needed for a new consequential claim, resolve Current owner/native/readback identity and emit successor/revalidation evidence under the Current contract.

`READ-COMPATIBLE LEGACY RECEIPT ≠ CURRENT PROMOTION-ELIGIBLE RECEIPT`.

Receipt-state rule: `KEEP` is invalid when `stale=true`, when independent review is not `KEEP`, when `coherence_state` is not `KEEP`, when `genericity_attack` remains `REVISE / REJECT / HOLD`, when any declared in-scope readback condition remains non-PASS, when an `artifact_ref` has no declared readback condition, or when a material in-scope Design hard-fail remains unresolved. A `REVISE` receipt must name the current dominant root cause rather than only listing cosmetic changes.

When `candidate_comparison.applicable=true`, the receipt must identify at least two actual candidate refs, the fixed comparison conditions that make the comparison fair, and the bounded decision. One polished option plus an undeveloped placeholder is not a valid comparison record.

For `DQ4 / DQ5`, reviewer independence is structurally required even when the overall receipt is not `KEEP`; this prevents a high-maturity declaration from being carried only by producer self-judgment. The validator checks the encoded independence field but does not infer reviewer competence or award the maturity.

`body_structure.title` must be a real decision/body title rather than a generic placeholder. `body_structure.section_coverage` normalizes the §4.0 semantic heading responsibilities while allowing professional-domain-native visible wording. A receipt may report `MISSING` during development, but an in-scope missing structural responsibility prevents final Design `KEEP` unless the item is explicitly `NOT_APPLICABLE_WITH_REASON`.

`knowledge_mounts` stores a reproducibility snapshot of an owner-issued R-B mount; it is not an OE decision surface. New consequential v1.1 snapshots must be traceable to `body_structure.knowledge_mount_refs`, identify their `source_mount_record_ref`, and bind the consumed owner-native revision/hash. The legacy field name `operational_eligibility` may be read only for v1.0 historical receipt compatibility; v1.1 records use `eligibility_snapshot`. In either form, the value is non-authoritative until the referenced Current R-B mount is dereferenced. The receipt must never inline the canonical knowledge body.

For **new v1.1 emissions**, the per-dimension fields `design_language_state / experience_state / human_relation_state / sensory_state / detail_craft_state / adaptation_state / meaning_memory_state / coherence_state / content_projection_state` are Design-review dispositions, not progress states. Their shared v1.1 vocabulary is `NOT_APPLICABLE_WITH_REASON / KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD`; progress/open work belongs in `open_items`, `reopened_items`, readback conditions and owner-native workflow state rather than an `OPEN / IN_PROGRESS` mini-state system. `NOT_APPLICABLE_WITH_REASON` is permitted only for a genuinely non-applicable dimension and must carry the corresponding reason; unresolved applicability is `HOLD`, not a fabricated N/A. For overall Design `KEEP`, the always-applicable core fields `design_language / experience / detail_craft / adaptation / coherence` must be `KEEP`, while the explicitly conditional human/sensory/meaning/content-projection fields may be `NOT_APPLICABLE_WITH_REASON` when the reason is real and bounded. Historical v1.0 `OPEN / IN_PROGRESS` values remain readable only as prior progress semantics and do not define the v1.1 vocabulary.

For v1.1, `independent_design_review` binds the reviewer identity plus every exact reviewed native input through `review_input_bindings[].artifact_ref + revision_or_hash`. This binding proves what was reviewed, not reviewer competence, authorization or the review verdict. `R-I` remains the review owner. A v1.1 `KEEP`, or a DQ4/DQ5 declaration that requires independent review, cannot close without this exact binding.

For v1.1, `in_claim_blocking_design_fail_count` is a machine-readable projection of unresolved blocking Design failures inside the claim. It does not replace defect/open-item bodies. `KEEP` requires the count to be zero; an unresolved in-claim hard Design fail cannot be hidden in free-text `open_items` while the machine summary claims closure.

The receipt validator may be run with `--project-root <path>` when project-local path refs are available. In that mode it dereferences path-like native artifact, Project Design DNA and Operational Mount refs and verifies a declared SHA-256 mount binding when the receipt encodes one. Opaque external/provider IDs remain the responsibility of their owner/readback route; the structural validator does not invent a universal file registry.

The receipt is a **decision record over current owners**, not the owner of the design itself. It should make the review reproducible enough that another reviewer can locate the current body, native artifact, readback condition, mounted knowledge/evidence and open boundary without relying on producer memory.

Receipt body and artifact state must agree. If the receipt says a dimension is resolved but the current native artifact/readback no longer contains the reviewed relation, the receipt is stale even if its JSON remains structurally valid.

#### Current / supersession boundary for body and receipt evidence

Design-development body records and DQ receipts consume the existing R-A Current / Supersession and Authority Snapshot boundaries; they do not create their own Current registry or version ledger. The runtime resolves which body/native/readback relation is Current from the owner-native identity and authority binding, then uses the existing dependency/reopen graph to determine which design evidence must be revalidated.

When a materially changed successor native artifact or body becomes Current:

```text
Current successor resolved by owner
→ affected DD/body/receipt consumers identified
→ predecessor body + receipt preserved as historical provenance
→ current claim stops consuming predecessor receipt where the relation changed
→ successor body binds Current native/readback refs
→ R-I actual readback/review reruns affected scope
→ successor or revalidation receipt records the new decision
→ R-J may consume only applicable Current non-stale receipt evidence
```

Do **not** edit an old receipt merely to make its historical `stale` flag describe a later event. If the receipt was truthful for its original authority snapshot, artifact revision and review context, it stays immutable historical evidence. Later supersession is represented by the Current relation and successor/revalidation evidence, not by rewriting the predecessor receipt.

A new optional schema field, later schema version or richer runtime projection likewise does not retroactively upgrade an old receipt. Migration or revalidation creates successor evidence when required.

The receipt does not need to become a second Authority carrier merely to remain traceable. Where the receipt schema does not inline an authority fingerprint, the Current consumption context resolves the applicable R-A authority snapshot/fingerprint, source revision and layer/handoff identity externally. A later fingerprint mismatch blocks direct Current reuse and triggers affected-scope re-resolution; it does not authorize backfilling or rewriting the historical receipt.

`NEW NATIVE REVISION ≠ OLD DQ RECEIPT UPDATED`.

`SUPERSEDED DQ RECEIPT ≠ FALSE HISTORICAL EVIDENCE`.

`HISTORICAL RECEIPT IMMUTABLE ≠ CURRENT CLAIM ELIGIBLE`.

For `KEEP`, the receipt should identify why the strongest design decision is worth retaining, not merely state that checks passed. For `REVISE`, identify the dominant root cause and affected scope. For `REJECT`, identify the design move/candidate being discontinued and what evidence or contradiction supports that decision. For `HOLD`, identify the missing condition required before judgment can resume.

Do not use the receipt as a dumping ground for copied research, complete design rationale or duplicate professional-domain reports. Keep locators and bounded decision consequences in the receipt; keep canonical bodies and native professional content with their owners.

## 28｜Machine-verifiable vs Human Design Judgment

Machines may validate, as applicable:

- required fields exist;
- IDs and refs resolve;
- stale state is coherent;
- required readback conditions are declared;
- every `artifact_ref` used by a Design `KEEP` has a declared readback condition and every such declared condition is `PASS`;
- applicable comparison records contain at least two candidate refs, fixed comparison conditions and a bounded decision;
- Design DNA reference/version is current;
- no invalid `KEEP + stale=true` combination;
- required independent review record exists;
- DQ4/DQ5 declarations encode reviewer independence;
- a Design `KEEP` does not coexist with `coherence_state != KEEP` or an unresolved genericity attack;
- promoted artifact references actual native/derived identities;
- declared language/content/figure/state fields are structurally complete;
- prohibited authority-state combinations are absent.

Machines must not auto-certify:

- beauty;
- elegance;
- originality;
- professional aesthetic quality;
- cultural appropriateness;
- coherence;
- memorability;
- craft;
- design maturity DQ3–DQ5;
- Design KEEP.

Automated measurements may generate diagnostic signals only.

Machine-detectable signals can legitimately trigger human review. Examples include text overflow, missing refs, inconsistent units, duplicate IDs, contrast/geometry thresholds where a declared rule exists, missing state variants, changed hashes, or a receipt whose referenced artifact no longer matches the reviewed baseline.

The machine may report that a declared relation is absent or structurally inconsistent; it must not infer that the replacement relation is aesthetically or professionally better. Likewise, a human reviewer may judge composition or craft, but must not override a machine-detected factual contradiction, stale reference or missing mandatory evidence by aesthetic preference alone.

The boundary is cooperative:

```text
MACHINE
→ structural consistency / measurable diagnostic / stale detection

HUMAN PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT
→ meaning / hierarchy / coherence / distinctiveness / craft / trade-off

AUTHORITY OWNER
→ statutory / licensed / field / source truth where required
```

No one layer inherits the authority of another.

## 29｜Design-quality contribution to Promotion eligibility

This section defines the **R-D contribution and dependencies** needed before the Master Runtime / `R-J` may compile a Promotion-eligibility decision. It does not make R-D the owner of Professional, Integration, Artifact, Technical, Evidence, persistence or Promotion state, and it does not collapse those verdicts into Design KEEP.

For the design-quality contribution to be eligible for Promotion consideration, the following independent owner states must be resolvable at the intended claim ceiling:

1. Current Authority and Design Intelligence Packet remain valid;
2. all triggered professional process dependencies are current at the promoted claim ceiling;
3. the current design artifact/native source has been actually read back at the relevant size/state/medium;
4. applicable design-development dimensions have been resolved to the level required by the claim;
5. material Design DNA / Design Language decisions are current where those claims are made;
6. no hard Design FAIL remains in promoted scope;
7. Truth and Performance blockers do not contradict the promoted result;
8. applicable Cross-Disciplinary Integration interfaces remain current;
9. Artifact Review and specialized technical/evidence gates close as required;
10. an independent design reviewer supports the intended Design `KEEP` at the declared Design claim ceiling; this review does not authorize Promotion;
11. the current `DESIGN_QUALITY_DEVELOPMENT_RECEIPT` is not stale;
12. persistence/release gates pass where triggered.

Items outside R-D are consumed by reference as prerequisites; R-D must not reissue their PASS/FAIL states. Design KEEP remains a professional judgment over the Design Axis, not a machine state inferred from checklist completion and not a substitute for Master/R-J Promotion compilation.

Promotion is claim-scoped and non-transitive. A promoted design-quality result does not automatically promote:

- professional engineering/compliance state;
- field or construction state;
- Knowledge Integrity or Operational Eligibility state;
- another medium/state/viewport not actually read back;
- another project or future version;
- a reusable Design Language / Pattern / Method into canonical Knowledge.

The promoted object must therefore remain bound to the exact current authority/baseline, native artifact identities, relevant derived outputs, readback conditions, professional/integration dependencies and receipt version that support the claim.

Open items may coexist with promotion only when they are explicitly outside the promoted claim or allowed by the controlling professional/integration contract without contradicting an in-claim decision. “Will be resolved later” is not a valid boundary by itself.

If multiple applicable axes have different ceilings, the promoted statement must use the lowest ceiling relevant to the actual claim rather than borrowing stronger language from the best-resolved axis.

`DESIGN KEEP ≠ UNIVERSAL PASS`.

`PROMOTED CURRENT ≠ PERMANENTLY VALID`.

`PROJECT DESIGN AUTHORITY ≠ REUSABLE KNOWLEDGE AUTHORITY`.

## 31｜Skill, Tool and Native Output Binding

Professional Design Requirement determines what must be resolved. Skill/Capability Resolution determines who can execute it. Tool selection determines the runtime used to make it.

Canonical relation:

`Professional Stage → Professional Question / Decision Object → Knowledge Inputs → Operational Knowledge Mount → Required Capability Roles → Current Execution Owners / Skills → Native Outputs → Actual Readback → Independent Review → Stage Closure`.

`Tool / Adapter`, DD responsibilities, interfaces and claim-scope bindings remain subordinate to this professional-stage spine; required-output analysis may inform capability needs but does not reorder the canonical stage nodes.

Under `OLEANDER_CURRENT_ARCHITECTURE_MAP_v2.1`, this is the shared Design-side view of the existing `R-E → R-G → R-H → R-I` route. R-D may specify the design relation/readback requirement; it does not select a tool by taste or acquire execution authority merely because the design body mentions software.

### 31.1｜Routing inputs and outputs

Before capability routing, the current task should be able to resolve:

`decision object / claim ceiling / required native output / editable-source requirement / target readback / professional owner when triggered / locked variables / allowed mutation scope / material interfaces / delivery constraints`.

R-G then resolves the minimum sufficient **callable** capability/owner/adapter route. A route is acceptable only when it can produce the required semantic/native carrier without silently weakening the claim. Tool familiarity, local installation or fast generation is not a substitute for capability fit.

The routing output should be recoverable as:

`required output → selected execution owner/capability → selected tool/adapter → source input identity → expected native artifact → expected readback → fallback/hold condition`.

### 31.2｜Fallback and substitution rule

If the preferred tool or execution surface is unavailable, a fallback is valid only when it preserves the required semantics, editability/authority, precision, evidence/readback and interface obligations. Where equivalence cannot be demonstrated, return the existing capability/execution HOLD instead of silently downgrading the artifact.

Examples:

- a raster redraw is not an equivalent fallback for an editable/vector master when later controlled editing is in claim;
- a render is not a fallback for required CAD/BIM/native geometry;
- a static screenshot is not a fallback for interaction/runtime behavior that must be executed;
- a generic spreadsheet may be a valid fallback for a simple tabular calculation only when formulas, units, provenance and required checks remain inspectable;
- a different CAD/modeling tool may be acceptable when exchange/readback proves the controlled geometry and semantic requirements survive.

If a fallback changes the source-of-truth carrier or materially changes round-trip behavior, reopen the affected execution/readback dependency even when the visible output looks similar.

### 31.3｜Execution and readback boundary

R-H proves only that the authorized mutation/output occurred and preserves artifact identity/provenance. R-I proves only the applicable actual-readback/review claims. Neither step self-awards Design KEEP. When a body record or professional stage record names a native artifact, R-I must review the actual Current binding rather than accepting a producer narrative, stale screenshot or prior version by filename similarity.

Required separation:

`CAPABILITY ROUTE FOUND ≠ TOOL CALL SUCCEEDED ≠ NATIVE ARTIFACT VALID ≠ ACTUAL READBACK PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

### 31.4｜Skill reuse and local invention boundary

Use the Current Skill Resolver/live registry and existing installed Skills first. A missing execution route may justify a bounded capability gap or Evolution/Skill candidate; it does not justify creating a shadow Skill merely to finish one project. New local helpers remain project/runtime implementation until they pass the existing Skill lifecycle/evaluation/promotion route.

Rules:

- Professional Stage ≠ Skill;
- Skill ≠ Tool;
- Tool ≠ METHOD;
- Tool availability ≠ Tool callability;
- Tool execution ≠ Artifact quality;
- Artifact quality ≠ Design KEEP.

No software product is a default design method.

Reopen capability/tool routing when the required native output changes, a controlling interface changes format/precision, a tool/adapter becomes unavailable or non-callable, the editable source authority changes, a fallback loses material semantics, or actual readback shows that the chosen route cannot expose/maintain the required relation.

## 34｜Canonical execution summary

The shared **R-D design-development** relation is:

`Authority → Design Question → Knowledge / Evidence → Intent → Concept / Generative Principle → Experience / Human / Formal Development → Design Language / Style → Prototype / Compare → Detail / Craft → Adaptation / Meaning / Coherence → Native Execution → Multi-scale / Multi-state Actual Readback → Independent Design Review → R-D KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD`.

Promotion remains a separate cross-owner compilation:

`R-D Design verdict + Professional state + Integration state + Artifact state + Technical / Evidence state + persistence/sync prerequisites → Master Runtime / R-J Promotion eligibility → authorized human Promotion → R-K G9 return`.

The `+` relation above means **independent inputs to compilation**, not score averaging and not authority transfer between owners.

This line is a **dependency/decision cycle**, not a mandatory one-pass waterfall. The Current runtime may enter at the first unresolved dependency for an existing project, preserve already verified work, and reopen an earlier relation when later evidence invalidates it.

### 34.1｜Executable cycle

For a material design decision, the minimum operational cycle is:

1. **Resolve** — confirm Current authority, decision object, claim ceiling, locked/open variables and relevant Knowledge mounts.
2. **Frame** — state the design question, intended relation, success/failure criteria, affected DD responsibilities and required professional/interfaces.
3. **Develop** — create/compare the minimum material design alternatives or transformations needed to answer the question.
4. **Bind** — identify the required native artifact/source of truth, capability route and body/stage locator records.
5. **Execute** — make the authorized native change while preserving source identity/provenance and dependency boundaries.
6. **Read back** — inspect the actual artifact/experience at the required scales/states/media and reopen stale/mismatched locators.
7. **Review** — perform triggered Design, professional, Integration, Artifact, technical/evidence and independent reviews without averaging them into one score.
8. **Decide** — record `KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD` at the bounded Design claim ceiling plus root cause/open/reopen logic.
9. **Persist / Promote / Sync when triggered** — preserve production evidence, stop machines at eligibility, require authorized human Promotion and actual target readback.
10. **Return learning** — route real outcomes to project reopen, bounded G9 Knowledge candidate and/or Evolution candidate without automatic reusable promotion.

### 34.2｜Stop and reopen conditions

The cycle stops or holds when the next step would require pretending a missing authority, knowledge eligibility, professional input, native carrier, interface maturity, actual readback or independent judgment is already resolved. It reopens only the dependency scope affected by a material change or contradiction.

Examples:

- missing canonical source may HOLD a claim while unrelated design exploration continues at a lower ceiling;
- failed native write/readback reopens execution, not automatically the design hypothesis;
- failed Design review may reopen the design relation even when Artifact Review passes;
- changed professional interface may reopen downstream geometry/readback without erasing unrelated Design DNA evidence;
- a post-use failure may reopen the project immediately while reusable Knowledge validation remains a separate later route.

### 34.3｜Completion evidence

A complete implementation pass for a bounded design scope should leave recoverable evidence of the current authority/baseline, design question, mounted inputs, actual design development, native artifact, readback, triggered reviews, decision, open items, reopen triggers and current receipt/locator references. Completeness of that chain proves **traceable execution**, not Design excellence by itself.

`TRACEABLE EXECUTION ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

`PROCESS COMPLETE ≠ PROFESSIONAL PASS`.

`CURRENT RECEIPT ≠ PERMANENT VALIDITY`.

The governing principle is:

> **OLEANDER must not treat correct process, correct evidence, consistent components, attractive styling or technical completion as substitutes for developed design. A promotable design must demonstrate intentional relations, coherent form/system behavior, credible human and contextual response, resolved detail at the claimed scale, appropriate adaptation, and independent design judgment at the actual artifact or experience state.**

---

## 35｜Implementation acceptance test

This specification is considered correctly implemented in a project only when the project can answer, at the applicable claim level:

1. **Intent** — what is the design trying to change, for whom, and why?
2. **Concept** — what generative relation actually shapes the design?
3. **Experience** — how does the design unfold through use, time, movement or reading?
4. **Form** — what controls hierarchy, composition, scale, proportion, figure-ground or mass-void?
5. **Human relation** — how does body, behavior, cognition or use materially affect the result?
6. **Sensory relation** — which sensory carriers are intentional and consequential?
7. **Design language** — which relations make the project recognizable without reducing it to a style recipe?
8. **Craft** — do details, edges, transitions and micro-relations sustain the larger intent?
9. **Experiment** — what was learned by making, comparing or prototyping rather than only by describing?
10. **Adaptation** — what remains invariant and what legitimately changes across states/scales/media?
11. **Meaning / memory** — what should be understood, associated with or remembered, and through which carrier?
12. **Coherence** — do intent, function, form, material, detail, experience and expression reinforce one another?
13. **Truth** — which claims are factual, inferred, assumed, predicted, proposed or verified?
14. **Performance** — what actually works at the current professional/technical claim ceiling and what remains open?
15. **Readback** — has the current artifact/experience been inspected at its real target condition rather than inferred from source intent?
16. **Decision** — what is KEEP / REVISE / REJECT / HOLD, why, and what exact change reopens the decision?

If these questions cannot be answered for the dimensions materially claimed by the project, the work may remain a useful candidate or exploration, but it is not Design Quality & Design Development PASS for that scope.
