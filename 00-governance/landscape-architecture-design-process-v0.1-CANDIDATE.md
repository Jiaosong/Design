# OLEANDER Landscape Architecture Design Process v0.1 — CANDIDATE

**Status:** `CANDIDATE PROFESSIONAL DOMAIN PROCESS / R-E / NOT CURRENT / NO PROMOTION`

**Domain:** Landscape Architecture

**Process ID:** `OLEANDER-LANDSCAPE-ARCHITECTURE-DESIGN-PROCESS`

**Authority position:** subordinate to the OLEANDER Master Runtime, Current Knowledge Architecture, `professional-domain-process-contract-v1.0.md`, Cross-Disciplinary Integration, applicable project/client/jurisdiction authority, and the responsible landscape professional.

**Purpose:** provide a domain-authentic Landscape Architecture development process from scope/site authority through analysis, planning/concept, developed landscape systems, technical documentation, construction/establishment, handover/maintenance and in-use stewardship without copying Architecture ADD stages or treating landscape as decorative residual space.

**Candidate boundary:** this document is not yet a Current professional-process definition. The Current Architecture Map remains authoritative where it states `Landscape Architecture = CONTRACT_ENVELOPE_AVAILABLE / DOMAIN PROCESS OPEN`.

---

## 1｜Why this candidate exists

Current OLEANDER already has:
- a shared Professional Domain Process Contract;
- Architecture, Structural Engineering and Building Services / MEP Current processes;
- Architecture ADD-12 Landscape and External-Space Integration;
- Cross-Disciplinary Integration;
- Design Intelligence and shared DD;
- project site/landscape analyses such as C04 CH02;
- bounded planting-composition practice evidence.

What is missing is an authentic Landscape Architecture professional owner capable of distinguishing:

`SITE / LANDSCAPE DESIGN INTENT`

from:

`SURVEY / TOPOGRAPHY / GRADING / DRAINAGE / STORMWATER / SOIL / PLANTING / IRRIGATION / HARD LANDSCAPE / ACCESSIBILITY / CONSTRUCTION / ESTABLISHMENT / MAINTENANCE / STEWARDSHIP`.

Without a professional process, OLEANDER risks two opposite failures:

1. landscape is reduced to planting graphics and atmosphere;
2. Architecture or Civil/MEP silently absorbs landscape responsibilities without an explicit domain owner.

This candidate therefore defines a professional envelope while preserving jurisdiction-specific responsibility boundaries.

## 5｜Trigger contract

### Project-flow trigger

Activate when a project makes a material landscape/site-system claim requiring professional landscape development, native outputs or professional judgment.

### Typical design questions

- What site opportunities/constraints materially govern the landscape?
- How should movement, landform, water, vegetation, use and maintenance form one landscape system?
- Which landscape concept should proceed?
- How do grading/drainage/planting/hardscape systems become spatially coordinated?
- Is the technical landscape information sufficient to build, price and coordinate?
- Is construction consistent with landscape intent and technical information?
- Has planting/soil/irrigation/landscape establishment reached the required handover condition?
- What does seasonal/in-use observation require the design or management plan to change?

### Not triggered when

- planting appears only as non-authoritative render dressing;
- external space is outside the claim;
- a diagram is purely geographic/contextual analysis without landscape-design claim;
- Architecture is only acknowledging a future landscape interface;
- the task is a graphic planting composition practice with explicit exclusion of horticultural/site truth.

## 6｜Claim-ceiling rule

The effective Landscape Architecture claim ceiling is the lowest current ceiling across:

`SITE/SURVEY AUTHORITY × PROFESSIONAL STAGE EVIDENCE × TECHNICAL THREAD EVIDENCE × INTERFACE MATURITY × ACTUAL READBACK × INDEPENDENT REVIEW × STATUTORY/FIELD AUTHORITY`.

A sophisticated landscape plan cannot outrun weak site/survey/hydrology/soil/field evidence.

## 7｜Continuous technical threads

The process is stage-based for orchestration but landscape technical truth is continuous. Every stage must declare which threads are active, deferred, external or out of scope.

### LT-01 Site / Survey / Existing-Condition Authority

Track:
- site boundary;
- datum / coordinate / vertical datum;
- topographic survey;
- utilities;
- existing structures/hardscape;
- vegetation/tree authority;
- water features;
- property/easement/access constraints;
- existing drainage;
- photographic/field observation limits;
- changed conditions.

`BASE MAP EXISTS ≠ SURVEY AUTHORITY`.

### LT-02 Landform / Grading / Earthwork

Track as applicable:
- existing/proposed contours;

- cut/fill logic;
- earthwork balance assumptions;
- tie-ins;

- accessible slope implications;

## 7A｜Cross-cutting practitioner control objects

These are not extra lifecycle stages. They are live professional work objects that carry landscape decisions, release conditions and change propagation across LA-DP0…7.

### 7A.1 Site / Base Authority & Survey Change Register

Record:
- base/survey source, revision, CRS/datum where relevant;
- site boundary and level authority;
- received date / superseded date;
- known gaps;
- consuming drawings/models;
- affected landscape decisions when the base changes;
- reopen scope.

A new topographic survey may invalidate grading, drainage, accessible-route and planting/root-zone decisions simultaneously.

### 7A.2 Grading / Drainage Interface Schedule

For each material zone/edge/threshold record:
- existing/proposed level authority;
- high/low points;
- slope direction;
- drain/inlet/outfall/overflow relation;
- building/road/utility threshold;
- accessible-route consequence;
- Civil/Hydraulic owner where applicable;
- current disposition;
- verification/readback ref.

`SPOT LEVELS PRESENT ≠ DRAINAGE CLOSED`.

### 7A.3 Earthwork / Soil Movement Schedule

Track:
- cut/fill or import/export basis;
- topsoil/growing-media strip/store/reuse;
- soil protection zones;
- compaction/contamination constraints;
- stockpile/haul/temporary-access assumptions;
- target soil profile/volume;
- owner and evidence source;
- field verification / change state.

Do not separate planting success from the soil/earthwork sequence that enables it.

### 7A.4 Retained Tree / Vegetation Protection Execution Register

Track:
- retained/protected element ID;
- authority/source;
- root/protection zone;
- access/excavation/service conflicts;
- protection method;
- temporary works/site logistics interface;
- inspection/hold point;
- changed-condition disposition;
- specialist owner where required.

A tree symbol on a plan is not a protection execution record.

### 7A.5 Planting / Soil Establishment & Replacement Register

Track by planting system/zone:
- species/community or role authority;
- nursery/provenance/availability issue;
- planting density/size/mature relation;
- soil/root-volume dependency;
- establishment irrigation;
- defects/replacement criteria;
- seasonal verification;
- maintenance owner;
- current establishment state.

### 7A.6 Irrigation / Water / Control Interface Register

Track:
- water source/quality authority;
- hydro-zone;
- establishment vs long-term demand;
- control/sensor strategy;
- point of connection;
- MEP/irrigation-specialist interface;
- backflow/water-safety boundary;
- access/winterisation/maintenance;
- test/commissioning or field-readback requirement.

### 7A.7 Material / Submittal / Sample / Mockup / Substitution Release Register

For consequential paving, edging, walls, soil/media, furniture, planting or system products record:
- design role / critical attributes;
- specified/reference identity;
- submitted identity/revision;
- sample/mockup requirement;
- visual + technical acceptance criteria;
- interfaces/tolerances;
- substitution impact;
- professional disposition;
- release state;
- affected drawing/spec/schedule refs.

`SIMILAR APPEARANCE ≠ EQUIVALENT LANDSCAPE PERFORMANCE`.

### 7A.8 Field Change / RFI / Nonconformity Register

Record:
- exact location/object;
- observed/current condition;
- issued-design relation;
- RFI/change/nonconformity;
- affected grading/drainage/planting/material/access/maintenance threads;
- owner;
- disposition;
- revised artifact refs;
- field readback / closure state.

### 7A.9 Maintenance / Operations Access Schedule

For each maintained landscape system record:
- task;
- frequency/season;
- access route/clearance;
- plant/equipment/tool requirement;
- water/power/shutdown need;
- safety/closure/degraded-state consequence;
- replacement strategy;
- owner/capability;
- design implication.

`MAINTENANCE PLAN TEXT ≠ MAINTAINABLE GEOMETRY`.

# 8｜LA-DP0 — Scope, Authority & Landscape Brief

- landscape appointment/scope;
- site boundary/evidence;
- known survey/existing-condition state;
- jurisdiction/permit route;
- project use and operations;
- related Architecture/Civil/etc. context.

### Required work
- resolve landscape scope and excluded scope;
- identify responsible professionals and authority gaps;
- define site/survey/data requirements;
- identify stakeholder/engagement requirement;
- identify critical technical threads;
- declare field-observation state;
- establish claim ceiling.

### Native outputs
- `LANDSCAPE_SCOPE_AUTHORITY_REGISTER`;
- `SITE_INFORMATION_REQUIREMENT_REGISTER`;
- initial landscape brief;
- interface owner map;
- field/evidence boundary note.

### DD mapping
Primarily DD-01 Intent, DD-12 Integration.

### Required readback
Read the actual source/site-information set against the declared landscape claim.

### Does not prove
- survey accuracy;
- existing drainage;
- soil suitability;
- species suitability;
- grading/drainage compliance;
- field condition;
- design quality.

# 9｜LA-DP1 — Inventory, Analysis & Site-System Diagnosis

**Professional question:** What existing site/context systems, opportunities, constraints and uncertainties materially govern landscape decisions?

### Required analysis as applicable
- landform/topography;
- hydrology/drainage;
- soils;
- vegetation/trees;
- ecology/habitat;
- climate/microclimate;
- access/circulation;
- utilities/services;
- views/visual context;
- use/behavior;
- heritage/cultural landscape;
- existing materials/assets;
- maintenance/operations;
- hazards;
- jurisdictional constraints.

### Evidence discipline

For each analysis layer:
`SOURCE → OBSERVED / DOCUMENTED CONDITION → INTERPRETATION → DESIGN CONSEQUENCE → UNCERTAINTY / FIELD NEED`.

Do not infer missing topography, soil, ecology or hydrology from photographs.

### Native outputs
- site/base authority map;
- site systems inventory;
- constraints/opportunities map;
- hydrology/drainage concept baseline where evidence allows;
- existing vegetation/tree/soil evidence register;
- user/activity/operation analysis where relevant;
- `LANDSCAPE_SITE_ANALYSIS_RECEIPT`.

### DD mapping
DD-01, DD-03, DD-05, DD-06, DD-11, DD-12 as applicable.

### Required readback
- cross-layer contradiction check;
- scale/CRS/datum check where spatial data is used;
- source-vs-inference audit;
- field evidence gap audit.

### Exit
Material site-system relations are explicit enough to generate landscape options at the current claim ceiling.

### Does not prove
- unseen field condition;
- detailed hydrologic performance;
- soil laboratory properties;
- ecological survey completeness;
- tree condition unless separately assessed;
- statutory acceptance.

# 10｜LA-DP2 — Planning & Concept Landscape Design

**Professional question:** Which landscape concept coherently organizes movement, use, landform, water, vegetation, hardscape, identity and stewardship while respecting current site authority?

### Required work
- generate materially different landscape concepts where alternatives exist;
- organize program/use zones and spatial sequences;
- establish primary circulation/access;
- establish landform/grading intent;
- establish water/stormwater landscape intent;
- establish planting/ecological structure;
- establish hardscape/material families;
- establish landscape structures/furnishings scope;
- establish seasonal/maintenance concept;
- identify grading/drainage/civil/structural feasibility dependencies;
- compare options under consistent evidence and claim ceilings.

### Native outputs
- `LANDSCAPE_CONCEPT_OPTION_SET`;
- concept/site plan(s);
- key sections;
- circulation/use diagrams;
- landform/water/planting system diagrams;
- concept material/planting palette by role, not merely species catalog;
- initial maintenance/establishment concept;
- concept selection record.

### DD mapping
DD-02, DD-03, DD-04, DD-05, DD-06, DD-07, DD-10, DD-11, DD-12.

### Failure attacks
- same layout with different planting colors called alternatives;
- water element drawn without catchment/outfall/maintenance consequence;
- planting concept detached from soil/water/light/maintenance;
- hardscape geometry detached from grading/drainage/access;
- landscape concept treats architecture/civil interfaces as later problems;
- one hero perspective substitutes for site-system concept.

### Exit
A concept can be carried forward at bounded professional concept ceiling, with technical unknowns and interfaces explicit.

### Does not prove
- detailed grading;
- stormwater capacity;
- species establishment;
- final material build-up;
- detailed accessibility;
- construction readiness.

# 13｜LA-DP5 — Procurement, Construction Administration & Field Change

**Professional question:** Is landscape design intent and technical information being correctly interpreted, substituted, installed and changed during procurement/construction within the appointed responsibility?

### Required work as appointed
- respond to tender/bid queries;
- review substitutions;
- review submittals/shop drawings;
- review material/plant samples;
- mockup review;
- RFI response;
- site observation;
- grading/level/drainage field issue review;
- planting/soil/irrigation installation observation;
- nonconformity/change review;
- design change disposition;
- cost/value-engineering consequence review;
- update affected design/interface evidence.

### Field evidence rule

`SITE OBSERVATION PROVES ONLY OBSERVED LOCATION / TIME / SCOPE`.

Do not extrapolate one observed area to universal construction conformity.

### Substitution rule

For material/plant/system substitution:
`PROPOSED SUBSTITUTE → ORIGINAL DESIGN ROLE / PERFORMANCE → EQUIVALENCE / DIFFERENCE → INTERFACE IMPACT → MAINTENANCE / SEASONAL IMPACT → PROFESSIONAL DISPOSITION → REVISED DOCUMENT OR REJECT/HOLD`.

“Similar appearance” is not sufficient equivalence.

### Native outputs
- submittal/sample/mockup review records;
- site observation records;
- RFI/change records;
- nonconformity/design disposition;
- updated drawings/details/specifications where required;
- construction-stage landscape receipt.

### Does not prove
- full inspection coverage;
- concealed work;
- universal contractor conformity;
- final plant establishment;
- as-built survey authority.

## 18｜Hard HOLD / mandatory REVISE conditions

A professional landscape claim cannot close when materially applicable and unresolved:

- base/survey authority is insufficient for the claimed dimensional/site result;
- proposed grading creates unresolved drainage/accessible-route contradiction;
- stormwater strategy lacks outfall/overflow/maintenance relation;
- planting relies on unsourced soil/light/moisture/maintenance assumptions;
- critical tree/vegetation condition is inferred rather than assessed;
- planting/soil/root-volume geometry is contradictory;
- irrigation source/quality/operation is unresolved where irrigation is essential;
- hardscape detail contradicts drainage/movement/accessibility;
- landscape structures exceed Landscape scope without Structural owner;
- construction docs conflict across plan/section/detail/spec/schedule;
- field substitution changes design role/performance without professional disposition;
- project claims establishment success from installation alone;
- old review remains “PASS” after material grading/drainage/planting/interface change;
- render/presentation/plant-symbol richness is used as professional landscape PASS.
