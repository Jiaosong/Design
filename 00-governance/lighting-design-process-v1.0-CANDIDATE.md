## 0｜Professional intent

Develop daylight and electric-light environments as task, spatial, visual, control and maintenance systems tied to measurable/observable conditions rather than fixture styling or rendered brightness.

The document is intentionally deeper than a stage checklist. A stage only closes when the underlying professional relation is actually developed, represented in an appropriate native carrier, read back, and bounded by explicit failure/reopen conditions.

`PROCESS DOCUMENTED ≠ PROFESSIONAL DESIGN DEVELOPED`.

`NATIVE ARTIFACT EXISTS ≠ ACTUAL CONDITION READ BACK ≠ PROFESSIONAL PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

### In scope
- visual-task and user/context lighting brief;
- daylight/electric-light concept and spatial hierarchy;
- photometric/brightness/glare/color-quality design basis;
- luminaire/source/optics/surface interaction;
- architectural/interior/landscape integration and detailing;
- controls, scenes, daylight response and user interaction;
- exterior/night/ecology/wayfinding interfaces;
- calculation, mockup, sample, aiming and commissioning readback;
- maintenance, replacement, post-occupancy tuning and change;

### Explicitly outside this owner's authority
- electrical system engineering approval or circuit protection design;
- emergency-lighting/fire/statutory approval;
- energy-code certification;
- accessibility or health/wellness certification;
- universal illuminance/CCT/CRI/UGR or spectral thresholds detached from Current source;
- render brightness as photometric proof;

Where another professional/statutory owner controls a criterion, this process records the shared variable, geometry/material/design consequence, required maturity and current evidence. It does not copy or reissue that owner's PASS.

## 1｜Evidence and decision discipline

For every material professional decision, preserve:

`QUESTION / USE / FAILURE RISK → CURRENT SOURCE + ASSUMPTIONS → REAL ALTERNATIVES OR DESIGN MOVE → NATIVE GEOMETRY / MATERIAL / CALCULATION / SAMPLE / MOCKUP → ACTUAL READBACK → STRONGEST CONTRADICTION → BOUNDED VERDICT → HANDOFF → REOPEN TRIGGER`.

Rules:
- quantitative thresholds must carry Current source, unit/metric, applicability and tolerance/condition; this process does not invent universal numbers.
- manufacturer/vendor data remains product/configuration evidence, not independent proof of installed/project performance.
- rendered or diagrammatic appearance cannot substitute for geometry, material, physical, field, calculation or specialist evidence when those are in claim.
- one observed location, mockup, season, user condition or project does not automatically represent unobserved work, future performance or universal practice.
- project observations remain observation until causal explanation is supported; reusable learning routes only as a bounded G9/R-B candidate with transfer boundary and counterevidence.
- R-F remains the actual interface maturity/disposition owner; this domain records stage-side requirements only.
- professional development can remain bounded while specialist/statutory items are OPEN only if those OPEN items do not contradict the current claim ceiling.

## 2｜Deep professional decision body

The following sections are the professional substance. They are not replaced by stage completion, matrix coverage or a receipt.

### 2.1｜Visual task, user adaptation and luminous-environment brief

- Start with what must be seen, recognized, compared, read, manipulated, navigated or perceived—and by whom, at what distance, orientation, duration and state—before selecting fixtures.
- Separate task visibility, spatial orientation, facial/social perception, ambience, display/art emphasis, vertical brightness, safety/wayfinding and identity; one average horizontal illuminance cannot represent all purposes.
- Identify adaptation context and brightness sequence. A space can meet a local task criterion yet feel visually difficult because approach/adjacent zones create extreme adaptation or reflected glare.
- Record occupancy schedules, daylight availability, age/visual capability considerations where material, control ownership and critical degraded/night states.
- For screens/AV/projected images, coordinate ambient light, reflections, contrast, viewing angle and control scenes instead of increasing display brightness as the default repair.
- For hospitality/exhibition/heritage or low-light environments, state which visual tasks remain mandatory and which darkness/contrast effects are deliberate; atmosphere cannot erase safety/task requirements.
- Any quantitative target must retain its Current source, metric, plane/orientation, condition, maintenance basis and tolerance/applicability rather than becoming a generic project-wide number.

**Decision attack:** if the current visual task, user adaptation and luminous-environment brief can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.2｜Daylight as geometry, time and material

- Treat daylight as a time-varying source shaped by orientation, sky/climate, aperture, shading, depth, surface reflectance/transmittance and occupancy—not as a render effect.
- Map useful daylight and risk together: direct sun, glare, overheating interface, screen/display conflict, fading/exposure, privacy, view and control behavior where relevant.
- Coordinate aperture/shading geometry with Architecture/Envelope; Lighting may test luminous consequence but does not own envelope thermal/weather performance.
- Use daylight simulation only with declared weather/sky/model/material assumptions and correct geometry revision; a false material reflectance or missing neighboring obstruction can invalidate result.
- Assess temporal conditions appropriate to the claim rather than one favorable time/day image. Seasonal/time-state selection must follow the decision risk.
- Daylight controls and blinds are part of the operating system. If the design depends on occupants using shading in a specific way, record the control assumption and failure/degraded state.
- Daylight metrics/thresholds come from Current project/standard authority; this process does not install one universal daylight factor, autonomy or glare value.
- When simulation drives a decision, freeze a daylight-model configuration record: location/time convention; weather/sky basis; north/orientation; geometry revision including reveals, frames, shading and neighboring obstructions; glazing visible-transmission/optical model; surface reflectance/specularity assumptions; blind/shade state; sensor grid or eye/view positions; occupied/task zone; calculation engine/version and settings that can change the result.
- Do not mix annual climate-based evidence with one critical date/time/sky claim. State which temporal model answers which risk, and separately test direct-sun or glare-critical conditions when an annual summary can conceal them.
- Run sensitivity checks on uncertain inputs that could reverse the design decision—such as obstruction, glazing/transmission basis, surface reflectance, shade position or geometry—and retain the input range plus consequence rather than one apparently precise output.
- Reopen only the affected daylight conclusions when a controlling geometry/material/weather-sky/control/sensor definition changes.

**Decision attack:** if the current daylight as geometry, time and material can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.3｜Luminous hierarchy, brightness and spatial composition

- Design the relation among luminous surfaces, task planes, people, circulation, vertical boundaries, ceiling, focal objects and darkness rather than distributing fixtures uniformly.
- Use luminance/brightness reasoning where spatial perception, glare, orientation or adaptation depends on what the eye sees; horizontal lux alone may miss a bright source, dark face or luminous wall condition.
- Establish which surfaces should carry ambient brightness, which should remain quiet, and which objects/edges need emphasis. This is a design-language relation that should survive fixture substitutions.
- Check the source-path-surface-viewer chain. A narrow beam can create desired emphasis but also specular reflection, facial shadow, hotspot, scallop or visible source glare depending on geometry/material.
- Vertical and facial light may matter for social interaction, wayfinding, security or camera/recording; treat it as a task/context criterion rather than a decorative fill.
- When recognition, social interaction, reception/security, camera capture or wayfinding depends on faces/vertical targets, define the actual observer–subject relation: approach direction, standing/seated state, face orientation range, background brightness, occlusion and scene state. Review vertical/cylindrical incident light and/or facial luminance/modeling only where they answer that task; one average vertical value is not facial-quality proof. Attack directional failures such as eye-socket/under-brow shadow, top-light dominance, facial flattening, bright-background silhouette and source glare at representative positions. Lighting owns luminous/task consequence; Security/AV/Accessibility owners retain recognition/camera/compliance acceptance where applicable;
- Darkness is a design resource only when users can still interpret boundaries, controls, steps, faces or hazards required by the task and when ecology/safety requirements permit it.
- Rendered contrast must not self-prove actual brightness hierarchy unless the rendering is tied to a calibrated/traceable model appropriate to the claim.

**Decision attack:** if the current luminous hierarchy, brightness and spatial composition can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.4｜Photometric model, metrics, assumptions and uncertainty

- Maintain exact luminaire photometric file/revision, lamp/source configuration, output factor, mounting/aiming, geometry, surface reflectance/transmittance and calculation grid/plane definitions when quantitative analysis is in claim.
- Distinguish maintained versus initial performance where the governing criterion requires it; document the maintenance/depreciation basis instead of treating catalog initial output as lifetime condition.
- Metrics such as illuminance, luminance, uniformity, glare indices, vertical/cylindrical values, daylight or spectral measures are selected because they answer a defined visual/performance question—not because software reports them.
- For every illuminance result, identify the receiving plane/normal, task relation and operating state. For every luminance result, identify the source or reflecting surface, viewing direction, observer position and scene state. A horizontal incident-light value cannot substitute for the directional brightness relation seen by the eye.
- Use illuminance to test incident-light delivery and luminance to test visible source/surface hierarchy, adaptation, source brightness and reflection where those are the actual problem; record both when surface reflectance/geometry couples them.
- Treat perceived brightness as a visual finding, not a synonym for luminance. Camera exposure, tone mapping and display brightness can change appearance without changing the physical scene.
- When ratios/contrast are used, name the compared surfaces/planes, viewer/state and why the relation matters; an unnamed ratio is not an acceptance criterion.
- A compliance-colored calculation plot is not enough. Inspect why the extrema occur, whether the calculation surface represents the task, and whether the geometry/material assumptions match Current design.
- Use point/line/image-based analyses where geometry demands them; coarse grids can hide local glare, scallop, dark thresholds or display reflections.
- Record uncertainty from photometric data quality, tolerances, dimming, voltage/driver behavior, surface properties, site aiming and dirt/depreciation where material to the claim.
- If site measurement materially disagrees with calculation, reopen model/input/installation before declaring the field result wrong or adjusting targets post hoc.

**Decision attack:** if the current photometric model, metrics, assumptions and uncertainty can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.5｜Glare, reflection, contrast and visual comfort

- Identify potential glare sources by viewer position and task: direct visible sources, windows/skylights, reflected highlights, glossy screens, polished floors/worktops, bright exterior views and specular art/display surfaces.
- Control strategies can include source shielding/cut-off, position/aim, aperture/shading, surface finish, luminance balance, diffusion, scene/output and viewer/task relocation; no one metric captures every glare mechanism.
- Evaluate critical viewing directions, not only room average. A luminaire acceptable from one axis may be uncomfortable from a seated/reclined/stair approach or screen-facing position.
- Check reflections on screens, glass, art, signage and polished surfaces using real geometry; simply reducing total light may damage task visibility without removing the reflected source.
- Classify the failure mechanism before repair: direct source glare; daylight/window glare; reflected-source glare; veiling reflection that reduces task contrast; specular hotspot; or adaptation difficulty caused by an adjacent bright field. Different mechanisms require different geometry/control responses.
- For reflection-sensitive tasks, trace the actual source → reflective surface → viewer path at critical eye positions and movement states. Preserve source position/size, surface orientation/gloss, viewer direction and task/screen plane; output reduction alone does not close a geometric reflection path.
- Any glare/image metric is valid only for its declared viewpoint, field of view, scene/exposure/model method and application. Pair metric output with underlying source/surface/view geometry and review plausible off-axis positions.
- Use mockup/site readback for glossy, mirrored, glazed, screen or art conditions when microfinish or observer movement can defeat simplified model assumptions.
- High contrast can support drama/orientation but can also create adaptation difficulty and lost detail. State the intended perceptual relation and the tasks that must survive it.
- Glare metrics/thresholds remain source/standard/application-specific. Use them when applicable, but retain qualitative/visual geometry review and mockup/site evidence for conditions the metric does not cover.
- User complaint or squint/avoidance is observed evidence requiring investigation; it does not by itself identify the causal source without controlled review.

**Decision attack:** if the current glare, reflection, contrast and visual comfort can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.6｜Color quality, spectrum, material and appearance

- Specify color quality from the visual/material task: skin tones, art, food, textiles, retail/product discrimination, heritage, medical/technical discrimination or general ambience may require different evidence.
- Do not use CCT as a proxy for spectral quality or color rendering. CCT describes chromatic appearance of the source, not fidelity/preference, and neither automatically predicts material appearance.
- Use CRI/TM-30 or other metrics only when required/appropriate to the Current project/source and interpret their scope; a single summary number should not hide critical saturated color or material behavior.
- Where spectral sensitivity, artwork conservation, ecology, photography/camera, circadian/wellness or specialist process is in claim, route to the appropriate current evidence/owner rather than importing generic spectral recipes.
- For light-sensitive art, collections, archives, displays or finishes, translate Conservation/curatorial evidence into a cumulative exposure relation rather than checking one instantaneous lighting state. Bind the exact object/material sensitivity classification and owner-issued exposure basis to daylight contribution, electric-light distribution/aim/output, operating duration/schedule, scene/control state, temporary/event/changeover conditions and any monitoring/logging dependency needed by the project. Combine daylight and electric-light contribution where the controlling Conservation basis requires it, and reopen the lighting response when object sensitivity, display duration/location, aperture/shading state, source spectrum/output or operating schedule changes. Lighting owns luminous source/time/control consequence; Conservation/curatorial authority retains sensitivity classification, exposure limit/budget and acceptance, while Envelope/Controls/MEP retain their system truth.
- Review actual material samples under candidate sources when color/finish choice is material; metamerism, fluorescence, gloss and texture can change appearance beyond screen/render prediction.
- Coordinate decorative RGB/dynamic color systems with white-light task requirements, controls and content/brand authority; dynamic color capability does not justify constant animation.
- Color consistency/tolerance across luminaires, dimming levels, batches and replacement stock is a procurement/maintenance issue when visible variation would damage the design.
- Separate chromatic appearance, color-rendering behavior, spectral distribution and consistency over drive level/batch/time. Similar CCT or nominal source labels do not establish equivalent spectral/material appearance.
- Where color discrimination is material, identify the critical materials/colors/skin/product/art task and compare representative samples under candidate sources, relevant dimmed scenes and daylight/electric-light mixtures. Record metameric mismatches rather than averaging them into one summary score.
- If dimming, tunable-white or color-changing operation is in claim, verify whether chromaticity/spectrum/color rendering shifts with output and whether scene transitions preserve the intended material hierarchy.
- For procurement/replacement, define Current-project-source-backed equivalence attributes for color/spectral behavior, batch consistency and dimmed-state behavior; nominal wattage/CCT alone cannot support substitution.
- Camera appearance, ecology, conservation, circadian or photobiological claims remain separate specialist evidence routes when triggered.

**Decision attack:** if the current color quality, spectrum, material and appearance can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.7｜Luminaire, source, optics and mounting selection

- Select luminaire/source/optics from required distribution, shielding, output/control, size, mounting, thermal/environment, access, appearance and maintenance—not product image alone.
- Read full photometric distribution and cut-off/shielding behavior against actual mounting height, spacing, surface distance and viewer positions. Beam angle labels alone are insufficient.
- For each selected optic, preserve the photometric coordinate/orientation convention, distribution symmetry/asymmetry, peak-intensity direction, shielding/cut-off behavior, accessories and the output state represented by the photometric data. A photometric file used with the wrong rotation/tilt reference can invalidate an otherwise correct calculation.
- Define aiming as geometry, not prose: luminaire ID and mounting point; target point/plane or surface zone; reference datum; pan/tilt/rotation relation; optic/accessory; scene/output state; and any project-source-backed tolerance or lock-off requirement.
- Check beam overlap, scallop, wallwash/graze continuity, focal-object spill and source visibility in the actual section/elevation, including tolerances that can expose trim/source or move the beam off its intended surface.
- A substitution with similar lumens or nominal beam angle remains `REVISE` until its full distribution, shielding, optical accessories and actual aiming consequences are shown equivalent for the current geometry.
- Coordinate source/driver/dimming/control compatibility and minimum/maximum output behavior with electrical/controls owners; Lighting does not own circuit protection or power-system approval.
- Check visible aperture and source brightness, trim/recess detail, ceiling thickness, insulation/air/plenum/fire/acoustic conditions, access for driver replacement and tolerance around trimless integration.
- For wallwash/grazing, coordinate setback/spacing/optics with wall plane, texture and workmanship; grazing can reveal defects as readily as material richness.
- For exterior/ground/recessed luminaires, consider drainage, sealing, corrosion/environment, impact/load, glare, vegetation growth, snow/debris and maintenance access through the proper technical evidence.
- A luminaire substitution reopens photometry, dimensions, visual appearance, controls, color quality, thermal/environment, maintenance and architectural integration when any controlling attribute changes.

**Decision attack:** if the current luminaire, source, optics and mounting selection can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.8｜Architectural, interior and landscape integration

- Coordinate luminaires with ceiling/wall/floor modules, joints, structure, services, sprinklers/detectors, diffusers, signage, acoustic treatment, curtains, furniture, art, planting and access panels before final lock.
- Lighting layout should express spatial hierarchy rather than treating remaining ceiling area as a device grid. Establish alignment/priority rules and documented exceptions.
- Hidden/recessed/cove details require source visibility control, reflector/surface behavior, ventilation/thermal/access, tolerances and continuity; a render can conceal gaps and bright-source views.
- At joinery/display/furniture lighting, resolve cable/driver location, heat, glare, touch, replacement, dimming and service without compromising the furniture function.
- Landscape integration must consider mature vegetation, irrigation, roots, mowing, public access and night ecology. Fixture positions valid at planting may fail after canopy growth.
- Façade lighting requires envelope/fixing/weatherproofing/maintenance authority and contextual spill/glare consideration; Lighting owns luminous intent, not facade structural/weather proof.
- Any geometry/material change that changes source-to-surface/viewer relation reopens affected lighting analysis and design review.

**Decision attack:** if the current architectural, interior and landscape integration can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.9｜Controls, scenes, daylight response and user interaction

- Define control intent by space/use/time state: occupancy, vacancy, cleaning, presentation, AV, event, night, daylight response, after-hours, emergency interface and manual override as applicable.
- Each scene should state which circuits/groups/loads change, target relation or output, transition behavior, trigger, user authority, feedback and recovery/manual override—not just a scene name.
- Sensor position/field-of-view, zoning and delay/transition can materially affect user experience and energy; controls must be coordinated with MEP/controls and tested in the actual space.
- Daylight-responsive control needs sensor/input logic and limits that preserve spatial hierarchy and user trust; visible hunting/flicker or unexpected dimming is a design defect even if energy logic is nominally active.
- Where dimming, driver modulation, rapid scene change, cameras/displays or visually sensitive tasks are material, treat temporal behavior as part of luminous quality rather than only a controls/electrical property. Read low-end dimming for stable output, dropout/pop-on, visible stepping, hunting, color shift and recovery; test representative scene transitions and interaction with occupancy/daylight commands.
- For camera/recording/display contexts, review banding/rolling-shutter interaction, exposure shifts, reflected-source paths and screen/projector contrast under the actual control/output state instead of assuming a visually acceptable scene records acceptably. Use Current application/source criteria where quantitative temporal metrics are required. Lighting owns visual/task/camera consequence; Electrical/Controls own driver/control implementation and specialist AV/Broadcast owners retain image-system acceptance.
- User interfaces should make relevant state and consequence understandable. A wall panel/app with ambiguous scene names or hidden overrides can make a well-designed lighting system unusable.
- Reduced/night/security modes should be treated as real lighting states, not lower percentages applied uniformly to every group.
- Control sequence execution and electrical/BMS correctness remain with their technical owners; Lighting defines/reads the luminous behavior and user consequence at the shared interface.
- Express each consequential scene as a state transition rather than a label: initiating input/condition → prerequisites → priority/arbitration → affected groups → target luminous relation/output → transition/ramp → hold/delay/timeout → feedback/proof → manual override → fail/degraded state → reset/recovery. Simultaneous inputs and power/network restart behavior must not be left implicit when they can change the user-visible result.
- For daylight response, bind sensor location/field of view, controlled daylight zone, blind/shade interaction where relevant, minimum/maximum commanded lighting state, override behavior and anti-hunting/recovery logic to the responsible controls owner.
- When daylight performance depends on blinds/shades, treat shade position and authority as an explicit coupled state with electric lighting. Define relevant manual/automatic inputs, priority/arbitration, occupied/unoccupied behavior, glare/direct-sun trigger intent, electric-light response, user override, hold/hysteresis/recovery, after-hours state and loss/restart behavior.
- Attack conflicting commands: a user opens a shade while glare control closes it; BMS setback disables the intended state; shade motion changes sensor reading and drives hunting; an emergency/shared condition requires a state that normal daylight logic must not inhibit. Preserve luminous consequence and handoff without Lighting taking motor, BMS, fire-smoke, envelope or electrical approval. Architecture/Envelope/MEP/Controls/Fire retain owner-native design and R-F retains interface maturity/disposition.
- Map the emergency-lighting interface without taking Fire/Electrical authority: identify which luminaires/groups/controls have emergency or shared roles as defined by the responsible owner; what loss-of-normal-power/emergency command must override or bypass; which normal scenes/dimming/daylight logic must not inhibit that response; and what restoration/reset state returns afterward.
- Lighting readback may verify that the required emergency command produces the intended shared luminous/interface state and that normal control does not mask it; emergency illuminance, duration, circuit, battery, statutory and fire-compliance PASS remain with the responsible owner.
- Reopen this interface on escape-route/occupancy/emergency-system topology, driver/control/substitution or backup-source change.

**Decision attack:** if the current controls, scenes, daylight response and user interaction can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.10｜Exterior, night environment, ecology and context

- Begin with night tasks and contextual darkness/brightness rather than illuminating every daytime design element.
- Control spill, upward light, glare and source visibility through optics/aiming/mounting/scene strategy appropriate to Current ecological/context/authority requirements; no universal external cut-off number is installed here.
- Check adaptation between interior/exterior zones, arrival/drop-off, paths, stairs/ramps, parking/service, landscape edges and destination recognition.
- Coordinate façade/landscape lighting with neighbors, bedrooms/sensitive receptors, road users, water reflections and ecological corridors where relevant.
- Treat spill/light-trespass as a `source → surface/path → receptor` relation, not a fixture cut-off label. For material boundaries identify direct source view, illuminated façade/ground/water reflection, receiving window/vertical plane/view direction, cumulative contribution from several luminaires, wet/leaf-on-off/temporary-event states and the control/curfew scene governing exposure. Test the receiving condition at the actual sensitive receptor and boundary geometry using Current planning/ecology/owner criteria where quantitative limits apply. A compliant luminaire or correct hero-view aim does not prove acceptable off-site exposure. Lighting owns luminous geometry/aim/scene consequence; Planning/Ecology/neighboring-property/statutory owners retain acceptance authority;
- Vegetation and seasonal change alter distribution/shadow/visibility; night design should consider mature and leaf-on/leaf-off states when material.
- Security/perceived-safety outcomes are multi-factor. More illuminance does not automatically create safety; route legibility, faces, concealment, surveillance and social use may matter.
- Actual night readback is required for claims about glare, hierarchy, dark adaptation, landscape appearance or source visibility that cannot be reliably judged in daytime drawings.

**Decision attack:** if the current exterior, night environment, ecology and context can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.11｜Mockup, sample, calculation and visualization evidence

- Choose evidence carrier by question: calculation for distribution/metric, physical mockup for source/surface/scale/glare/detail, sample for material/color response, calibrated photo/measurement for installed condition, render for bounded prediction/communication.
- A render is not photometric proof unless produced from a controlled model and used within a declared predictive ceiling; artistic post-processing cannot repair calculation or field failure.
- Full-scale or representative mockups should reproduce source/optics/mounting/material/viewing geometry closely enough for the question being tested. Miniature/demo-board success may not transfer to room scale.
- Record luminaire/driver/control state, dimming level, material/sample, ambient/daylight condition, camera/exposure if photographic evidence is used, observer position and question under test.
- Compare predicted and observed result. Differences should feed model correction, aiming, output, detail or material decisions rather than being hidden by camera edits.
- Before comparing mockup and calculation, create a like-for-like correlation key: same geometry dimensions/scale relation; exact luminaire and photometric revision; source/driver/output and dimming state; optic/accessory; mounting/aim; surface material/reflectance/gloss; daylight/ambient condition; scene/control state; observer and measurement points/planes.
- Compare predicted and observed values/appearance only at matched points, views and states. Where the mockup is partial or simplified, state which relation can transfer and which cannot.
- Classify a material discrepancy before correction: measurement/instrument setup; product output or photometric data; geometry/aim; material property; control/driver state; ambient/daylight condition; observer/camera method. Record the hypothesis and discriminating recheck instead of tuning multiple variables until the model appears to agree.
- If model calibration is justified by measured evidence, preserve the pre-calibration baseline, changed parameter, evidence for the change and rerun the affected critical scenes; calibration to one point must not silently validate untested views/states.
- Retain failed mockups and rejected fixture/optic combinations when they explain a critical design decision or future substitution boundary.
- Mockup PASS does not prove final site installation if geometry/material/control/configuration changes; exact tested configuration matters.

**Decision attack:** if the current mockup, sample, calculation and visualization evidence can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.12｜Commissioning, aiming, measurement and final night readback

- Commission lighting as a luminous and control system: verify installed identity, aiming/orientation, grouping/addressing, scene/control logic, dimming behavior, sensor behavior, user interfaces and critical measured/observed conditions.
- Measurement plans should state instrument, metric, location/plane/orientation, scene/state, ambient/daylight condition and tolerance/source basis when quantitative verification is required.
- Aiming/focus should be recorded for adjustable luminaires where exact target, beam overlap, glare and spill matter; final aiming is a design action, not incidental contractor adjustment.
- Check scenes in real operating sequences with occupants/operations where possible: entry, normal use, presentation/event, cleaning, night/after-hours, daylight transition and manual override.
- Where daylight-responsive electric lighting is in claim, commissioning must hand off the design daylight basis into an installed correlation: actual aperture/glazing/shading and relevant obstruction/season condition; sensor location/field of view/calibration state; controlled daylight zones/groups; electric-light scene/output; and transition/override/recovery behavior. Read representative daylight conditions or use a bounded staged/input method when natural conditions are unavailable, explicitly retaining what remains unverified. A successful night scene or one favorable daytime commissioning condition cannot close daylight-electric behavior across untested sky/solar/shade states. Lighting owns luminous/readback consequence; Envelope owns aperture/glazing/shading system truth; MEP/Controls own sensor/control implementation and calibration; R-F retains actual interface maturity/disposition.
- If installed measurements differ materially from calculation, reconcile luminaire/driver output, aiming, geometry, surface reflectance, scene level and instrument/method before changing the criterion.
- Close defects only after repair/reprogram/re-aim readback when they affected the in-claim lighting relation.
- Night tuning is a system-level read, not fixture-by-fixture beautification. Review adjacent adaptation zones, arrival/route/task/focal hierarchy, sensitive views/receptors, source visibility, scene transitions and final aiming/output together under representative real operating states.
- Record every tuning change against the final configuration identity: luminaire/optic, actual aim, dimming/output, group/scene, control override, affected surface/task/view and reason. A field adjustment that improves one view but creates spill, glare, dark adaptation or hierarchy failure elsewhere remains OPEN.
- Where measurement is used, retain instrument/method, point/plane/view, scene, ambient/daylight state and source/tolerance basis; where qualitative night judgment is used, bind representative viewpoints and the observed success/contradiction.
- For exterior/façade adjustable lighting, bind final aim to mounting point, datum/target zone, optic/accessory/output/scene, shielding, sensitive receptor/view, wet/reflective-surface state, relevant planting/season and access method. Night tuning must attack spill/glare created by off-axis views, wet façades/paving/water, temporary/event scenes and field tolerances rather than only the intended hero viewpoint.
- Where measurement supports closure, record instrument identity/calibration state where available, geometry/plane/view, background/ambient/daylight condition, stabilization/output state, repeatability or sampling spread and known method limits; do not treat one precise reading as exact truth when uncertainty can change the decision.
- Show how cleaning, driver/source replacement or maintenance access can occur and restore the approved aim/scene without destructive access or uncontrolled re-aiming. Lighting owns luminous/aim/readback consequence; Electrical/Envelope/Access/MEP owners retain safe isolation, fixing/weatherproofing, access equipment and technical approval.
- Commissioning PASS does not self-award Design KEEP, electrical safety approval, emergency compliance or future maintained performance.

**Decision attack:** if the current commissioning, aiming, measurement and final night readback can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.13｜Maintenance, replacement, tuning and post-occupancy learning

- Identify access for cleaning/replacement, driver location, safe isolation dependency, lift/scaffold requirement, aiming restoration and spare/compatible replacement strategy where material.
- Design should anticipate lumen/color/control drift, dirt, failed units and discontinued products where these materially affect visual continuity or task performance.
- Replacement source/luminaire must be checked against output/distribution/color/control/dimensions/appearance, not accepted solely because nominal wattage or CCT matches.
- Where maintained performance is in claim, keep the depreciation basis explicit: source lumen-maintenance evidence, luminaire/environment dirt exposure, cleaning interval/assumption, operating hours/drive/thermal condition where material, surface aging/soiling and failed-unit/replacement policy. Do not collapse these into an unexplained maintenance factor.
- A maintenance plan that underpins the maintained-lighting claim is a design dependency; changed cleaning access/frequency, environment, operating hours or replacement policy reopens the affected maintained-performance conclusion.
- Replacement/substitution equivalence must preserve the controlling distribution/output, shielding/aim, color/spectral behavior, dimming/control compatibility, size/mounting/detail and maintenance-access attributes at the Current claim ceiling; technical/electrical/environment ratings remain with their owners.
- In-use evidence includes scene overrides, occupants disabling controls, glare avoidance, blinds permanently closed, unplanned task lights, maintenance difficulty and dark/overbright areas; treat these as signals for diagnosis.
- Tune controls/aiming/output when evidence shows a bounded mismatch, preserving what changed and why. Do not silently shift targets to match installation.
- Preserve a commissioned luminous-control baseline for consequential scenes: group/address identity, sensor location/coverage and calibration state where available, scene/output values, transition/timing logic, schedules, override state, daylight/shade dependencies and the controls revision/configuration represented by the accepted readback. In post-occupancy review, distinguish intended tuning from configuration drift caused by schedule edits, persistent overrides, sensor relocation/obstruction, BMS handoff, replacement driver/source, firmware/controller change or altered daylight/shade thresholds. Compare current behavior to the accepted baseline before attributing the symptom to user preference or luminaire aging; re-read affected scenes/loops after correction. Controls/BMS owners retain implementation truth; Lighting owns user-visible luminous consequence and bounded recommissioning requirement;
- Separate observed behavior from causal explanation and from reusable rule. One successful scene or complaint does not establish a universal lighting principle.
- Cross-project reusable learning requires transfer/counterevidence through the existing G9/R-B lifecycle.

**Decision attack:** if the current maintenance, replacement, tuning and post-occupancy learning can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

## 5｜Native output and readback hierarchy

Use the lowest-fidelity carrier that can actually test the relation, then increase fidelity only when the failure cannot be resolved otherwise. Depending on the question this may include editable plan/section/model/schedule, calculation, material sample, physical mockup, control/state model, shop/submittal data, field measurement, installation observation or in-use readback.

## 7A｜Adversarial practitioner failure attacks

Reject / REVISE / HOLD when applicable:

1. A photometric report uses the wrong geometry, aiming, output factor, surface reflectance or photometric-file revision.
2. A calculation meets a numeric target but the receiving plane/view/task is not the actual design question.
3. Horizontal illuminance is used to prove visible brightness, face quality or glare without the needed viewer/surface relation.
4. A render or exposure-adjusted image is used as photometric proof.
5. A luminaire schedule omits optic/driver/control/mounting/access attributes that materially define performance.
6. “Same lumens / same watts / same CCT” is accepted as a substitute without optical/color/glare/control/dimension/maintenance comparison.
7. TM-30 or another color metric is copied as a number without a source/project reason or phase-specific specification consequence.
8. Control intent exists only as scene names; groups/triggers/override/failsafe/sequences/test criteria are absent.
9. Lighting tags do not map to the circuits/drivers/control groups needed to implement and test the intended scene.
10. A DALI/0-10V/etc. protocol label is treated as proof of commissioned behavior.
11. Daylight simulation uses one favorable time/sky or omits material obstruction/shade/surface assumptions.
12. Exterior brightness is used as a proxy for safety while glare, adaptation, spill, ecology or neighbor impact remain unresolved.
13. A mockup/sample is generalized beyond its exact product/geometry/material/control/daylight setup.
14. A site submittal changes optic/driver/trim/control/dimension but prior calculations/mockup/review remain falsely current.
15. Installation observation is generalized to unobserved locations.
16. Commissioning is closed because power/controls respond, without actual criterion/result/retest evidence.
17. Aiming/focusing changes are not captured in the final configuration identity.
18. Measured result is reported without instrument/method/location/state/configuration context appropriate to the claim.
19. O&M identifies the luminaire but not driver/control/address/access/replacement-equivalence requirements.
20. A replacement source/luminaire fits physically but changes distribution/color/control behavior without reopen.
21. Lighting professional PASS is used as Electrical/Fire/Accessibility/Energy/Health certification.
22. Stage-level completion lacks material decision-object/claim/native-output/readback binding for a new promotion-relevant claim.
