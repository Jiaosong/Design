## 0｜Professional intent

Develop landscape as a living, graded, drained, accessible, ecological and maintainable site system across seasons and establishment states rather than decorative planting around architecture.

The document is intentionally deeper than a stage checklist. A stage only closes when the underlying professional relation is actually developed, represented in an appropriate native carrier, read back, and bounded by explicit failure/reopen conditions.

`PROCESS DOCUMENTED ≠ PROFESSIONAL DESIGN DEVELOPED`.

`NATIVE ARTIFACT EXISTS ≠ ACTUAL CONDITION READ BACK ≠ PROFESSIONAL PASS ≠ DESIGN KEEP`.

### In scope
- site baseline, survey, existing landscape and ecological constraints;
- outdoor program, movement, accessibility and public-realm experience;
- topography, grading, earthwork and threshold relationships;
- surface/subsurface water, drainage and irrigation interfaces;
- soil/growing medium/rooting conditions;
- planting structure, species strategy, habitat and seasonal change;
- hardscape, edges, furniture, structures and material assemblies;
- microclimate, shade, wind, lighting, wayfinding and utilities coordination;
- construction protection, establishment, maintenance and in-use adaptation;

### Explicitly outside this owner's authority
- survey, civil, geotechnical or drainage engineering certification;
- arboricultural/ecological statutory approval when separate specialist authority is required;
- fire/accessibility approval;
- lighting engineering approval;
- universal plant palette or climate formula;
- field survival or biodiversity outcome inferred before establishment/readback;

Where another professional/statutory owner controls a criterion, this process records the shared variable, geometry/material/design consequence, required maturity and current evidence. It does not copy or reissue that owner's PASS.

## 1｜Evidence and decision discipline

For every material professional decision, preserve:

`QUESTION / USE / FAILURE RISK → CURRENT SOURCE + ASSUMPTIONS → REAL ALTERNATIVES OR DESIGN MOVE → NATIVE GEOMETRY / MATERIAL / CALCULATION / SAMPLE / MOCKUP → ACTUAL READBACK → STRONGEST CONTRADICTION → BOUNDED VERDICT → HANDOFF → REOPEN TRIGGER`.

Rules:
- quantitative thresholds must carry Current source, unit/metric, applicability and tolerance/condition; this process does not invent universal numbers.
- manufacturer/vendor data remains product/configuration evidence, not independent proof of installed/project performance.
- rendered or diagrammatic appearance cannot substitute for geometry, material, physical, field, calculation or specialist evidence when those are in claim.
- one observed location, mockup, season, user condition or project does not automatically represent unobserved work, future performance or universal practice.
- project observations remain observation until causal explanation is supported; reusable learning routes only as a bounded G9/R-B candidate with transfer boundary and counterevidence.
- R-F remains the actual interface maturity/disposition owner; this domain records stage-side requirements only.
- professional development can remain bounded while specialist/statutory items are OPEN only if those OPEN items do not contradict the current claim ceiling.

## 2｜Deep professional decision body

The following sections are the professional substance. They are not replaced by stage completion, matrix coverage or a receipt.

### 2.1｜Site baseline, evidence confidence and existing-condition reading

- Separate verified survey, observed field condition, remote/GIS evidence, historical record, inference and assumption. Do not draw precise levels/tree positions/utility routes from weak imagery without declaring the ceiling.
- Map landform, drainage paths, low/high points, existing vegetation, canopy, root-sensitive zones, soil disturbance, compaction, structures, utilities, access, desire lines, microclimate exposures and neighboring conditions before selecting landscape form.
- Existing vegetation must be classified by actual retention/protection/health/value evidence where available; a green blob in survey/CAD does not prove species, canopy extent, root protection or retention viability.
- Identify site processes that continue during and after construction: runoff, sediment movement, seasonal flooding, leaf litter, shade migration, freeze/thaw, irrigation demand, maintenance access and public shortcuts.
- Document uncertainty spatially. Unknown drainage outfall, soil profile or tree-root condition should create an investigation/open item, not be silently filled with a design assumption.
- Landscape concept should respond to the strongest site evidence and constraints rather than using ecology/topography as post-rationalization after geometry is chosen.
- Any updated survey, arboricultural/ecological finding, utility detection or hydrology evidence that changes site geometry reopens affected landscape decisions and R-F interfaces.

**Decision attack:** if the current site baseline, evidence confidence and existing-condition reading can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.2｜Outdoor program, behavior, movement and public-realm use

- Translate program into outdoor activity states: arrival, waiting, gathering, play, learning, dining, rest, event, service, emergency, maintenance, after-hours and seasonal closure where material.
- Map desired and undesired movement: accessible route, everyday shortcut, cycle/service conflict, emergency access, delivery, maintenance, play spill-out and crowd/event conditions.
- A path network is not resolved because lines connect destinations. Check desire, visibility, decision points, gradient/level change, passing, resting, queueing, edge conditions, weather exposure and destination recognition.
- Outdoor rooms require spatial enclosure/porosity, shade/sun, acoustic/social distance, surveillance/supervision, ground comfort and proximity to amenities appropriate to their use.
- Design for legitimate informal occupation without blocking primary routes, drainage, planting establishment or emergency/service access.
- Seating is a system of posture, companion relation, shade/sun, view, edge/back support, accessibility, drainage, temperature, vandalism/maintenance and cleaning—not an object count.
- Test night and low-occupancy states separately from daytime hero conditions where safety, orientation or social comfort is material.

Treat public-space safety as changing spatial operation, not a fixed daytime plan. For critical areas map normal, event/peak, low-occupancy, wet/leaf/snow/ice, heat/storm/flood, maintenance, partial-closure and after-hours states where relevant; show which routes/edges/water contacts/seating/play/service areas remain open, constrained, diverted or closed and how users understand the change.

Attack temporary barriers, queue spill, maintenance vehicles, slippery/debris-covered surfaces, water-level change, reduced sightlines/leaf-on-off conditions, seasonal furniture removal and closed amenities. Landscape owns spatial layout, visibility, edge/material and seasonal operating consequence; Fire/Accessibility/Security/Operations/Civil retain their statutory/technical criteria and R-F retains actual interface disposition.

**Decision attack:** if the current outdoor program, behavior, movement and public-realm use can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.3｜Topography, grading and earthwork as design structure

- Use levels to shape movement, drainage, spatial enclosure, views, accessible continuity and planting conditions simultaneously; grading is not a late civil overlay.
- Establish existing/proposed spot levels, contours, thresholds, high/low points, slope transitions, retaining needs and earthwork consequences at fidelity appropriate to the claim.
- Avoid micro-terracing or isolated ramps that technically connect points while producing awkward crossfall, drainage traps, residual wedges, inaccessible desire paths or maintenance difficulty.
- At building thresholds coordinate finished floor level, external paving level, drainage, waterproofing/upstand, door operation, accessible transition, snow/rain splash and interior finish interface with the proper owners.
- Retaining walls, steps, slopes and embankments require explicit interface with structure/geotechnical/civil authority; Landscape owns spatial/material intent and grading coordination, not hidden structural capacity.
- Earthwork balance should be considered with soil quality, contamination, reuse suitability, tree protection and logistics; nominal cut/fill volume alone is not a sustainability proof.
- Treat earthwork as a material-routing problem, not volume arithmetic. Separate reusable topsoil/growing media, reusable fill, unsuitable/contaminated/oversize material and imported-material need according to Current evidence; do not net unlike materials into one “balanced” quantity.
- Compare grading options against where cut/fill occurs, temporary stockpile and haul access, retained tree/root and water-protection zones, working room, sequencing and final soil horizons. A nominally balanced scheme that depends on damaging haul routes, double handling or loss of viable soil remains professionally inferior.
- Preserve formation/subgrade intent through field change. Unexpected groundwater, rock, contamination or unsuitable excavated material must reopen affected levels, retaining, drainage, planting and logistics rather than being absorbed by forcing the original contours.
- Read earthwork by zone/material class and sequence at the claim ceiling; a Landscape earthwork diagram coordinates design consequences but does not self-prove civil quantities or disposal compliance.
- Read grading in section and 3D at critical paths/drainage/edges; contour drawings alone can conceal uncomfortable or impossible transitions.

For each material retaining / slope / water-edge condition, read upper/lower landform, toe/crown geometry, adjacent public use, surcharge/activity, groundwater/seepage/drainage path, exposed soil/rock/root zone, overflow/erosion path, edge/guard interface, inspection/maintenance access and construction sequence in one sectioned condition. Attack saturated backfill, blocked drainage, concentrated overflow, toe erosion/scour, local washout after planting failure, settlement/movement and temporary/partial-construction states where relevant.

Landscape owns landform, public-realm, planting/material and visible water-edge consequences; Structure/Geotechnical/Civil/Hydrology retain stability, earth pressure, foundation, scour/hydraulic capacity and temporary-works authority. A planted or visually softened slope does not prove stabilization.

**Decision attack:** if the current topography, grading and earthwork as design structure can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.4｜Water, drainage, infiltration and irrigation logic

- Begin with source-path-storage/infiltration-outfall-receptor logic. Identify where water comes from, how it moves, where it is delayed/absorbed, and what happens during exceedance or failure.
- Coordinate rainfall/runoff criteria, allowable discharge, infiltration feasibility, groundwater/soil constraints and drainage capacity with civil/hydrology owners; Landscape must not invent engineering design storms or capacity values.
- Separate hydrological site behavior from drainage engineering. Landscape must understand catchment boundaries, contributing surfaces, antecedent saturation/groundwater condition, overland flow, soil/media storage and downstream receptors; Civil/Hydrology retains rainfall-frequency assumptions, hydraulic capacity, sizing and compliance.
- Trace intended and failure routing through actual grading: normal conveyance, exceedance, blocked inlet/outlet, saturated soil and unavailable outfall. Show where water goes when the preferred infiltration/storage path is bypassed.
- Do not count nominal planted area as storage/infiltration merely because it is shown green. Receiving levels, inlet connectivity, media/subgrade continuity, controlling-owner infiltration basis and overflow route must be compatible.
- Include sediment/debris, erosion and maintenance access; a planted feature that only works clean and unobstructed is not operationally resolved.
- Surface design should visibly reveal or deliberately conceal water paths without creating ponding at entrances, accessible routes, seating, play, planting crowns or sensitive assemblies.
- Swales, rain gardens, basins and planted drainage features require grading, soil/media, inlet/outlet, overflow, erosion protection, sediment/maintenance access and plant tolerance as one system.
- Irrigation strategy should follow establishment need, climate, soil, species and water-source constraints. Permanent irrigation is not assumed, and zero-irrigation claims require evidence appropriate to the planting establishment phase.
- Develop irrigation from plant-zone demand and establishment trajectory rather than a generic coverage plan. Map hydrozones/plant groups, source and isolation boundary, seasonal/establishment operating states, emitter/application relation, exposure/wind/runoff constraint, soil infiltration/root-zone consequence, manual override, sensor/weather-input dependency and failure/degraded condition.
- Distinguish Landscape demand/coverage intent from MEP/irrigation hydraulic sizing, pressure, backflow, controls execution and water-quality authority. Read commissioning and early establishment for dry/overwatered zones, blocked/damaged emitters, runoff, hidden leaks and controller/sensor mismatch; then state how watering is intentionally reduced, retained or changed as the planting system matures. `IRRIGATION INSTALLED ≠ ROOT-ZONE WATERING WORKS`.
- When irrigation uses reclaimed/recycled/non-potable, saline, treated or otherwise variable water, translate controlling-owner water-quality evidence into landscape consequences: plant-zone compatibility, soil salt/sodium/chemistry accumulation, emitter/filter/clogging risk, spray/contact constraints where applicable, leaching/drainage demand, material staining/corrosion and seasonal/source-quality variability. Show which consequence changes plant palette, soil/media, application method, drainage/flush strategy or source/treatment request; source availability does not prove agronomic or operational compatibility. MEP/Water/water-quality/hygiene authorities retain source, treatment, backflow and health/compliance acceptance; Landscape owns plant/soil/spatial/material/maintenance consequence and reopens affected design when the source-quality basis changes.
- Water features require separate hygiene, treatment, make-up, electrical, slip, access, winterization and maintenance authority as applicable; visual intent cannot stand in for system design.
- Drainage/infiltration performance is not proven by a landscape plan. Use calculations/tests/field evidence from the controlling owner when performance is in claim.

**Decision attack:** if the current water, drainage, infiltration and irrigation logic can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.5｜Soil, growing medium, rooting volume and below-ground conflicts

- Treat soil as a designed living/structural medium with texture, structure, compaction, drainage, organic matter, chemistry, contamination, depth and rooting constraints relevant to the project evidence.
- Do not specify planting on a generic topsoil layer if existing/subgrade compaction, rubble, salinity, contamination, poor drainage or utility congestion controls establishment.
- Rooting volume and geometry must be coordinated with pavements, structures, waterproofing, utilities, drainage, lighting bases, foundations and future access; tree pits are not isolated plan symbols.
- Distinguish gross soil volume from effective rootable soil. Isolated pockets, persistent saturation, construction compaction, structural zones, membranes, utilities and other non-rootable conditions cannot be counted as equivalent continuous rooting resource.
- Read rooting as three-dimensional continuity from trunk flare through growing medium to the subsoil/interface condition, including aeration, drainage/water availability, compaction and future excavation risk; plan-area tree pits alone are insufficient evidence.
- Where shared rooting zones extend below hardscape, resolve pavement support, rootable continuity, water/air movement, service access, future trenching and replacement sequencing as one assembly.
- A soil strategy must state which observed/tested limitation it is changing and how the installed condition will be read back; imported-soil recipe compliance alone does not prove intended root-zone behavior.
- For trees in hardscape, resolve soil continuity, aeration/drainage, structural support where applicable, root guidance/barrier intent, irrigation/establishment, trunk flare level and future canopy/root conflicts.
- Soil amendment/replacement decisions should state the observed/tested problem and the intended change; one imported soil recipe is not universally correct.
- Protect soil from construction compaction and contamination. A technically correct soil specification can still fail if haul routes/storage/trade sequencing destroy the installed/retained condition.
- Reopen planting and drainage decisions if soil investigation or field infiltration/rooting evidence contradicts the assumed profile.

Tie each consequential soil decision to the question the investigation must answer: rooting continuity, compaction/structure, drainage/infiltration, chemistry/salinity, contamination, organic matter or bearing/interface condition as applicable. Define sampling locations/depths from actual landscape zones and likely variability rather than one convenient sample; record method, date/moisture/site condition, laboratory/field source, unsampled limits and how the result changes soil reuse/amendment/removal, drainage, planting or construction protection.

Read installed/retained soil back against the same design question after trafficking, stockpiling, placement and decompaction. Landscape interprets planting/design consequence; Geotechnical/Civil/soil specialists retain test validity and engineering acceptance where assigned. A compliant imported-soil certificate does not prove the installed root zone.

**Decision attack:** if the current soil, growing medium, rooting volume and below-ground conflicts can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.6｜Planting structure, species strategy, succession and seasonal change

- Design planting first as spatial/ecological structure—canopy, understory, ground layer, edge, screen, opening, seasonal field—before choosing species as decorative scatter.
- Species/cultivar selection should reconcile site climate/exposure, soil/moisture, mature size/form, establishment, maintenance, pest/disease risk, biodiversity/habitat role, toxicity/allergen/thorn/fruit/litter issues where relevant, and supply provenance.
- Record nursery size/stock intent separately from mature design geometry. A planting plan that only looks full at installation may fail long-term spacing; one that only shows maturity may ignore establishment gaps and weed pressure.
- Check mature canopy/root/spread against buildings, lights, signs, paths, sightlines, security/supervision, overhead/underground services and maintenance access.
- Plant communities should include competition, shade succession, moisture gradient, replacement/recruitment and management assumptions when ecological behavior is in claim.
- Define assemblage roles across installation, establishment and mature states: long-lived framework, matrix/fill, temporary/nurse cover, seasonal accent and regeneration/replacement layer only where project intent needs them. Do not freeze every layer at one visual density.
- Derive spacing/density from mature habit, desired closure/opening, competition, maintenance method, replacement tolerance and early bare-ground/weed pressure rather than schedule count or installation-day fullness alone.
- Test differential growth and loss: identify which taxa/groups may fail or be substituted without breaking spatial/ecological function and which losses require redesign.
- Link establishment management to changing plant state: watering, support, weed control, cutting/pruning, infill and replacement should change as canopy and ground-layer competition develop; establishment is a transition strategy, not a static task list.
- Seasonal design should address leaf-on/leaf-off, flowering/fruiting, dormancy, snow/rain, heat/drought and night conditions as relevant; a single summer render is insufficient evidence.
- Native/local provenance may be important but cannot be used as automatic ecological superiority without project ecology/source evidence. Retain counterevidence and invasive/disease constraints where material.
- Where naturalization, self-seeding, vegetative spread or strong competitive recruitment is intended or plausible, map `source population → dispersal/spread path → receiving habitat/maintenance boundary → acceptable recruitment zone → escape/invasiveness trigger → containment/removal response`. Distinguish desired succession from competitive exclusion, loss of spatial hierarchy, obstruction of route/sightline/drainage function, or spread beyond the project boundary. Define which compositional/ecological roles may migrate over time and which must remain bounded. Repeated suppression of one aggressive taxon may indicate a design/root-cause reopen rather than a maintenance-frequency problem. Ecology/Arboriculture/statutory owners retain invasive-status, biosecurity and ecological-outcome authority; Landscape owns spatial/compositional consequence;

**Decision attack:** if the current planting structure, species strategy, succession and seasonal change can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.7｜Habitat, biodiversity and ecological interface

- Identify existing habitat value, connectivity, disturbance, water/soil regime and target ecological functions before claiming biodiversity gain or restoration.
- Landscape design can create habitat opportunity but cannot self-prove ecological outcome before establishment/monitoring. Separate designed provision from measured response.
- Coordinate lighting spill, human/dog access, maintenance timing, mowing/cutting, pesticide/fertilizer strategy, deadwood, water edge and nesting/foraging resources where relevant to ecological intent.
- Avoid cosmetic 'biodiversity' metrics that count species or habitat objects without considering area, condition, connectivity, management and target organisms/context.
- Existing mature vegetation/habitat loss cannot be assumed immediately compensated by young replacement planting; temporal lag and establishment risk must remain visible.
- Where habitat connectivity or pollinator/foraging continuity is part of the design intent, map source/refuge → movement/stepping/edge → foraging/nesting resource → seasonal gap/disturbance → receiving habitat at the applicable organism/group evidence ceiling. Test edge effects from route/vehicle/dog pressure, mowing/cutting, lighting, wind/exposure, hard boundaries and maintenance access, plus flowering/seed/cover timing across relevant seasons; isolated habitat objects or one-season bloom do not prove functional continuity. Ecology retains organism/ecological-outcome authority; Landscape owns patch/edge geometry, planting/resource sequence and disturbance/maintenance consequence, and reopens affected design when a pinch point or seasonal resource gap defeats the intended function.
- Where statutory ecology/arboriculture approval is required, route authority to the responsible specialist. Landscape may coordinate design consequences but does not self-award ecological compliance.
- Post-installation ecological observations require time/season/method context and cannot establish causality from one visit or one project.

**Decision attack:** if the current habitat, biodiversity and ecological interface can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.8｜Paths, accessibility, steps, ramps and edge conditions

- Resolve route geometry using continuous levels, gradients/crossfalls, clear widths, landings/resting, surface, drainage, thresholds, handrails/guards and destination/decision-point logic as applicable.
- Accessibility criteria must come from Current governing standards/owner; Landscape must preserve the required geometry and field-readback without inventing a universal gradient or dimension.
- Check desire-line pressure: if the shortest intuitive route conflicts with the designed accessible/safe route, expect shortcutting and erosion unless the spatial logic changes.
- Steps and ramps are spatial elements with arrival/departure, visibility, drainage, hand/guard interfaces, adjacent planting and night-lighting consequences—not isolated symbols.
- Edges between path/planting/water/level change need explicit containment, trip/rollover, wheel/cane, maintenance and drainage reasoning.
- Where play/activity areas meet accessible routes, seating, planting, water or level change, resolve the actual activity/fall/run-out envelope together with accessible approach/transfer, circulation desire line, supervision/seating, drainage, containment and maintenance access. Do not solve inclusive access by routing mobility users through an impact/fall zone or by placing edge protection where it defeats transfer/play use;
- Read level-change interfaces in continuous section from route surface through kerb/edge/guard/planting/water/retaining condition. Attack wheel/cane/rollover, foot/heel entrapment, abrupt material stiffness, drainage slot/gap, debris/snow/leaf state, edge visibility and maintenance tolerance. Landscape preserves spatial/detail geometry; Playground Safety/Accessibility/Structure/Civil owners retain their criteria and approvals;
- Surface texture/joint/module should support mobility, wheeled use, slip/wet state, cleaning/snow/leaf removal and repair. Material appearance alone cannot close route usability.
- Field tolerances, settlement and drainage can invalidate compliant-looking drawings; critical accessible routes require installed level/surface readback by the proper owner.

### 2.9｜Hardscape, furniture, walls, edges and material assemblies

- Choose paving/kerbs/walls/decks/furniture from load/use, water/freeze/heat, contamination, wear, repair, source/provenance and design-language requirements—not image reference alone.
- Resolve base/subbase/support/edge restraint/joint/drainage/transition relation with Civil/Structure as applicable; surface module cannot float independently of construction movement and drainage.
- Treat the hardscape section as a load-water-movement-maintenance assembly. Surface, bedding, base/subbase, subgrade, restraint, jointing and drainage must respond to current traffic/use and ground condition; component naming alone does not demonstrate a working build-up.
- Attack applicable failure chains: differential settlement, pumping/loss of fines, heave/shrinkage movement, loss of edge restraint, joint washout, root uplift, blocked drainage, ponding and differential movement at thresholds/structures.
- Give transition zones their own judgment: changes in stiffness, build-up depth, drainage path, material module or structural support are common failure locations and should be read in section/detail.
- Define repair/replacement logic so local intervention does not destroy adjacent planting, accessible-route continuity, drainage or material composition.
- Mockup/sample readback should inspect joint, edge, transition, wet/dirty condition and repairability where those control design quality, not finish appearance alone.
- Paving modules should be coordinated with path width, curves, drainage channels, tree openings, tactile/edge conditions and building thresholds to avoid uncontrolled slivers and field improvisation.
- Furniture location should account for use, companion/accessible positions, shade/sun, views, noise, drainage, cleaning, security, fixing and maintenance/vehicle conflicts.
- Walls/planters/seat edges require waterproofing/drainage/root/soil interfaces and structural authority where applicable; decorative section drawings do not prove retained-soil capacity.
- Material weathering is part of design. Record expected patina/fade/stain/biological growth/corrosion and whether it is acceptable, maintainable or a failure mode.
- Mockups are required where jointing, large-area color/texture, slip/wet appearance, edge craft or complex transitions materially determine design quality.

**Decision attack:** if the current hardscape, furniture, walls, edges and material assemblies can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.10｜Microclimate, shade, wind, thermal and sensory outdoor comfort

- Use sun/shade, wind exposure, reflected heat, cold-air/frost, humidity, water and vegetation to shape program placement and seasonal use rather than treating comfort as a post-design simulation graphic.
- Frame microclimate by receptor, activity and duration. A condition acceptable for a moving route may be unacceptable for waiting, seating, play, queueing or maintenance; avoid one site-wide “comfort” judgment.
- Test seasonal conflicts: hot-period shade may worsen cold/wet use; evergreen wind protection may suppress useful winter sun or sightlines; deciduous canopy changes shade, wind, glare and enclosure. Record which trade-off controls each place.
- Distinguish geometry-driven levers (orientation, landform, enclosure, canopy/structure, surface) from operational levers (hours, seasonal closure, movable elements, management) and identify which one carries the claimed response.
- Sensitivity-test assumptions that can reverse the conclusion, especially establishing-vs-mature canopy, leaf-on/off, neighboring massing, surface wetness and water/irrigation operation. If the design only works under one favorable assumption set, keep the claim bounded/reopen it.
- Separate modelled microclimate prediction from observed field condition; record weather/climate dataset, geometry/vegetation assumptions and scenario when quantitative performance is claimed.
- Shade design should account for time/season, mature versus establishment canopy, fixed structures, use duration and competing daylight/solar/ecology/security needs.
- Wind mitigation through planting/screens/landform requires porosity, height, spacing, season and maintenance reasoning; dense symbolic planting is not aerodynamic proof.
- Surface materials can materially alter glare, heat, drainage, sound and tactile comfort. Coordinate color/reflectance/thermal behavior with use and lighting where relevant.
- Outdoor acoustic experience depends on source, distance, barriers/landform, activity zoning and ambient masking; planting alone should not be claimed as acoustic isolation without evidence.
- A comfort claim needs a defined activity/user/time/weather envelope. 'Comfortable year-round' is invalid without a defensible environmental and operational basis.

**Decision attack:** if the current microclimate, shade, wind, thermal and sensory outdoor comfort can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.11｜Landscape lighting, wayfinding, safety perception and night ecology

- Establish what must be seen at night: route, step/edge, face/meeting, sign, destination, landscape feature or security interface. Do not light every object by default.
- Coordinate lighting with tree maturity, foliage season, glare/view direction, water, reflective surfaces, path hierarchy, controls/curfew and maintenance access.
- Landscape defines night-spatial intent and mounting/integration consequences; Lighting owns photometric/control design and emergency/security requirements remain with their proper authorities.
- Avoid placing luminaires where future canopy growth, irrigation, mowing, root expansion, snow/storage or public use predictably creates conflict.
- Wayfinding should combine path hierarchy, landmarks, sightlines, naming/signage and light hierarchy. More signs or more brightness cannot repair incoherent route topology.
- Where ecological darkness is material, coordinate spectral/intensity/timing/spill requirements from Current ecological/lighting evidence; do not import generic 'dark-sky' numbers without applicable authority.
- Treat darkness as a spatial resource where ecology evidence requires it. Map habitat movement/foraging/refuge/water-edge zones that need darkness or continuity alongside human routes/decision points that genuinely require lighting.
- Resolve conflict zones through hierarchy, timing/control, shielding/mounting and vegetation geometry rather than simply lowering every light. Landscape issues the spatial dark/light boundary, canopy/growth assumptions and route/edge consequence; Lighting and Ecology retain performance criteria/verdicts.
- Test temporary/event/security/maintenance lighting and seasonal leaf-off states. A nominal-night plan can fail when exceptional operation bypasses the darkness strategy.
- Read transitions between brighter public realm and darker habitat/route states, including visual adaptation, reflective water/paving and future canopy change; spill can change even when fixture placement does not.
- Night readback should occur in actual or representative darkness for critical areas; daytime plan review cannot prove glare, adaptation, hierarchy or perceived safety.

**Decision attack:** if the current landscape lighting, wayfinding, safety perception and night ecology can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.12｜Utilities, structures, maintenance and below-ground coordination

- Map utility corridors, chambers, drainage, irrigation, lighting power, foundations/bases, tree/root zones and future excavation access before final planting/hardscape lock.
- Avoid placing high-value trees or complex paving over assets that require predictable excavation unless a deliberate access/reinstatement strategy exists.
- Maintenance routes need vehicle/tool access, turning/parking, hose/water, waste/green-waste handling and safe work zones where material.
- Coordinate root barriers, sleeves, ducts and foundations by actual conflict and responsible owner rather than installing generic protective details everywhere.
- For consequential tree–structure conditions, coordinate retained/new tree root plate/rootable zone, trunk and mature canopy with foundations, retaining/roof/deck systems, façade/overhead elements and occupied/service targets. Include construction excavation or root loss, future growth, wind exposure, branch clearance, inspection/pruning and eventual removal/replacement access. Landscape owns siting, rootable geometry, clearances and public-realm consequence; Arboriculture owns tree condition/failure-risk judgment and Structure owns capacity/load/fixing acceptance. A nominal offset, tree pit or root barrier does not by itself resolve the combined interface.
- Small landscape structures, decks, pergolas, walls and water-feature supports require structural/fabrication authority at the appropriate ceiling; Landscape retains spatial/material intent.
- Planting/irrigation plans should be coordinated with service trenches to prevent field relocation that destroys spacing/composition or root viability.
- Any late utility route or chamber change reopens affected grading, drainage, planting, hardscape, lighting and maintenance decisions.

**Decision attack:** if the current utilities, structures, maintenance and below-ground coordination can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.13｜Construction protection, planting establishment and field adaptation

- Define protected retained areas, no-go/limited-access zones, haul/storage routes, temporary drainage and soil/vegetation protection before construction damages the evidence base the design depends on.
- Planting quality depends on delivered stock, roots, planting depth, soil condition, drainage, staking/support where needed, watering and aftercare—not species name alone.
- Inspection should bind observation to location/date/season/revision and distinguish sampled observation from whole-site compliance.
- Field substitutions need explicit review of mature size/form, ecological role, seasonality, soil/moisture fit, design composition, supply and maintenance; botanical similarity alone is insufficient.
- Establishment criteria should include survival/health, coverage/competition, irrigation/maintenance, weed/pest/disease response and replacement logic appropriate to the design claim.
- Read establishment against intended trajectory as well as survival: patchiness, dominance shift, bare soil, repeated replacement or dependence on extraordinary inputs may indicate assemblage failure even when individual plants remain alive.
- Landscape is time-dependent. Practical completion, establishment completion and mature design intent are different states; do not collapse them into one PASS.

For material trees, bind delivered identity/stock condition, root architecture/rootball condition, trunk flare/planting level, excavation/root-zone geometry, soil-interface condition, drainage/aeration, staking/guying/support where used, irrigation/watering method, mulch/surface relation, protection from construction/public damage and initial pruning/remedial work to the exact location.

Read establishment as a trajectory: water status, new growth/canopy response, root-zone condition, support removal/adjustment, settlement/lean, pests/disease/damage and replacement history. Do not infer success from survival alone or retain support/irrigation indefinitely by habit. Arboriculture/Ecology and supplier/nursery evidence retain their acceptance authority; Landscape owns spatial/compositional consequence and project reopen.
- Field adaptation is legitimate when actual site/soil/plant evidence changes, but the deviation must preserve or deliberately revise the governing design relation and be read back afterward.

**Decision attack:** if the current construction protection, planting establishment and field adaptation can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

### 2.14｜Maintenance, seasonality, monitoring and adaptive management

- Every planted/material system should expose the maintenance action that preserves its intended state: watering, mowing/cutting, pruning, replacement, cleaning, decompaction, mulching, sediment removal, joint repair or surface treatment as applicable.
- Maintenance intensity is a design variable. A landscape requiring resources/skills the operator does not possess is not operationally resolved even if installation is feasible.
- Define maintenance against intended landscape states, not task frequency alone: state which spatial, ecological, access, drainage and material conditions must be preserved, which change is acceptable succession/patina, and which condition triggers intervention or design reopen.
- For each material intervention, test access, working space, public closure/diversion, waste handling, seasonal timing, replacement stock/material availability and conflicts with habitat, drainage and other users. A task is not maintainable merely because it can be named in a schedule.
- Separate routine maintenance, establishment correction, capital repair and redesign. Repeated correction of the same failure should reopen the design root cause rather than being normalized as an FM burden.
- Use adaptive management as a controlled project loop: pre-action observation → competing explanations → bounded intervention → expected consequence → follow-up readback → retain/reverse/escalate. Prefer local/reversible action while evidence is weak.
- Judge replacement/substitution against governing function/composition/seasonal role and future maintenance burden, not nominal species/product equivalence alone.
- Define acceptable change over time: canopy growth, meadow succession, patina, gaps, leaf litter, seasonal dieback, flood sediment, user desire lines and furniture movement may be intended, tolerated or defects.
- Use monitoring to distinguish establishment failure, maintenance failure, site-condition mismatch, design error and normal succession; do not attribute cause from one visual symptom alone.
- Replacement strategy should preserve genetic/species diversity and composition intent without creating an impossible dependence on one nursery stock source.
- Adaptive management decisions should record observation, competing explanations, action, expected consequence and follow-up readback.
- Reusable knowledge requires cross-context evidence and counterevidence through G9/R-B; one successful planting or public-space outcome is not a universal landscape rule.

**Decision attack:** if the current maintenance, seasonality, monitoring and adaptive management can only be defended by labels, a hero render, generic precedent or a report saying it was reviewed, the relation remains `REVISE / HOLD` until the native/readback evidence appropriate to the claim exists.

## 5｜Native output and readback hierarchy

Use the lowest-fidelity carrier that can actually test the relation, then increase fidelity only when the failure cannot be resolved otherwise. Depending on the question this may include editable plan/section/model/schedule, calculation, material sample, physical mockup, control/state model, shop/submittal data, field measurement, installation observation or in-use readback.

Preferred evidence order when the claim escalates:

`SOURCE / BASIS → EDITABLE NATIVE DESIGN → COORDINATED CURRENT CONFIGURATION → SAMPLE / MODEL / MOCKUP / ANALYSIS → INSTALLED / FIELD / RUNTIME READBACK → PROFESSIONAL INTERPRETATION`.

A derivative can support communication but cannot silently replace the authority-bearing/native carrier.

---

## 6｜Handoff and reopen semantics

A Landscape Architecture handoff must identify the exact decision, shared variable, native source/revision, required receiving-owner maturity, remaining OPEN item and change that would invalidate the handoff. 'Coordinated' as free prose is insufficient.

Material changes propagate only through affected dependencies; they do not reset the whole process by default. Historical evidence remains truthful for its original condition and is never rewritten to impersonate a successor.

---

## 7｜Independent review and anti-overclaim

Independent review must attack the strongest plausible Landscape Architecture failure, not simply confirm document completeness. Review should include adverse/edge/maintenance/field states appropriate to the claim and bind the exact native/configuration revision reviewed.

Hard boundaries:
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove survey, civil, geotechnical or drainage engineering certification.
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove arboricultural/ecological statutory approval when separate specialist authority is required.
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove fire/accessibility approval.
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove lighting engineering approval.
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove universal plant palette or climate formula.
- `LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE PROFESSIONAL PASS` does not itself prove field survival or biodiversity outcome inferred before establishment/readback.
- Definition/schema validation does not prove project exercise or professional PASS.
- One project success/failure does not establish a universal rule or causality.
- Candidate process validation does not change Current architecture state.
