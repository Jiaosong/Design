## Translation-depth framework T0–T4

| Level | Operation | Decision boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T0 Symbol attachment | Apply visible motifs, calligraphy, roof/brick imagery, etc. | Insufficient by itself to establish contemporary Chinese translation. |
| T1 Material / craft reuse | Reuse a historical material or craft | Must preserve provenance and re-test performance, manufacturing, construction, rights and maintenance. |
| T2 Relational mechanism | Translate module, joint, hierarchy, void/density, enclosure, path, opening, repetition | Form may change if the underlying relation remains explicit and testable. |
| T3 Behavior / production | Preserve or reframe making, use, maintenance, collaboration or participation | The process must actually occur; it cannot be represented only as imagery. |
| T4 Value / governance | Address knowledge rights, attribution, local production, renewal pace and long-term stewardship | Values require evidence and responsibility; slogans do not qualify. |

Working rule: T0 may be present, but meaningful translation normally requires evidence from at least one deeper layer. Terms such as “Chinese”, “Eastern”, “Song”, “Zen” or “minimal” are not sufficient evidence.

## First evidence set: Wang Shu × Lu Wenyu × Amateur Architecture Studio

Institutional evidence supports a collaborative rather than solo-author model: Wang Shu and Lu Wenyu founded Amateur Architecture Studio in Hangzhou in 1997; MoMA collection records for Ningbo History Museum, Xiangshan Campus and Wencun Village use joint authorship and identify the studio.

### Transferable mechanisms
- Begin with site, existing relations, material availability, living patterns and construction conditions rather than a predefined historical style.
- Treat material provenance as part of meaning: demolition, recovery, sorting, remaking, labor and time can form an evidence chain.
- Treat drawing/model and construction feedback as one quality-control loop; site deviations, mockups and craft experiments may change the design judgment.
- Translate traditional knowledge through spatial, material, production and relational mechanisms rather than copying roofs, brick textures or “vernacular” appearance.
- Preserve co-authors, craftspeople, institutions and production participants in the evidence record.

### Representative evidence objects
- Xiangshan Campus, China Academy of Art, 2004 / 2007 — building group, terrain, paths, thresholds, multiple scales and material reuse.
- Ningbo History Museum, 2008 — large public volume versus heterogeneous reused material; provenance/time as a material information layer.
- Wencun Village, 2016 — village renewal, housing type, everyday use and production conditions; locality is not reducible to a visual style.

## OLEANDER translation questions

Replace low-information prompts with evidence questions:

- “How can it look more Chinese?” → Which historical relation actually matters here: space, material, craft, behavior, narrative or governance?
- “Can we add reclaimed brick / timber lattice?” → Is provenance, performance, connection, supply and maintenance relevant to the project proposition?
- “How do we make it feel more Song?” → What exact object or text is the source, and are we translating proportion, void, viewing, object relations, making or institutions?
- “This feels Eastern.” → What are the first-reading unit, hierarchy, rhythm, geometry, scale, material and behavioral evidence?
- “Traditional craft means cultural continuity.” → Who owns the knowledge, who makes it, who is attributed, and does the production/maintenance mechanism actually continue?

## AI collaboration boundary

AI may extract candidate mechanisms from verified sources, classify fact / author view / professional interpretation / analysis, build cross-case matrices, generate parameterized variants and flag vague style vocabulary.

Human judgment remains required for cultural source and rights, real spatial/body scale, material and construction experience, selection of historical references, and deciding which mechanisms are relevant to OLEANDER.

## Engineering boundary

Historical use of a material or craft does **not** establish current compliance, structural suitability, fire performance, procurement approval or site acceptance. Any project adoption must return to OLEANDER engineering evidence gates for product identity, applicable standards, system construction, test reports, mockups, procurement and G9 feedback.
