# THEORY-MOVEMENT-METABOLISM-JP-001

## Japanese Metabolism｜固定骨架—可变单元—时间性城市

Status: ACTIVE / E3
Primary layer: Culture / CU02
Supporting layers: CU01, CU04, Spatial SP02–SP04, IP03
Canonical source of dynamic truth: Notion

## Core interpretation
Metabolism is treated here as a systemic theory of change over time, not as a visual style. The stable transferable pattern is:

`long-life frame → governed interface → shorter-life replaceable unit → maintenance / replacement / retirement`

The movement's historical capsule, megastructure and techno-futurist imagery is not an OLEANDER formal template.

## Evidence anchors
- Metabolism 1960: The Proposals for New Urbanism. CCA catalogue confirms Tokyo publication in 1960, Japanese/English text, and participants Kiyonori Kikutake, Masato Otaka, Fumihiko Maki, Kisho Kurokawa and editor/critic Noboru Kawazoe.
- Pritzker's Fumihiko Maki record identifies him as a founding Metabolist and highlights changeability and flexibility.
- MoMA's Helix City record documents Kurokawa's 1961 project as an expandable service-tower/infrastructure scheme.
- MoMA's Capsule A1305 record documents a surviving/restored element of the 1970–72 Nakagin Capsule Tower and its post-demolition preservation history.

## Representative objects
1. Metabolism 1960 — manifesto / collective discussion framework.
2. Sky House — Kiyonori Kikutake, Tokyo, 1958; retained as an early reference for frame and future change. Detailed construction-change history remains open pending primary drawings.
3. Helix City — Kisho Kurokawa, 1961; transferable lesson is repeatable generative relationships, not helix-shaped form.
4. Nakagin Capsule Tower — Kisho Kurokawa and team, 1970–72; serves both as a prototype of replaceable units and as a counterexample showing that intended replaceability can fail when maintenance, economics, ownership, interfaces and supply do not support it.

## OLEANDER transferable rules
1. Define `LONG / MID / SHORT` life layers before form-making.
2. Treat interfaces as first-class design objects: connection, tolerance, access, compatibility, responsibility and exit path.
3. Versioning does not require visual sameness; preserve relationship rules while allowing component difference.
4. Open growth requires stop/split/restructure conditions.
5. Move G9 considerations—maintenance, replacement, repair, reuse and retirement—into concept-stage decisions.

## Do not transfer directly
- capsule aesthetics;
- megastructure imagery;
- biomorphic curves presented as "organic growth";
- technological optimism without lifecycle, cost, rights and maintenance evidence;
- a postwar-Japan urban prescription detached from its historical conditions.

## AI collaboration
AI may classify components by expected lifetime, map interface dependencies, generate compatibility matrices and run failure-seeking evolution scenarios. Human judgment remains responsible for deciding what deserves permanence, what should change, and whether replaceability is materially, economically, culturally and operationally real.
