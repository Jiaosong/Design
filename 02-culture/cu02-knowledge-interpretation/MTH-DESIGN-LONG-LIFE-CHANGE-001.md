> This is an OLEANDER method translation derived from cross-case study of Kikutake. It is not presented as a standardized formula authored by Kikutake. Real structural, fire, MEP, maintenance, cost and user evidence remain project-specific.

## Method

1. **Layer** — classify elements as `LONG-LIFE / MID-LIFE / SHORT-LIFE`.
2. **Interface** — map connection, removal, access, tolerance, service, data and responsibility across layers.
3. **Capacity** — test whether long-life layers reserve structural, spatial, MEP, data or governance capacity for later change.
4. **Scenario** — run `NOMINAL / ADVERSE / FAILURE-SEEKING` conditions.
5. **Decision** — mark each interface `KEEP / REVISE / REMOVE / UNKNOWN`.
6. **Future Test** — define the real prototype, maintenance, retrofit or migration evidence needed to replace assumptions.

## Why Kikutake is useful

Institutional sources support a recurrent concern with renewal, transformation and evolving living platforms across Kikutake's practice. DOCOMOMO describes Sky House (1958) as a reinforced-concrete dwelling whose continuous living zone is carried by four structural supports while movable service/private `movenette` units occupy the perimeter and the house changed with family needs. Hotel Toko-en (1964) separates functional and structural integrities. The Izumo Administration Building (1963) uses prestressed precast concrete super-beams to produce a large column-free interior. Harvard GSD's 2016 monograph treats Marine/Ocean City as a long-running urban-scale project within this larger trajectory.

## OLEANDER transfer

- **Spatial:** separate enduring frame, service infrastructure, fit-out and short-cycle use layers; design access and replacement paths before visual treatment.
- **Product:** distinguish platform, replaceable module, wear part, software/firmware and consumable life cycles.
- **IP:** keep naming/identity architecture distinct from campaign, editorial and experimental layers; version interfaces must remain explicit.
- **Website:** separate durable information architecture and component contracts from fast-changing content and visual experiments.

## Failure-seeking prompts

- A short-life layer cannot be removed without damaging the long-life layer.
- A critical component is discontinued.
- A service route becomes inaccessible after fit-out.
- Capacity reserve is exhausted earlier than expected.
- A new version breaks compatibility with old assets.
- Maintenance cost makes replacement theoretically possible but operationally irrational.

A simulated success may only be called `DESIGN-READY FOR FUTURE TEST` or `SIMULATED PASS UNDER ASSUMPTIONS`. It is never project approval.

## Boundaries

This method is **not** equivalent to generic modularity or Miesian universal space. Large or column-free space can create capacity but does not by itself establish replaceable interfaces, lifecycle governance, maintenance access or version compatibility. Metabolist megastructure imagery, floating platforms and capsule aesthetics are not OLEANDER form rules.
