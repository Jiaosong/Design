# OLEANDER Physical Signage + Environmental Graphics Extension

Status: `CANDIDATE EXTENSION / VISUAL-DESIGN`

Use when orientation, destination finding, physical signs, exhibition graphics or environmental communication must work for bodies moving through real space. This is not a building-code, accessibility-certification, evacuation-engineering or fabrication owner.

## Core contract

`DESTINATION / TASK → BODY APPROACH → DECISION POINT → SIGHTLINE / AVAILABLE DECISION TIME → CARRIER ROLE → CONTENT + DIRECTION HIERARCHY → PHYSICAL PLACEMENT → REDUNDANT CUES → ACTUAL APPROACH / READBACK`.

Physical wayfinding is not a web navigation menu printed at larger size. The viewer has a direction of travel, partial field of view, changing distance, competing environmental stimuli and a cost for missing the information before committing to a path.

## Decision-point gate

For each material decision point, record:
- approach direction(s);
- available route/destination choices;
- what the visitor must know before commitment;
- physical point where the information must first become available;
- candidate sign/carrier location and why it enters the sightline in time;
- what happens if the sign is missed, occluded or approached from another direction;
- Return/recovery cue when the route can fail.

A sign placed after the turn can be visually excellent and still be a functional failure.

## Carrier-role model

Use a bounded working decomposition when useful:
- identification / destination confirmation;
- directional / route decision;
- orientation / map-directory overview;
- informational / explanatory content;
- regulatory / mandatory behavior;
- landmark / environmental identity cue.

This is not an exhaustive universal taxonomy. One carrier may combine roles only when hierarchy remains clear and the combination does not hide a safety/decision function.

## Sightline and physical placement

Check the real relation between body and carrier:
- approach direction and probable head/body orientation;
- occlusion by architecture, planting, crowds, displays or vehicles as relevant;
- mounting/placement relation to the path and decision point;
- environmental light/glare/background competition;
- whether the message remains readable soon enough to act;
- whether map orientation or arrow direction corresponds to the actual spatial decision.

Do not invent viewing-distance, cap-height, mounting-height or contrast formulas from this extension. Use the current jurisdiction, accessibility standard, venue/supplier specification and actual field geometry.

## Progressive disclosure

Match information depth to spatial stage:
- arrival/entry: establish orientation, major destinations and system identity;
- transition/decision point: expose only the choices required now plus recovery-critical cues;
- destination: confirm arrival and provide local/detail information;
- interpretation/exhibition: add deeper content without competing with route/safety ownership.

More information is not automatically more helpful. If a sign must be read while moving, low-priority narrative content may need another carrier or later location.

## Redundant orientation cues

Critical meaning should not rely on one fragile channel. Depending on context, combine appropriate cues such as:
- text + arrow/pictogram;
- numbering/naming;
- repeated placement logic;
- spatial landmark;
- floor/wall/environmental banding;
- map + destination confirmation;
- color as a support channel, not sole meaning where accessibility/truth requires redundancy.

Redundancy must clarify the same route logic, not create competing systems.

## Situated map gate

When a physical map is used, preserve the map's geometry/data authority and separately verify its situated use:
- `YOU ARE HERE` identity and actual location;
- viewer-facing orientation or clearly communicated north-up/rotated convention;
- route/destination labels that match physical signage;
- visible nearby landmark/path cues where relevant;
- Return/recovery logic;
- no false GPS/live-state implication if the map is static.

A technically accurate map can still fail if its orientation and physical placement contradict the viewer's immediate scene.

## Environmental identity boundary

Supergraphics, color fields, material changes and large-scale typography may reinforce zone identity and landmarks. They must not impersonate regulatory/safety authority or replace directional information when the route choice is non-obvious.

Brand expression remains subordinate to actual path, content and accessibility truth. Do not import a third-party venue's visual identity as a universal OLEANDER signage language.

## Readback attacks

At candidate stage inspect, as applicable:
- entry approach;
- at least one ambiguous junction;
- destination confirmation;
- reverse/Return approach;
- sign/map with surrounding environment visible;
- grayscale or color-removal when color carries system meaning;
- occlusion or crowding scenario;
- seated/alternate approach where accessibility makes it material.

Use real site photos/plans/3D/field evidence when available. Remote diagrams without field measurement remain concept evidence.

## Cross-owner routing

- route/topology/map data → `oleander-data-viz` and current spatial authority;
- digital navigation/recovery → `oleander-web-ui/INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE_WAYFINDING_EXTENSION.md` and route/wayfinding specialist;
- typography/icon system → corresponding `oleander-visual-design` extensions;
- dimensions, mounting, fabrication, structural safety and accessibility compliance → current technical/VALIDATION authority;
- exhibition/board narrative → `oleander-story-and-board` when the carrier is part of a larger sequence.

## Rejected external defaults

Do **not** promote as universal OLEANDER rules:
- fixed ADA mounting heights, corridor widths or contrast percentages;
- fixed text-height/viewing-distance formulas;
- fixed visitor-density values;
- fixed sign-size/material prescriptions;
- fixed viewing distances for exhibit media;
- claimed wayfinding success percentages without actual study;
- one mandatory color-coding or four-sign taxonomy for every environment.

## Required output

Return destination/task authority, approach-direction map, decision-point ledger, carrier-role/sign-family map, sightline/placement rationale, content/direction hierarchy, redundant cue strategy, situated-map checks when applicable, actual approach/readback evidence and all code/accessibility/fabrication/field holds.
