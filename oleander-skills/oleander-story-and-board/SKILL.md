# Oleander Story and Board

Build a single evidence-to-story spine so reports, boards, decks, and films tell the same project rather than becoming unrelated artifacts.

## Narrative spine

Define:

1. Context: what situation exists?
2. Tension: what is missing, changing, or contested?
3. Insight: what did research reveal?
4. Proposition: what does Oleander do?
5. System: how does the design work?
6. Experience: what will people see, feel, and do?
7. Proof: what evidence, analysis, or prototype supports it?
8. Invitation: what should the audience believe or do next?

Every section should have one primary claim and one primary visual.

## Assembly workflow

1. Pull approved claims and sources from Notion.
2. Pull validated charts from `oleander-data-viz`.
3. Pull model manifests and approved renders from `oleander-3d-pipeline`.
4. Create a content inventory before layout.
5. Establish grid, hierarchy, type system, palette, caption style, image treatment, and page/board numbering.
6. Produce a low-fidelity sequence before polishing.
7. Reuse the same narrative IDs across report sections, boards, deck slides, and film scenes.
8. Run content, visual, and production reviews separately.

## Deliverable variants

- Board: distance-readable hierarchy, restrained text, explicit sequence.
- Report/book: evidence depth, citations, cross-references, appendices.
- Deck: one decision or claim per slide.
- Brand story: human motivation, distinctive promise, proof, voice.
- Film storyboard: scene purpose, image, motion, narration, sound, duration, source assets.

## Technical drawing hierarchy inside presentation surfaces

When a board, report, deck or web page contains a section, elevation, axon, exploded view, node, assembly or maintenance drawing, do not treat the drawing as a flat image block. Preserve an explicit near-to-far reading hierarchy inside the technical figure itself.

Default visual order:

`PRIMARY FORM / CUT → STRUCTURE / RELATION → EDGE / CONNECTION → DIMENSION / NOTE`

Apply these rules:

1. Primary form must be readable before labels at the intended viewing distance.
2. Structural or spatial relations are secondary and must not visually equal the cut/profile or other primary geometry.
3. Edge, safety and connection information appears at near-read scale and uses explicit targets where leaders are needed.
4. Dimensions, evidence qualifiers, maintenance notes and explanatory text are support information; they must not compete with geometry.
5. Human figures may establish relative scale, but they must not imply field-measured accuracy unless that evidence exists.
6. If a note overlaps or competes with the object it describes, first move, shorten or reduce the note; do not solve the conflict by making all geometry heavier.
7. Additional dimensions or annotations are not a substitute for hierarchy. If the figure is already complete but hard to read, revise graphic weight and spacing before adding content.
8. Preserve project truth boundaries such as NTS, FIELD OPEN, PROVISIONAL, source-grounded or engineering-review-required directly in the figure where relevant.

Use ISO 128-series conventions as a professional reference for technical drawing line, leader and representation logic where applicable, without claiming compliance unless the actual drawing has been checked against the relevant standard.

## Required output

Return the narrative outline, content inventory, page/board/scene map, missing-assets list, production specification, and final editable/source deliverable.

## Quality checks

- Claims match approved research.
- No placeholder or unlicensed asset reaches final output.
- Captions identify what, where, when, and why it matters.
- Text remains readable at target print/view distance.
- The conclusion follows from the evidence rather than visual mood alone.
- Technical figures preserve a clear primary-form → relation → connection → annotation reading order.
- A technically complete drawing that fails first-read hierarchy remains `REVISE`, even if all notes and dimensions are present.
