# Oleander Story and Board

Build a single evidence-to-story spine so reports, boards, decks, and films tell the same project rather than becoming unrelated artifacts.

## Narrative spine

Define:

1. Context: what situation exists?
2. Tension: what is missing, changing, or contested?
3. Insight: what did research reveal?
4. Proposition: what does Oleander do?
5. System: how does the design work?
6. Experience: what will people see, feel, and do?
7. Proof: what evidence, analysis, or prototype supports it?
8. Invitation: what should the audience believe or do next?

Every section should have one primary claim and one primary visual.

## Existing-mature-design first

Before layout or story redesign:

1. Open the strongest Current-Authority-permitted mature design/source.
2. Identify what it already solves in first-read, composition, proportion, image scale, typography, narrative rhythm, project specificity, and professional finish.
3. Treat those solved qualities as the baseline to preserve or improve, not as optional inspiration.
4. Do not replace a mature spatial/product/brand composition with a lower-quality governance diagram, methodology page, card wall, or research-report aesthetic merely because the latter is easier to explain.
5. A new board/page that is clearer logically but weaker visually is `REVISE / REVERT TO EXISTING`, not a design improvement.

## First-visual composition gate

The first visual must communicate the project before explanatory reading begins. It has independent veto power over an otherwise correct page.

### Required hierarchy

For a normal MAIN page/board, prefer this visual order unless the project demands another explicit hierarchy:

`PRIMARY SPATIAL / PRODUCT / BRAND VISUAL → RELATION / ACTION → SUPPORTING EVIDENCE → CAPTION / TECHNICAL PROOF`

The primary visual must own the largest perceptual mass. Evidence, state, metrics, and methodology remain visible but subordinate unless the page is specifically a technical-proof page.

### Reject or revise when

- cards, panels, tables, or status boxes become the dominant first-read;
- text carries most of the project specificity while the image could belong to any project;
- all modules have similar weight and the eye has no entry point;
- the board reads like a research report, dashboard, student board, or process document instead of mature design work;
- technical proof visually overwhelms lived experience on a page whose role is experience, landscape, product, or brand;
- composition has no large-scale spatial tension, crop decision, focal mass, or intentional void;
- the page is more complete than the previous version but still weaker than the strongest existing mature design.

## Diagnostic-to-repair loop

Do not stop at `looks weak`, `too flat`, `too much text`, or another vague critique. Convert visual mismatch into a repair instruction.

Use this sequence:

`FIRST-READ TEST → MISMATCH MAP → CAUSE → ONE MATERIAL REPAIR → REOPEN AT TARGET SCALE → COMPARE AGAIN`

For each mismatch, record:

- **Observed failure**: what is seen, not the intention.
- **Likely cause**: grid, scale, crop, hierarchy, spacing, type, image choice, tonal balance, material, perspective, state, motion, etc.
- **Repair operation**: one concrete edit with measurable or inspectable effect.
- **Expected first-read change**: what should become clearer, quieter, stronger, or more specific.
- **Verification**: target-scale reopen plus comparison against the existing-mature baseline or the locked reference.

Examples:

- `Observed: six equal modules compete.` → `Cause: equalized area and contrast.` → `Repair: merge support modules into one low-contrast evidence strip; give the primary visual >50% of perceptual area.`
- `Observed: project identity depends on headings.` → `Cause: generic placeholder imagery.` → `Repair: replace the hero with source-grounded project-specific geometry/image and remove descriptive copy that merely compensates for weak visuals.`
- `Observed: technical labels read before the experience.` → `Cause: labels are too dark/large and scattered.` → `Repair: consolidate into one aligned secondary band and reduce contrast/size until the primary visual wins at distance.`

## Distance / scale readback

Review each artifact at the actual intended reading conditions, not only zoomed-in editing view.

- Board: perform far-read, mid-read, and near-read.
- Web/slide: reopen at target viewport, including mobile when relevant.
- PDF/report: inspect full page first, then reading-scale detail.
- Film/storyboard: inspect frame hierarchy at playback size, not only individual stills.

At far/first-read, ask only:

1. What is the primary subject?
2. What is the dominant spatial/product/brand relation?
3. Where does the eye go next?

If these answers require reading explanatory paragraphs, the first-visual gate is not passed.

## Assembly workflow

1. Pull approved claims and sources from the Current Authority / approved project records.
2. Pull validated charts from `oleander-data-viz`.
3. Pull model manifests and approved renders from `oleander-3d-pipeline`.
4. Open the strongest existing mature design/source before making a new layout.
5. Create a content inventory before layout.
6. Establish grid, hierarchy, type system, palette, caption style, image treatment, and page/board numbering.
7. Produce a low-fidelity sequence before polishing, but do not expose low-fidelity placeholders as finished design.
8. Build one page/board to professional-review level and run the first-visual composition gate before propagating the system.
9. Reuse the same narrative IDs across report sections, boards, deck slides, and film scenes.
10. Run content, visual, and production reviews separately.
11. The producer may mark `EXECUTED / SELF-CHECKED / REVIEW PENDING / REVISE / REJECT`; KEEP-class verdicts require independent design readback.

## Reference-bound training rule

When a training exercise claims reproduction/reconstruction, follow the repository `Reference Reconstruction Fidelity Gate`:

`ORIGINAL REFERENCE → 1:1 RECONSTRUCTION → SIDE-BY-SIDE / OVERLAY CRIT → FIDELITY REPAIR → METHOD EXTRACTION → TRANSFER VARIANT`

A style study, inspired variant, or simplified diagram is not a reproduction. If the source cannot support honest 1:1 comparison, label the exercise `STRUCTURAL RECONSTRUCTION / METHOD STUDY / REFERENCE-BOUND STUDY` and keep fidelity `HOLD`.

## Deliverable variants

- Board: distance-readable hierarchy, restrained text, explicit sequence.
- Report/book: evidence depth, citations, cross-references, appendices.
- Deck: one decision or claim per slide.
- Brand story: human motivation, distinctive promise, proof, voice.
- Film storyboard: scene purpose, image, motion, narration, sound, duration, source assets.

## Required output

Return the narrative outline, content inventory, page/board/scene map, missing-assets list, production specification, and final editable/source deliverable.

For a design-skill training run, also return:

- the actual practice artifact or editable prototype;
- the first failed or weaker state when it reveals a useful failure mode;
- the concrete repair made;
- the validation result at target scale;
- the reusable rule added/modified in the skill;
- transfer notes showing where else in OLEANDER the rule applies and where it does not.

## Quality checks

- Claims match approved research and Current Authority.
- The primary visual wins the first-read before evidence and explanation.
- Project specificity is visible in form/image/space/product/interaction, not carried mainly by headings.
- Composition avoids dashboard/card-wall/report aesthetics unless that is explicitly the artifact role.
- Captions identify what, where, when, and why it matters.
- Text remains readable at target print/view distance.
- Technical proof stays visible but does not replace the design experience.
- The conclusion follows from the evidence rather than visual mood alone.
- Export/build/traceability success is not treated as design quality.
- A stronger previous mature design is not superseded by a weaker but more complete new layout.
