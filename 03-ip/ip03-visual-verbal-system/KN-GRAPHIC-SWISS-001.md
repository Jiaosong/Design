## Core proposition
International Typographic Style / Swiss Style is useful to OLEANDER as a system for organizing information relations, not as a recipe of Helvetica + 12 columns + black/white/red + “minimalism.”

The historical field includes internal differences: Zürich work associated with Josef Müller-Brockmann is especially useful for grid, modular relation, series consistency and image/type organization; Basel work associated with Armin Hofmann and Emil Ruder adds contrast, negative space, image compression, teaching exercises and typographic criticism.

## Transferable rules
1. **Grid = relation protocol, not a fixed column count.** Define content classes, alignment lines, reusable roles and breakpoints before choosing 2/4/6/12 or any other column structure.
2. **Hierarchy = controlled difference.** Size, position, weight, direction, density, whitespace and image ratio can all carry hierarchy.
3. **Whitespace = structural variable.** It separates groups, sets rhythm, creates pause and protects focus; it is not a synonym for “premium.”
4. **Typography = information behavior.** Typeface, line length, leading and spacing depend on language, medium, viewing distance and content; Swiss Style does not yield universal numeric values.
5. **Series consistency reduces relearning.** Stable component roles and information locations help users predict successive pages, screens or artifacts.
6. **Controlled deviation belongs to the system.** Off-axis placement, overlap, spanning and scale jumps are valid only when their reading relationship remains explainable.

## Representative evidence
- Josef Müller-Brockmann, Tonhalle concert poster, 1953 — geometry, alignment, whitespace and information hierarchy.
- Josef Müller-Brockmann, `Der Film`, 1960 — typography itself carries motion, direction and image function.
- Armin Hofmann, `Giselle`, 1959 — image/negative-space/type contrast shows that structural discipline is not equivalent to low expression.
- `Typografische Monatsblätter`, 1950s–60s — professional journal, education and international circulation show that a design movement is also an institutional knowledge network.

## Explicitly rejected as universal rules
Unless separately sourced and validated for a defined medium/language/task, the following are **not** Swiss Style laws:
- fixed 12-column grid;
- fixed 45–75 character line length;
- “0.5 second” hierarchy recognition;
- ≤3 colors;
- fixed 1.25 / 1.5 type scale;
- 45-point style score or similar pass/fail totals.

These may be project hypotheses or heuristics, but cannot be promoted to historical or universal evidence.

## OLEANDER use
- IP03: shared relation protocol for grid, type, image, information hierarchy and motion.
- IP02: content order and editorial responsibility precede decoration.
- IP04: keep component roles stable across website, portfolio, proposal, social and physical touchpoints while allowing medium-specific variation.
- Culture: never treat Swiss modernism as culturally neutral or the default definition of “modern.” Compare it with other information traditions on equal terms.
- Business: systemic consistency may lower production/maintenance burden, but not at the expense of market language, accessibility or task relevance.
- Spatial: only transfer into wayfinding/exhibition/environmental graphics after spatial validation; poster grids do not become architectural grids automatically.

## AI collaboration
AI may propose alternative grid relations from a content tree, detect alignment/hierarchy drift, generate multilingual stress tests, compare strict/weak/controlled-break variants, and flag superficial “Swiss/Helvetica/minimal” prompting.

Human judgment remains required for first-reading priority, typography quality, actual reading distance, image ethics, editorial responsibility, accessibility and deciding whether a deviation strengthens meaning.

## Pending validation
`Grid as Relation Protocol — same content, three systems`

Create A strict modular grid, B hierarchy-first weak grid, and C controlled-deviation layout using the same OLEANDER content. Do not pre-set 12 columns. Treat columns, baseline, spacing and type sizes as practice assumptions. Compare first-reading unit, hierarchy, rhythm, relation, proportion, whitespace, responsive behavior and multilingual robustness. No readability improvement may be claimed until actually tested.
