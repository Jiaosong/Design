## Canonical relation model
`Question → Service → Time → Change → Feedback → Exit`

This is an OLEANDER analytical abstraction, not a Cedric Price-authored formula.

## Primary cases
1. **Fun Palace** — activity tables, systems/services, circulation, modular grids, structural connections, cost material and cybernetics records show change as a combined spatial/organizational/information problem.
2. **Potteries Thinkbelt** — rail/road networks, transfer areas, learning/housing nodes and reuse of existing infrastructure frame education as a distributed regional service rather than a single campus object.
3. **Generator** — variable cubes, use-characteristic charts, working electronic model, computer/plotter records and configurable site studies bring feedback and reconfiguration into the design object.

## Transfer rules
- Define the change worth supporting before selecting technology.
- Treat INITIAL / USE / CHANGE / MAINTENANCE / EXIT as explicit states.
- Separate stable infrastructure from replaceable events/components.
- Record actor, permission, trigger, allowed change, cost/risk and rollback.
- Make feedback capable of changing the next design decision.
- Design REMOVE / REPURPOSE / DECOMMISSION paths; permanence is not a default success criterion.

## Non-transferable shortcuts
Industrial frames, open grids and high-tech visual language are not an OLEANDER style rule. Unbuilt conceptual projects do not prove construction, compliance, durability or business viability. Historical computer-feedback experiments must not be restated as current AI-adaptive systems.
